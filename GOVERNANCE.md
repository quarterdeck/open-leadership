# Governance

Version 0.1.0 (draft).

## Who can use Open Leadership

Anyone. All content is under the MIT licence. Any person or organisation can read, teach,
assess, certify, adapt and sell services based on Open Leadership, with no permission and no
fee. The only conditions are those of the licence and the rules on honest claims below.

## Who maintains it

Quarterdeck is the steward of the framework. The steward keeps this repository, decides which
changes are accepted and publishes releases. The steward does not approve centres, assessors,
certificates or CPD activities. The long-term owner of the framework is not yet decided.

## How a change is made

1. **Propose.** Open a GitHub issue that states the problem and the change. A small correction
   can go directly to a pull request.
2. **Discuss.** The discussion is public, in the issue.
3. **Submit.** Open a pull request. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).
4. **Review.** A maintainer checks the change against the hard rules, the writing standards and
   the level descriptors.
5. **Decide.** A maintainer accepts or declines the change and gives the reason in the pull
   request.

A change to the level descriptors, the credit and assessment rules, or this document is a major
decision. It stays open for comment for at least 14 days before a maintainer decides.

## Versions

Each unit, qualification, profile and rule document has its own version number with three
parts: major, minor and patch.

| Part | Changes when |
|---|---|
| Major | The change alters what a learner must do or show, or how a profile is scored |
| Minor | The change adds content or makes a requirement clearer |
| Patch | The change corrects an error |

Version 0 means a draft. Version 1.0.0 is the first version that is ready for use. The change
history of each file is in the repository.

## Rules that no change can break

1. No material from a proprietary qualification or profiling tool. All content is original.
2. No trade marks of other organisations in product names.
3. No statement that Open Leadership is accredited, regulated, endorsed or approved, or
   equivalent to another qualification.
4. No branding that looks like the branding of another organisation.
5. Open foundations only, with each source in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).
6. Profiling tools protect personal data by design.

## Honest claims by users

An organisation that uses Open Leadership must not say that Quarterdeck, a regulator or a
professional body approves its delivery or its certificates. The wording for certificates is in
the [credit and assessment rules](framework/credit-and-assessment-rules.md).

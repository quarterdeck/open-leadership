# The Open Leadership manifesto

**Leadership belongs to everybody.**

Every team deserves a good manager. Every person who leads others deserves the means to learn
how. Today, much of that knowledge sits behind membership fees, licence agreements and
certified-practitioner schemes. We think it should be open.

## What we believe

1. **Good management is a public good.** Poor management costs people their health, their
   progress and their pay. The knowledge that prevents it should be free to read.
2. **Nobody owns the knowledge of how to lead.** It comes from decades of public research and
   from the daily practice of millions of managers. It is common property.
3. **A standard should be open to inspection.** Anyone should be able to read what a
   qualification asks for, how it is assessed and why.
4. **Price should never decide who learns to lead.** A charity, a school, a start-up and a
   ten-person firm need good managers as much as a large corporation does.
5. **Tools about people should be transparent.** If a questionnaire describes you, you should be
   able to see the questions, the scoring and the evidence behind them.
6. **Practice is the proof.** What a manager does at work matters more than the fee they paid or
   the letters after their name.
7. **Open work gets better.** A method that anyone can test, question and improve will outgrow a
   method that is kept secret.

## What we do

- **We publish everything.** Every unit, guide, questionnaire item and line of scoring code is
  in this repository, free to use, under the MIT licence.
- **We build on open foundations.** Public standards, published research and public-domain
  resources. We list every source.
- **We write our own material.** We write the content ourselves, and we use public-domain and
  openly licensed material that we name in our sources. We take nothing from proprietary
  programmes or tools.
- **We show our working.** We publish our evidence and our limits, including what we have yet to
  prove.
- **We invite challenge.** Anyone can propose a change, and the reasons for each decision are
  public.

## What we are honest about

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. No regulator or professional body
accredits or endorses it, and we will never suggest otherwise. Its value rests on the quality of
the content, which anyone can read and judge.

## The invitation

Take it. Teach it. Adapt it. Improve it. Share what you learn.

Leadership is too important to keep behind a gate.

# OL3-CHG-01: Supporting change and improvement

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to help a team through a change and make a small improvement to how work is done.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Explain why change happens and how it affects a team | 1.1 Describe the reasons for a change in their organisation<br>1.2 Explain how people commonly react to change and why their reactions differ<br>1.3 Identify the effects of a change on the work and on the members of their team |
| 2. Apply methods that help a team through a change | 2.1 Explain a change to the team: what changes, why, when and what stays the same<br>2.2 Use questions and listening to find the concerns of team members<br>2.3 Plan the practical steps that the team needs, such as training, new procedures and cover<br>2.4 Identify problems during the change and report them to the person who is responsible for it |
| 3. Carry out and review a small improvement to how work is done | 3.1 Identify a problem in a work process, with evidence<br>3.2 Plan a small improvement with the people who do the work<br>3.3 Carry out the improvement and use a measure to show the result<br>3.4 Review their own part in the change and the improvement, and identify what to do differently |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Sources of change: customers, costs, technology, law, new owners or leaders,
errors and complaints. Change that the team chooses and change that arrives from above. Common
reactions over time: shock, resistance, trial, acceptance. Reasons for different reactions:
what the person loses, past experience, trust in managers, how much say they had. Effects on
tasks, skills, hours, status and relationships.

**Outcome 2.** The position of the team leader: they did not choose the change, and they must
explain it. Honest answers, which include "I do not know yet". Team briefings and one-to-one
conversations. Rumour, and how to correct it. Concerns that the team leader can solve and
concerns that they pass up. Practical readiness: who needs training, who covers the work, when
the old way stops. Early problems as useful information for the people who lead the change.

**Outcome 3.** Kinds of waste in a process: waiting, rework, double entry, movement, unused
skills. Simple evidence: a tally, a time log, a count of errors, the complaints of customers. A
process map on one page. Ideas from the people who do the work. A small trial before a
permanent change. A measure before and after. The limits of the team leader's authority, and
when to ask for approval. How to make the new way the standard way.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Mei leads the evening shift of 11 packers at a bakery that supplies supermarkets. In three
weeks the factory replaces paper batch sheets with handheld scanners. The day shift had
training last month. The evening shift has had none, and the rumour is that the scanners will
measure each person's speed. Two long-serving packers say that they will refuse to use them.
Mei has also seen that her shift loses about 20 minutes each night while people look for the
correct labels.

Tasks:

1. Plan what Mei says to the shift about the scanners, and what she asks her manager first.
2. Identify the practical steps that the evening shift needs before the start date.
3. Plan a small improvement to the label problem. State how Mei will measure the result.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, witness statement,
reflective account, professional discussion.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence for outcomes 1 and 2 comes from a real change that affected the learner's team
  or a team that they worked with. The learner did not need to choose the change.
- The evidence for outcome 3 is one small improvement that the learner carried out, with a
  measure taken before and after.
- A witness statement from a manager or a team member confirms the learner's part in the
  change and in the improvement.
- The names of team members do not appear in the evidence.

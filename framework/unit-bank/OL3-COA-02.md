Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to complete and review a set of coaching conversations at work.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan a set of coaching conversations with a person at work | 1.1 Explain to the person what the coaching is for, how the conversations work and what stays private<br>1.2 Identify with the person the aim of the coaching and the signs that will show progress<br>1.3 Plan the number, length, time and place of the conversations |
| 2. Carry out a set of coaching conversations | 2.1 Carry out each conversation in stages that they can explain, from the aim of the person to an action that the person chooses<br>2.2 Use the actions from one conversation as the start of the next<br>2.3 Use a short record of each conversation that keeps private information safe<br>2.4 Identify a matter that is outside the limits of coaching and refer it to the correct person or service |
| 3. Review the coaching and their own practice | 3.1 Review with the person the progress made against the aim<br>3.2 Use feedback from the person to identify the strong and weak points of their coaching<br>3.3 Plan their own development as a coach |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** A simple coaching agreement, spoken or written: the aim, the roles, the number of
conversations, what stays private and the exceptions. Aims that the person owns, compared with
aims that the manager gives. Signs of progress that both people can see. A private place and
protected time. Coaching a person in your own team compared with a person from another team.
The agreement of the person's manager, where one is necessary.

**Outcome 2.** The stages of a conversation and how much time each one needs. How to start a
conversation with a review of the last actions. What to do when an action was not done. Records:
date, aim, actions, no private detail that is not necessary. Where records are kept and who can
see them, with reference to UK data protection law. Limits of coaching: health, a grievance, a
disciplinary matter, a personal crisis. Sources of help: the line manager, the people team, an
employee assistance service, a doctor.

**Outcome 3.** A last conversation that looks back at the aim. Evidence of progress: results,
the view of the person, the view of their manager. Questions that get honest feedback about the
coach. Patterns across the set of conversations, compared with the review of one conversation.
A development plan with one or two actions and a date.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Aisha leads a team of eight in the council tax office of a district council. Her manager asks
her to coach Marek from the benefits team, who wants to become a team leader. They agree to
have five conversations in ten weeks. In the third conversation Marek says that he has not done
his actions. He then says that he sleeps badly and is afraid of his own manager. He asks Aisha
to tell nobody. Aisha's manager has already asked her how Marek is doing.

Tasks:

1. Describe what Aisha and Marek must agree before the first conversation.
2. Explain what Aisha does about the things that Marek told her, and what she says to her
   manager.
3. Plan the fourth conversation.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: portfolio of work evidence, observation at work, witness statement, reflective
account, professional discussion.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from at least six hours of coaching with at least two people at work. Each
  person has three or more conversations.
- It includes the agreement made with each person, a short record of each conversation and the
  review of progress at the end.
- An assessor or a competent witness observes at least one conversation, with the consent of
  the person coached, or reviews a recording made with that consent.
- Records are made anonymous. Feedback from each person coached is included.

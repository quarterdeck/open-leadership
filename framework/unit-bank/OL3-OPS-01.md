its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to plan, allocate and monitor the work of a team.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan the work of a team for a set period | 1.1 Identify the tasks, deadlines and standards that the team must meet in the period<br>1.2 Explain how they decide the order of tasks when time is short<br>1.3 Plan the work for the period, with the time and the people that each task needs |
| 2. Apply the plan when they allocate work to team members | 2.1 Explain how they match tasks to the skills, workload and working hours of each person<br>2.2 Use a briefing to give a team member a task, its standard and its deadline<br>2.3 Identify the tasks that depend on other teams or suppliers, and agree the timing with them |
| 3. Review progress and adjust the plan | 3.1 Use a simple method to monitor progress against the plan<br>3.2 Carry out a change to the plan when work falls behind or a person is absent<br>3.3 Describe the point at which they escalate a problem to their manager<br>3.4 Review how well their planning worked and state what to change next time |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Where the team's work comes from: orders, rotas, service standards, requests from
the manager. Daily, weekly and monthly plans. Urgent work compared with important work. How to
estimate the time for a task from past records. Fixed points in the period: deliveries, opening
hours, cut-off times, leave that is already approved. Spare time in a plan for work that nobody
expected.

**Outcome 2.** A fair share of work, which includes the tasks that nobody likes. Skills,
experience, contracted hours and agreed adjustments. A record of who can do which task. The
content of a good briefing: the task, the reason, the standard, the deadline, the person to ask.
A check that the person understood. Work that passes between teams, and what each side needs
from the other.

**Outcome 3.** Simple methods to monitor work: a task board, a checklist, a short daily meeting,
counts at fixed times. Early signs that work is behind. Options when it is: move people, change
the order, ask for more time, ask for help. The limits of the team leader's authority. What
the manager needs to know, and when. A short review at the end of the period.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Callum leads the early shift of eight sorters and drivers at a parcel depot. Volumes rise by a
third in the four weeks before Christmas. Two agency workers start on Monday and do not know
the scanners. His most experienced driver has asked for leave in the week that the depot
manager has marked as the peak. Yesterday the sort finished late and 140 parcels missed the
vans. The depot manager wants a plan for next week by Friday.

Tasks:

1. Make a plan for next week's early shifts that shows who does which work and when.
2. Decide how to deal with the leave request. State what Callum can decide and what he must
   escalate.
3. Propose a simple check that shows by 6.30 each morning if the sort is on time.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
witness statement, professional discussion.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own planning of real work for a team or a set of tasks,
  over a period of at least four weeks.
- It includes at least two plans that the learner made, with a record of the progress against
  each one.
- It includes one example of a change to a plan, with the reason and the result.
- Rotas and records that name real people are made anonymous.

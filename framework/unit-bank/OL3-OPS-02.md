# OL3-OPS-02: Solving problems and making decisions

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to find the cause of a problem at work, compare options and make a decision.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify the cause of a problem at work | 1.1 Describe the problem with facts: what happens, where, when and how often<br>1.2 Use information from more than one source to find the possible causes<br>1.3 Identify the most likely cause and explain the reasons |
| 2. Plan a solution from a comparison of options | 2.1 Identify two or more options that deal with the cause<br>2.2 Use stated criteria to compare the options<br>2.3 Explain the decision, and who has the authority to make it |
| 3. Carry out a decision and review the result | 3.1 Plan the actions, the people and the dates that the decision needs<br>3.2 Explain the decision to the people that it affects<br>3.3 Review with evidence how far the decision solved the problem<br>3.4 Review their own approach to the problem and state what to change next time |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** A symptom compared with a cause. A problem statement that contains facts and no
blame. Sources of information: records, counts, observation of the work, the people who do the
work, customers. Simple methods to find a cause: ask "why" again and again, sort the possible
causes into groups (people, method, equipment, materials, surroundings), compare the times when
the problem occurs with the times when it does not. A test of the likely cause before action.

**Outcome 2.** A quick fix compared with a solution that removes the cause. How to collect
ideas from the team. Criteria for a comparison: cost, time, risk, effect on customers, effect
on the team, rules and safety. A simple table of options against criteria. Decisions that the
team leader can make, decisions for the manager, and decisions to make with the team. The cost
of no decision.

**Outcome 3.** An action plan with names and dates. The people to tell, and what each one needs
to know. A measure, set before the change, that shows if the problem has gone. A date for the
check. What to do if the problem comes back. Common errors: a decision made too fast, a
decision made on one person's account, a solution that moves the problem to another team.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Priya supervises the housekeeping team of eleven people at a 90-room hotel. On three days last
week, more than twenty rooms were not ready at the 3 pm check-in time. Reception staff blame
slow cleaning. The housekeepers say that clean linen arrives late from the laundry contractor
and that reception agrees late check-outs without telling them. The general manager wants to
bring in agency cleaners, which the department's budget cannot pay for after this month. Guest
reviews now mention the delays.

Tasks:

1. List the facts that Priya needs before she can name the cause. State where she can get each
   one.
2. Compare three options against criteria that you select.
3. State which decision is Priya's own and which decision she must take to the general manager.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
witness statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real problem in the learner's own area of work, which the learner
  followed from the first report to a review of the result.
- It includes the information that the learner collected, the comparison of the options and the
  action plan.
- It includes evidence of the result, such as counts or records from before and after the
  decision.
- People who are named in the evidence are made anonymous.

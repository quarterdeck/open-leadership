## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to use data and common digital tools to monitor work and report results.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify the data that shows how the work of the team is going | 1.1 Identify the measures that the team is responsible for, and the source of the data for each one<br>1.2 Explain how they check that data is accurate, complete and up to date<br>1.3 Describe their duties under UK data protection law and the organisation's rules when they handle data about people |
| 2. Use digital tools to organise, analyse and share information | 2.1 Use a spreadsheet to sort, filter and total a set of data<br>2.2 Use a chart or a table to show a trend or a comparison<br>2.3 Use shared digital tools to plan work and keep the team informed<br>2.4 Apply the organisation's security rules for passwords, devices and the sharing of files |
| 3. Use data to report results and review their practice | 3.1 Explain what a set of data shows and what it does not show<br>3.2 Use data to recommend an action in a short report to their manager<br>3.3 Review how they use data and digital tools, and state what to change next time |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Common team measures: volume, time, quality, cost, attendance, customer
feedback. Where data comes from: work systems, forms, counts by hand. Causes of bad data: late
entry, missing entry, two versions of one file, unclear definitions. Simple checks: compare
with a second source, look for gaps and values that cannot be right. Personal data and
sensitive personal data in general terms. Use data only for its purpose, keep it only as long
as necessary, and let only the right people see it.

**Outcome 2.** Spreadsheet basics: rows and columns, sort, filter, sum, average, count, simple
formulas. The right chart for the job: a line for a trend, bars for a comparison. A chart that
misleads, for example an axis that does not start at zero. Shared calendars, shared documents,
task lists, team messages and video meetings. One agreed place for each file. Strong passwords,
locked screens, care with personal devices, and the report of a suspicious message. A check of
the output of an automated or artificial intelligence tool before it is used.

**Outcome 3.** A total compared with an average. One unusual week compared with a trend. Small
numbers, and why they need care. Data that shows what happened, compared with the reason that
it happened. A short report: the question, the data, the finding, the recommendation. Honest
reports of bad results. Time that a tool saves, and time that a tool costs.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Gareth leads a team of seven advisers who answer council tax enquiries for a county council.
Each month he copies figures by hand from three systems into a report for his manager. The work
takes most of a day. Last month a total was wrong, and a councillor quoted it in a public
meeting. Gareth has also learned that an adviser emailed a spreadsheet of residents' names and
arrears to her personal address so that she could finish it at home. She is his best performer
and meant well.

Tasks:

1. Propose a faster way to make the monthly report, with a check that would have found the
   wrong total.
2. State what Gareth must do about the spreadsheet that left the council, and whom he must
   tell.
3. Select two charts for the report. State what each one shows.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
professional discussion, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from data about the real work of the learner's team or area, from a period
  of at least four weeks.
- It includes a spreadsheet and a chart that the learner made, and one short report that the
  learner sent to a manager.
- It shows the learner's use of at least one shared digital tool with other people.
- Personal data is removed or made anonymous before the evidence leaves the workplace.

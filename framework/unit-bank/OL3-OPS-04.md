## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to plan and deliver a small project on time.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan a small project | 1.1 Describe the aim, the expected result and the limits of time and cost, as agreed with the person who asked for the project<br>1.2 Identify the tasks, their order and the person who does each one<br>1.3 Identify the people that the project affects and plan how to keep them informed<br>1.4 Identify the main risks to the project and plan a response to each one |
| 2. Carry out the project | 2.1 Use the plan to monitor progress against the dates<br>2.2 Carry out a change to the plan when a problem occurs, and record the reason<br>2.3 Explain the progress and the problems of the project to the person who asked for it |
| 3. Review the project | 3.1 Carry out a handover, so that the result stays in use<br>3.2 Review the result against the aim<br>3.3 Review their own part in the project and state what to change next time |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** A project compared with daily work: a start, an end and a result. A small
project at this level: a few weeks, a few people, small cost or no cost. A one-page statement of
the aim, the result, the end date and the things that the project does not include. A list of
tasks, each with an owner and a date. Tasks that must wait for other tasks. A simple chart of
tasks against weeks. The people who use the result, the people who do the work and the people
who can stop it. Simple risks: absence, late supply, a lack of time next to daily work.

**Outcome 2.** A weekly check of the tasks that are done, late or blocked. Early action on a
late task. Requests for extra work, and how to answer them. Changes that the team leader can
make and changes that need agreement. A short, regular progress report that tells the truth.
How to ask for help in good time.

**Outcome 3.** Handover: instructions, training, the name of the person who owns the result
after the project. A check some weeks later that the result is still in use. A comparison of
the result, the time and the cost with the plan. A short review with the people who took part:
what went well, what did not, what to do differently.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Ngozi is a team leader at a 40-bed care home. The home manager has asked her to move the shift
handover notes from paper to tablets within eight weeks, before an inspection that is expected
in the spring. There is money for six tablets and no extra staff time. Ngozi still works her
normal shifts. The night staff say that paper is quicker. The wireless signal is weak in one
wing. A relative recently complained that a message about her mother's diet was lost between
shifts.

Tasks:

1. Write a one-page plan: the aim, the tasks in order, the people and the dates.
2. Name three risks to the project. State a response to each one.
3. Describe how Ngozi can tell after the eight weeks that the project worked.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, portfolio of work evidence, work-based assignment, witness
statement, professional discussion.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real project that the learner planned and led, of at least four
  weeks, with at least two other people involved.
- It includes the plan, at least two progress reports and the review.
- It includes a witness statement or other confirmation from the person who asked for the
  project.
- If the project is not complete when the assessment occurs, the learner reviews the progress to
  that date and explains the plan for the remaining work.

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to lead a small team so that its members know what to do, work well together and meet their objectives.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Explain the role of a team leader in a small team | 1.1 Describe the responsibilities of a team leader and the limits of their authority<br>1.2 Explain how the objectives of the team support the work of the organisation<br>1.3 Identify the skills, experience and working preferences of each team member |
| 2. Plan and direct the daily work of a team | 2.1 Plan the allocation of work so that it fits the skills and the workload of team members<br>2.2 Carry out a team briefing that states the task, the standard and the deadline<br>2.3 Use regular checks to follow progress and deal with a delay<br>2.4 Identify a problem that is outside their authority and escalate it to the correct person |
| 3. Review how well the team works together and how well they lead it | 3.1 Use methods that build cooperation and motivation in the team<br>3.2 Review the results of the team against its objectives<br>3.3 Review their own practice as a team leader and state what to change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The difference between doing the work and leading the people who do it. The move
from team member to team leader, and the leadership of former peers. What a team leader decides
and what a manager decides. Where the objectives of the team come from. Ways to learn about team
members: conversation, observation, records of training. Fair treatment of each member.

**Outcome 2.** Allocation of work by skill, workload, development need and fairness. The content
of a clear briefing: what, why, who, when, to what standard. Checks that the team has understood.
Delegation of a task compared with the issue of an instruction. Short daily or weekly progress
checks. Common causes of delay: absence, unclear priorities, missing materials, a dependency on
another team. What to escalate, to whom and with what information.

**Outcome 3.** What helps people to cooperate: a shared purpose, clear roles, respect, reliable
behaviour from the leader. What motivates different people: recognition, interesting work,
progress, a say in decisions. Team meetings that people find useful. Simple measures of team
results: output, quality, time, the views of customers. Sources of feedback on the leader: the
team, the manager, the results. A short written plan of changes.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Dariusz became team leader of the night shift at a parcel sorting depot three weeks ago. He
worked on the same shift for four years, and the seven people in the team are his friends. The
shift has missed its sorting target on six nights this month. Two team members swap tasks
between themselves without telling him, and a new starter says that nobody has shown her how to
use the scanner. Dariusz gives the briefing at the start of each shift from memory, and it is
different each night.

Tasks:

1. Describe what Dariusz must decide himself and what he must take to the depot manager.
2. Plan the briefing and the allocation of work for the next shift.
3. State how Dariusz can check progress during the shift and what he does if the team falls
   behind.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
witness statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own leadership of a team of at least two people over a
  period of at least six weeks.
- It includes a plan of work that the learner allocated, and an observation or a witness
  statement of a briefing that the learner gave.
- It includes one example of a problem that the learner escalated, or an account of how they
  would escalate the problem in the scenario.
- Records of real people are made anonymous.

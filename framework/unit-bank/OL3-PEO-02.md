# OL3-PEO-02: Managing individual performance

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to set clear objectives, give feedback and deal with good and poor performance.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Set clear objectives and standards with a team member | 1.1 Explain the standard of work and behaviour that the organisation expects in a role<br>1.2 Agree objectives with a team member that are specific, measurable and achievable<br>1.3 Describe how they will check progress with the person between reviews |
| 2. Give feedback and recognise good performance | 2.1 Identify evidence of the performance of a person from the work and from observation<br>2.2 Give feedback that describes what the person did and the effect that it had<br>2.3 Use recognition that fits the person and the achievement<br>2.4 Carry out a one-to-one meeting and record the points agreed |
| 3. Deal with poor performance within the limits of their role | 3.1 Identify possible causes of poor performance in a conversation with the person<br>3.2 Plan support, a clear target and a review date with the person<br>3.3 Explain when and how to refer a case to their manager under the organisation's procedure<br>3.4 Review how they handled a performance conversation and state what to change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Where the standards of a role are written: the job description, procedures,
targets, the code of conduct. Standards for results and standards for behaviour. What makes an
objective clear to the person who must meet it. The number of objectives that one person can
hold in mind. Short, regular checks compared with a wait until the formal review.

**Outcome 2.** Facts compared with opinions and hearsay. Feedback soon after the event, in
private, about the work and not about the character of the person. Praise that names what was
good, so that the person can repeat it. Low-cost recognition: thanks, a mention to the manager,
a new responsibility. Differences in how people like to be recognised. The structure of a
one-to-one meeting: the view of the person, the view of the team leader, the next steps. A short
record that both people can see.

**Outcome 3.** A question before a conclusion: does the person know the standard, have the skill,
have the tools, have a problem outside work? Support that a team leader can give: a
demonstration, practice, a partner, more frequent checks. The difference between an informal
conversation and a formal procedure. Why a team leader does not issue warnings alone. Health or
disability as a possible cause, and the need for advice. Notes that are factual and dated.
Confidentiality in a small team.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Priya is the team leader of the fresh food counters in a large supermarket. Marcus joined eight
months ago and customers like him. In the last month he has been late four times, and twice the
temperature log on his counter was not filled in. Priya has said nothing so far, because the
store is short of staff and she does not want him to leave. Yesterday a colleague complained
that she covers for him each morning. On the same counter, Bronwen has cut waste by a fifth with
her own ordering checklist, and nobody has thanked her.

Tasks:

1. State the facts that Priya needs before she speaks to Marcus, and plan the conversation.
2. Write two objectives that Priya can agree with Marcus, with a review date.
3. Describe how Priya can recognise the work of Bronwen.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
witness statement, professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own one-to-one work with at least two team members over
  a period of at least six weeks.
- It includes objectives that the learner agreed with one person, and a record or account of
  two feedback conversations: one about good work and one about work below the standard.
- Records of real people are made anonymous.
- For criteria 3.1 and 3.2, if no case of poor performance occurred in the period, the learner
  uses the scenario.

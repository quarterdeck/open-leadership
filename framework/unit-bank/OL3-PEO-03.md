Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to communicate clearly with a team, with managers and with customers, in speech and in writing.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan communication that fits its purpose and its audience | 1.1 Identify the purpose of a message and what the audience needs from it<br>1.2 Explain the advantages and the limits of the channels that are available at work<br>1.3 Describe barriers to communication and ways to reduce them |
| 2. Communicate in speech with a team, with managers and with customers | 2.1 Carry out a spoken briefing that is clear and in a logical order<br>2.2 Use questions and listening to check what another person means<br>2.3 Describe a problem to a manager with the facts, the effect and a proposed action<br>2.4 Use words and a tone that fit the needs of a customer |
| 3. Communicate in writing and review their communication | 3.1 Use plain language and a clear structure in a written message or a short report<br>3.2 Apply the organisation's rules on confidentiality and personal data to a communication<br>3.3 Review feedback on their communication and state what to change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Purposes: to inform, to instruct, to ask, to persuade, to record. What the
audience knows already and what it must do next. Channels: a conversation, a meeting, a
telephone or video call, email, a messaging application, a notice, a report. When a written
record is necessary. Barriers: jargon, noise, shift patterns, distance, a first language that is
not English, a hearing or sight impairment, strong feelings, too many messages.

**Outcome 2.** The main point first. One subject at a time. Checks of understanding that do more
than ask "is that clear?". Open and closed questions. Listening without interruption, and a
summary of what the other person said. Body language and tone of voice, and their limits on a
call. A report to a manager that is short and gives the facts before the opinion. Customers who
are confused, hurried or upset. What a team leader can promise a customer and what they cannot.

**Outcome 3.** Short sentences and common words. Headings, lists and a clear subject line. The
action and the date stated plainly. A check of facts, names and figures before the message is
sent. Tone in email and messaging, and how it is misread. Who should receive a copy and who
should not. UK data protection law in general terms: personal data is shared only with people
who need it. Sources of feedback: replies, questions, mistakes that follow a message, the views
of colleagues.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Callum leads a team of six in the council tax office of a district council. From Monday the
office telephone line will close at 4 pm, one hour earlier, so that the team can clear a backlog
of post. His manager told him on Thursday afternoon in a corridor. Two of the team work part
time and are not in on Friday. Last month Callum announced a rota change in a group message, and
half the team said that they did not see it. Residents who call after 4 pm will hear a recorded
message that nobody has written yet.

Tasks:

1. Plan how Callum tells each member of the team about the change, and how he checks that they
   understand it.
2. Write the recorded message for residents in plain language.
3. Write a short email from Callum to his manager that states one risk and proposes an action.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
witness statement, presentation, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence includes at least three real communications by the learner: one spoken to a
  team, one to a manager and one written.
- At least one spoken communication is observed, or is confirmed by a witness statement.
- If the learner has no contact with external customers, an internal customer of the team is
  acceptable for criterion 2.4.
- Names and personal data are removed from the work documents.

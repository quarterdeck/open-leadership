# OL3-PEO-04: Handling conflict and difficult conversations

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to prepare for and hold a difficult conversation, and resolve conflict in a team.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Explain the causes and the effects of conflict in a team | 1.1 Describe common causes of conflict between people at work<br>1.2 Identify early signs of conflict in a team<br>1.3 Explain the effect on people and on the work when conflict is not dealt with |
| 2. Prepare for and hold a difficult conversation | 2.1 Plan a difficult conversation: its purpose, the facts, the place and the time<br>2.2 Carry out a conversation that states the issue plainly and lets the other person reply<br>2.3 Use listening and calm language when the other person is upset or angry<br>2.4 Record the result of the conversation and the actions agreed |
| 3. Resolve conflict between team members and review their practice | 3.1 Use a structured method to help two team members agree how they will work together<br>3.2 Explain when to refer a conflict to their manager or to a formal procedure<br>3.3 Review how they handled a conflict or a difficult conversation and state what to change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Causes: unclear roles, unfair workload, competition for resources, different
standards, different styles of work, poor communication, change, pressure, old grievances.
Disagreement about the work, which can be useful, compared with conflict between people. Early
signs: silence in meetings, small groups, complaints through third parties, messages copied to
the manager, absence. Effects: errors, delay, stress, people who leave, a problem that becomes a
formal complaint.

**Outcome 2.** Why people delay a difficult conversation, and the cost of delay. Typical
subjects: lateness, behaviour, personal hygiene, a refused request, bad news. Preparation: the
purpose, the facts, the result that is wanted, the likely reply. A private place and enough
time. The opening sentence. Description of behaviour and its effect, without labels. Silence,
tears and anger, and when to pause the meeting. The team leader's own feelings and what sets
them off. Agreement on the next steps. A short factual note.

**Outcome 3.** Responses to conflict: to avoid it, to give way, to impose, to split the
difference, to work on the problem together. When each response fits. A simple method for two
people: each person speaks without interruption, the leader states the points of agreement, the
two people agree the next steps and a date to review. The neutral position of the leader. Cases
that are not for a team leader to settle: bullying, harassment, discrimination, threats, a
grievance. The organisation's procedures and where to get advice. Follow-up after an agreement.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Ngozi is a senior care assistant who leads the day shift in a residential care home. Two of her
care assistants, Sheila and Jordan, no longer speak to each other. Sheila says that Jordan
leaves the hardest personal care for other people. Jordan says that Sheila criticises him in
front of residents. Handovers between them are now short, and last week a change to the
medication of one resident was not passed on. The home manager has asked Ngozi to deal with it
before it becomes a formal complaint. Ngozi trained under Sheila and finds her hard to
challenge.

Tasks:

1. Plan the conversation that Ngozi must have with Sheila. Write her opening sentences.
2. Describe a method that Ngozi can use to bring Sheila and Jordan to an agreement.
3. Identify what Ngozi must report to the home manager now, and why.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, observation at work, witness statement, professional
discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence covers at least one difficult conversation that the learner held and one conflict
  between other people that the learner helped to resolve.
- It includes the learner's written plan for the conversation and a reflective account of what
  happened.
- If the learner cannot use a real case, a role play of the scenario that an assessor observes
  is acceptable for criteria 2.2, 2.3 and 3.1.
- Real people are made anonymous, and the learner does not record a real conversation without
  the consent of the other person.

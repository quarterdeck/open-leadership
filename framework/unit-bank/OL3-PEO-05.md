## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to support the wellbeing of team members and treat people fairly.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Explain the part of a team leader in wellbeing and fair treatment | 1.1 Describe the features of work that affect the wellbeing of team members<br>1.2 Explain the responsibilities of a team leader under the organisation's policies on health, safety and equality<br>1.3 Identify the sources of support that the organisation and outside services offer |
| 2. Support the wellbeing of team members | 2.1 Identify signs that a team member is under strain<br>2.2 Carry out a supportive conversation that stays within the limits of their role<br>2.3 Plan changes to the daily work of the team that reduce pressure<br>2.4 Explain when and how to refer a concern to their manager or to a specialist |
| 3. Treat team members fairly and include each of them | 3.1 Apply fair and consistent treatment when they allocate work, shifts and opportunities<br>3.2 Use ways of working that let each team member take part<br>3.3 Describe how to respond to behaviour that excludes or demeans a person<br>3.4 Review their own practice and state what to change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** What affects wellbeing at work: workload, hours, control over the work,
relationships, clear expectations, security, the physical conditions. Life outside work: caring,
money, health, bereavement. The duty of care of an employer under health and safety law, in
general terms. The characteristics that UK equality law protects, in general terms, and the idea
of a reasonable adjustment. What the team leader does and what the manager or the people
function does. Support: occupational health, an employee assistance service, a GP, charities,
trade union representatives.

**Outcome 2.** Signs of strain: a change in behaviour, mistakes, lateness, withdrawal, short
temper, long hours, frequent short absences. A private question, asked with care. Listening
without diagnosis and without advice on medical matters. What the leader can keep confidential
and what they must pass on. Changes within the authority of a team leader: the order of tasks,
breaks, rotation of hard duties, a partner for a task. Regular contact during an absence, if
the person agrees. A return to work.

**Outcome 3.** Fairness in the things that a team leader controls: rotas, overtime, the popular
and unpopular tasks, training places, praise. Favourites, and how they look to the team.
Part-time staff, night staff and remote staff. Meetings and social events that all members can
attend. Different needs: faith, disability, caring duties, age, language. Jokes and remarks that
exclude. A quiet word, a direct challenge, a report: when each fits. Reflection on whom the
leader talks to most and whom least.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Megan is the housekeeping team leader in a city-centre hotel with 140 rooms. Her team of eleven
includes four people for whom English is a second language. Since two people left in the summer,
each room attendant cleans three more rooms a day. Ioana, usually the fastest in the team, has
been in tears twice this week and has started to skip her break. Megan writes the rota, and the
same three people get the weekend shifts off each month because they ask first. The morning
briefing is fast and spoken, and some of the team copy what the others do afterwards.

Tasks:

1. Plan the conversation that Megan has with Ioana. State what Megan must not promise.
2. Identify two changes within the authority of Megan that reduce pressure on the team, and
   one matter that she must take to the hotel manager.
3. Describe how Megan can make the rota and the briefing fair to all members of the team.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, witness statement,
professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own work with a team over a period of at least six
  weeks.
- It includes one example of support that the learner gave to a team member, and one example
  of a change that made the treatment of the team more fair.
- The learner does not include health information about a real person. Accounts of real cases
  are made anonymous and leave out details that identify the person.
- For criteria 2.2 and 3.3, if no case occurred in the period, the learner uses the scenario.

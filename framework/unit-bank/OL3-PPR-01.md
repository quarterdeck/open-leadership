## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to describe the work of a people function from recruitment to exit, and their own role in it.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Describe the work of a people function and how it supports the organisation | 1.1 Describe the services that the people function gives to managers and to employees<br>1.2 Explain how the people function in their organisation is organised and who does which work<br>1.3 Explain how the work of the people function supports the aims of the organisation |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** What a people function does: recruitment, records and pay data, learning, advice
to managers, employee relations, policy. The difference between administration, advice and
decisions. Ways to organise the function: one generalist, a small team, a shared service desk,
specialists, work that an outside supplier does. What line managers do and what the people
function does. Small organisations with no people function. How people work affects cost,
service, safety and the reputation of the employer.

**Outcome 2.** The stages: attraction, selection, offer, onboarding, probation, development,
performance, pay and progression, changes to terms, absence and family leave, exit. For each
stage: the task, the document, the owner and the time limit. Examples of failure: a late
contract, a missed probation review, a leaver who is paid in error, a return from long absence
with no plan. What the employee sees at each stage. Hand-overs between the people function,
payroll, IT and the line manager.

**Outcome 3.** The limits of an administrator or assistant role. Matters to pass on: a legal
question, a complaint, a disclosure about health, a request that breaks a policy. Confidentiality
and trust. Service standards and response times. Sources of feedback: managers, new starters,
leavers, colleagues in payroll. Small improvements: a checklist, a template, a clearer hand-over.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Callum is an HR assistant at a family-owned food manufacturer with 240 employees. The people
team is Callum and an HR manager. Last month a packer who left was paid for four more weeks, and
her door pass still worked. Two new starters arrived on a Monday with no locker, no safety boots
and no login. Shift managers say that the HR forms are slow, so they now send changes directly
to payroll by text message. The HR manager wants every change to come through Callum. The shift
managers want fewer forms.

Tasks:

1. Draw the stages from offer to exit at this company. Show who owns each hand-over.
2. Identify where the leaver and the new starters were failed, and the cause in each case.
3. Propose two changes that Callum can make himself, and one matter that he must pass to the HR
   manager.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
witness statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own work in or with a people function over a period of
  at least two months.
- It includes a description of the employee life cycle in the learner's organisation, with the
  documents and owners of each stage.
- For criterion 2.4, it includes work evidence or a witness statement for tasks in two or more
  different stages.
- Records of real people are made anonymous.

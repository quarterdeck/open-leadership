## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to support a fair recruitment process and the first weeks of a new employee.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Explain what makes a recruitment process fair and lawful | 1.1 Describe the steps of the recruitment process in their organisation and the purpose of each step<br>1.2 Explain how UK equality law and the duty to check the right to work apply to recruitment<br>1.3 Identify the points in the process where unfair bias can enter, and a way to reduce it at each point |
| 2. Carry out the practical tasks of a recruitment process | 2.1 Use a job description and a person specification to prepare an advertisement<br>2.2 Apply agreed criteria to a set of applications and record the reason for each decision<br>2.3 Plan interviews or other selection activities, with adjustments for candidates who need them<br>2.4 Carry out the communication with candidates and the checks before employment to the organisation's procedure |

Plan and review the first weeks of a new employee | 3.1 Plan an induction that covers the job, the people, the rules and the practical arrangements<br>3.2 Carry out the onboarding tasks that belong to the people function, on time<br>3.3 Use the views of the new employee and their manager to review the onboarding<br>3.4 Review their own part in the process and identify what to change next time |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The steps: approval of the vacancy, job description, person specification,
advertisement, shortlist, selection, offer, checks, start. Protected characteristics in UK
equality law, direct and indirect discrimination, and reasonable adjustments for disabled
candidates. The right to work check: every candidate, the same way, before the start date.
Where bias enters: the wording of an advertisement, where it is placed, unstructured interviews,
decisions on "fit". Ways to reduce it: criteria agreed before applications open, the same
questions for each candidate, more than one assessor, written reasons.

**Outcome 2.** Essential and desirable criteria. Plain words in an advertisement, and
information on pay, hours and place of work. A scoring sheet for the shortlist. Interview
logistics: rooms, online links, panel packs, timings, access needs. Selection activities other
than interviews: a work sample, a short test, a presentation. Prompt and polite replies to each
candidate, which includes the ones who are not selected. References, qualification checks and
other checks that the role needs. Offer letters and written terms. Data protection for candidate
records, and how long to keep them.

**Outcome 3.** Contact between offer and start. The first day: a welcome, equipment, access,
safety information, a named person to ask. The first weeks: introductions, training, early
objectives, check-ins. Probation dates and reviews. The parts that belong to the people
function, to the line manager and to IT. Early signs that a new employee could leave. Questions
for a review at four weeks. Measures: time to fill a vacancy, offers accepted, leavers in the
first three months.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Oluwaseun is the recruitment coordinator for a group of six care homes. The homes need twelve
care assistants before winter. One home manager wants to skip the shortlist scoring and
interview everybody who "sounds right" on the telephone. Two candidates last month waited three
weeks for the checks that the role needs and then took other jobs. Of the eight care assistants
who started in spring, three left in the first month. One of them said that nobody expected her
on the first day.

Tasks:

1. Explain to the home manager why the scoring step matters, and offer a faster way to keep it.
2. Plan the contact with a candidate between the offer and the first day.
3. Make a first-week induction checklist for a new care assistant. State who owns each item.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
witness statement, professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own part in at least one real recruitment process, from
  the advertisement to the first weeks of the new employee.
- It includes an advertisement that the learner prepared, a shortlist record with reasons, and
  an induction plan.
- It includes the review of the onboarding with the views of one new employee and their manager.
- The names and details of candidates and employees are removed.

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to find a learning need and support a learning activity for a person or a group.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Identify a learning need of a person or a group | 1.1 Explain the difference between a learning need and other causes of a gap in performance<br>1.2 Use two or more sources of information to identify a learning need<br>1.3 Describe the need as a statement of what people will do at work after the learning |
| 2. Plan and support a learning activity that meets the need | 2.1 Identify a suitable learning activity from the methods available, with a reason<br>2.2 Plan the practical arrangements, which include the access needs of the learners<br>2.3 Carry out their own part in the activity<br>2.4 Explain how the manager of a learner can help the learner to use the learning at work |
| 3. Review the learning activity and their own part in it | 3.1 Use the views of the learners and one sign of change at work to review the activity<br>3.2 Carry out the record-keeping for the activity to the organisation's procedure<br>3.3 Review their own part in the activity and identify what to change next time |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Causes of a gap in performance other than skill or knowledge: unclear
instructions, poor tools, too little time, no feedback. Sources of information on a need: the
manager, the person, observation, errors and complaints, a new system or rule, a change of role.
Needs of one person, of a team and of all employees, such as mandatory training. A statement of
the result: "after the session, the cashier can process a refund without help".

**Outcome 2.** Methods and when each one fits: a demonstration, a short session, shadowing, a
job aid, an online module, a practice task with feedback, a buddy. Cost, time away from work and
the number of people. Arrangements: dates, places, equipment, invitations, materials, joining
instructions. Access needs: shift patterns, language, disability, confidence with technology.
The learner's own part can be a short session, a demonstration, a job aid or support for a
trainer. Before and after: the manager states why the learning matters and gives a chance to use
it.

**Outcome 3.** Simple questions at the end of an activity and some weeks later. Signs of change
at work: fewer errors, faster work, fewer requests for help, a manager's observation. Training
records: who attended, what, when, and expiry dates for certificates. Data protection for
learning records. Reflection on their own preparation, explanation and support for learners.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Bronwen is the learning coordinator for a group of four hotels in North Wales. Guest reviews
complain about slow check-in since a new booking system arrived. The operations manager asks her
to book all thirty reception staff on a full-day course. Bronwen watches two shifts. Most staff
use the system well. The night staff, who missed the launch sessions, use paper and enter the
data later. The system also freezes when two people open the same booking. Night staff cannot
attend daytime courses.

Tasks:

1. Separate the learning need from the other causes of slow check-in. State who must deal with
   the other causes.
2. Plan a learning activity for the night staff that fits their shifts.
3. State how Bronwen will know in one month whether the activity worked.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
witness statement, professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real learning activity for a person or a group that the learner
  planned and supported from the need to the review.
- It includes the sources that the learner used to identify the need, the plan for the activity
  and the materials or records that the learner made.
- An observation or a witness statement confirms the learner's own part in the activity.
- The names of learners are removed from feedback and records.

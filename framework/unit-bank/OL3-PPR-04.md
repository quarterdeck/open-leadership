## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to keep accurate people records and apply the main rules of employment law and data protection.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Explain the main rules of UK employment law that apply to daily people work | 1.1 Describe the main rights of a person at work from the offer of a job to the end of employment<br>1.2 Explain in general terms why the status of a person as an employee, a worker or a self-employed contractor matters<br>1.3 Identify the questions that are outside their authority, and the person or source to consult |
| 2. Apply UK data protection law to people records | 2.1 Explain the principles of UK data protection law as they apply to people records<br>2.2 Identify the personal data that the people function holds, where it is, and which data needs more protection<br>2.3 Apply the organisation's rules on access, sharing, storage and retention<br>2.4 Describe the action to take when a person asks for their own data and when data is lost or sent in error |
| 3. Keep accurate people records and review their own practice | 3.1 Carry out changes to people records accurately and on time<br>3.2 Use checks to find and correct errors in records<br>3.3 Review their own handling of records and data, and identify what to change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Areas of UK employment law at an introductory level: written terms, pay and
deductions, working time and holiday, family leave, sickness, protection from discrimination,
notice, and fair treatment in discipline and dismissal. Rights that start on the first day and
rights that need a period of service. Employment status and why it changes rights. The law
changes, so the learner uses a current official source. The difference between information and
advice. When to pass a question to a senior colleague or a legal adviser.

**Outcome 2.** The principles in plain words: a lawful reason, a stated purpose, only the data
that is necessary, accuracy, a time limit, security. Data that needs more protection: health,
ethnic origin, religion, trade union membership, criminal records. Where people data is: the HR
system, payroll, shared drives, email, paper files, managers' own notes. Who can see what, and
why a manager's curiosity is not a reason. Retention periods for applicants, employees and
leavers. A request by a person for their own data, and its time limit. A data breach: record it
and report it immediately to the named person.

**Outcome 3.** Records that the law or the organisation requires: contracts, right to work
checks, pay, hours, absence, training, cases. The cost of an error: wrong pay, a missed check, a
lost case. Sources of error: double entry, late forms, free text, old spreadsheets. Checks:
a second person, a report of missing fields, a comparison with payroll. Clear file names and
version control. A clear desk and a locked screen.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Tomasz is the HR administrator for a trust of five primary schools. Staff records are in the HR
system, in a spreadsheet at each school office and in paper files. A head teacher emails him to
ask for the full sickness history of a teaching assistant, "to see what is really wrong with
her". The same week, a former caretaker writes to ask for a copy of everything that the trust
holds about him. Tomasz then finds that a school office sent a list of staff home addresses to
the wrong email group.

Tasks:

1. Decide what Tomasz can give to the head teacher, and what he says in his reply.
2. List the steps and the time limit for the former caretaker's request. State who must be told.
3. State what Tomasz does about the email in the first hour, and one change that makes a
   repeat less likely.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation at work,
witness statement, professional discussion.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own work with people records over a period of at least
  two months.
- It includes examples of record changes that the learner made and of a check that found an
  error, with all personal data removed or replaced.
- For criterion 2.4, if no request or breach occurred in the period, the learner explains with
  reference to the organisation's procedure how they would act in the scenario.
- The evidence shows that the learner used a current, reliable source for a point of law. It
  does not need legal detail from memory.

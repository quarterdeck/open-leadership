## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to explain the purpose, structure and customers of their organisation and how their team contributes.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Describe the purpose, structure and customers of their organisation | 1.1 Describe the purpose, aims and values of the organisation and where its money comes from<br>1.2 Describe the structure of the organisation and where decisions are made<br>1.3 Identify the customers or service users of the organisation and what they need from it |
| 2. Explain how the world outside the organisation affects it | 2.1 Identify the outside groups that affect the organisation and what each group expects<br>2.2 Identify the rules and standards that the organisation must meet in their area of work<br>2.3 Explain how one change outside the organisation has affected the work of their team |
| 3. Explain and review the contribution of their team | 3.1 Explain how the work of the team supports the aims of the organisation<br>3.2 Identify the teams that depend on their team and the teams that their team depends on<br>3.3 Use information from customers or from other teams to plan one improvement to the team's contribution<br>3.4 Review how they explain the aims of the organisation to their team and state what to change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Types of organisation: private company, public body, charity, social enterprise,
partnership, co-operative. Purpose, aims and values, and where they are written down. Sources
of money: sales, grants, contracts, taxes, donations, fees. Structures by function, product,
place or customer. Layers, reporting lines and the organisation chart. Who decides what, and who
is accountable to whom. External customers and internal customers. What customers need: the
right result, on time, at a fair cost, with respect.

**Outcome 2.** Outside groups: customers, competitors, suppliers, partners, funders, owners,
regulators, inspectors, trade unions, neighbours, the media. Outside changes: prices, new
technology, new rules, public opinion, the labour market. Rules that apply to most
organisations, in general terms: health and safety law, UK equality law, UK data protection
law, employment law. Standards and inspections that are specific to a sector. What happens to
the organisation and to customers when a standard is not met.

**Outcome 3.** The line from the aims of the organisation to the tasks of the team. The chain
of work between teams: inputs, outputs and handovers. Where handovers fail and who pays the
cost. Sources of information: complaints, compliments, surveys, measures, a conversation with
the next team. Ways to explain aims to a team in plain words and with examples from the work.
Questions that team members ask about "why", and where to find the answers.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Kelly leads the night team of five care assistants at a care home. The home is one of nine that
a private group owns. The group wants more beds filled this year. The last inspection report
asked for better records of residents' food and drink. Kelly's team says that the charts take
time away from residents and that "head office only cares about numbers". The day team
complains that the morning handover is rushed and has gaps. A resident's daughter has
complained that nobody could tell her how her mother slept.

Tasks:

1. Explain to Kelly's team, in plain words, why the charts matter to residents, to the
   inspector and to the group.
2. Identify the people and teams that depend on the night team, and what each of them needs.
3. Plan one improvement to the handover. State the information that Kelly uses to check if it
   worked.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, presentation, professional discussion, portfolio of
work evidence, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence is about the learner's own organisation and team. A learner who is not in work
  uses an organisation that they know well, with the agreement of the assessor.
- It includes a simple map or description of the structure, the customers and the outside
  groups, in the learner's own words. Copied text from a website or a handbook is not evidence.
- For criteria 3.3 and 3.4, it includes real information from customers or from another team,
  and a record or a witness statement of the learner's explanation of the aims to the team.
- Customers, service users and colleagues are not named. No confidential business information
  is included.

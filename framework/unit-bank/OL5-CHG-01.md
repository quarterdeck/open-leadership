Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to plan and lead a change, and bring the people affected with them.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the case for a change and the conditions for it | 1.1 Analyse the causes of the need for a change, with evidence<br>1.2 Assess the forces for and against the change and the capacity of the team to absorb it<br>1.3 Analyse the effect of the change on each group of people that it touches |
| 2. Plan a change | 2.1 Select an approach to the change that fits its urgency, its size and the people affected, with reasons<br>2.2 Plan the stages, resources, responsibilities and measures of the change<br>2.3 Assess the risks to the change and to normal work while the change happens |
| 3. Apply methods that bring the people affected with the change | 3.1 Plan communication and involvement for each group of people affected<br>3.2 Apply methods of involvement that give people a real part in the design of the change<br>3.3 Analyse the causes of resistance and select a response to each cause<br>3.4 Apply the legal and contractual duties of the organisation to consult when a change affects jobs, terms or working patterns |
| 4. Evaluate a change and their own leadership of it | 4.1 Evaluate the results of the change against its measures, which include whether the new way of working has lasted<br>4.2 Evaluate their own leadership of the change and plan changes to their practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Evidence of need: data on performance, the views of customers and staff, a
change in the strategy. A case for change that states the cost of no change. Forces that push
for the change and forces that hold it back, and the strength of each. The history of change in
the team. Change fatigue and the present workload. Groups affected: the team, other teams,
customers, suppliers, union representatives. Who gains and who loses.

**Outcome 2.** Approaches: directed from above or designed with the people affected, one step
or stages, a pilot first or all sites at once. The conditions that suit each. The common
elements of staged models of change: a clear reason, a picture of the
result, support from people with authority, new habits made permanent. The limits of such
models: real change does not move in a straight line. A plan with owners, dates and measures.
Risks: loss of people, a fall in service, cost, delay. Capacity to do normal work at the same
time.

**Outcome 3.** Communication in two directions. The message, the messenger and the timing for
each group. Involvement: workshops, trials, staff who lead parts of the change. Consultation
that can change the decision compared with consultation for show. Causes of resistance: loss,
doubt about the reason, low trust, no skill yet, a real flaw in the plan. Resistance as
information. The duty to consult staff and their representatives under UK employment law, in
general terms, and when to get specialist advice. Support for managers below the learner who
must explain the change.

**Outcome 4.** Measures of result and measures of adoption. A review at a fixed date after the
change. Signs that people have returned to the old way. Unintended effects. The learner's own
behaviour during the change: visibility, consistency, response to bad news. Feedback from the
people affected.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Kwame manages three residential care homes for a care provider in the West Midlands. The
directors have decided that all homes move from paper care plans to tablets in four months,
after an inspection found gaps in records. Many care workers have done the job for 20 years.
Some have little confidence with technology, and night staff say that nobody ever asks them. One
home manager supports the change. Another tells her staff that "it will be dropped by summer,
like the last system". Agency use is high and there is no spare time on the rota.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the forces for and against this change in the three homes.
2. Plan the change for the four months. Justify where it starts and how night staff are
   involved.
3. Select a response to the home manager who expects the change to fail.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, portfolio of work evidence,
professional discussion, reflective account, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real change that the learner led or co-led and that affected at
  least five people. The change has reached a stage at which its first results can be measured.
- It includes the analysis of the case for change, the plan, examples of communication and
  involvement, and the results against the measures.
- It includes the views of at least two people who were affected by the change.
- Records of real people are made anonymous.

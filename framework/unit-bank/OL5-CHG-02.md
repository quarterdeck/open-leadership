Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to develop and test a new idea that improves a product, a service or a way of working.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse opportunities for innovation | 1.1 Analyse the needs of customers, service users or colleagues to find a problem that is worth solving<br>1.2 Apply methods that produce ideas from people with different views of the problem<br>1.3 Select one idea against stated criteria, with reasons |
| 2. Plan and apply a test of a new idea | 2.1 Plan a small test that states the assumption, the measure and the result that counts as success<br>2.2 Assess the risks, the cost and the ethics of the test for customers, staff and data<br>2.3 Apply the test and collect the evidence |
| 3. Evaluate a test and justify a recommendation | 3.1 Evaluate the results of the test against the measure of success<br>3.2 Justify a recommendation to stop, change or extend the idea, with its costs and benefits<br>3.3 Plan how the idea becomes part of normal work, if the recommendation is to extend it |
| 4. Evaluate the conditions for innovation in their team | 4.1 Assess the conditions in their team that help new ideas and the conditions that block them<br>4.2 Evaluate how their own behaviour affects the readiness of people to suggest and test ideas, and plan changes |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Innovation as a new idea put to use: in a product, a service, a process or a
way to organise work. Small steps compared with a large jump. Sources of opportunity:
complaints, workarounds, observation of users, front-line staff, other sectors, new technology.
The problem stated from the user's side before any solution. Idea methods: individual ideas
before group discussion, questions of the form "how can we", deliberate use of outsiders.
Criteria: value to the user, cost, fit with strategy, ease of test.

**Outcome 2.** The riskiest assumption in an idea. The cheapest test that can prove it wrong: a
mock-up, a manual version, one site, one week. A measure and a threshold set before the test,
to stop wishful reading of the results. Permission and sponsorship. Risks to customers and to
the reputation of the organisation. Consent, and UK data protection law where the test uses
personal data. What to do with normal work during the test.

**Outcome 3.** Results against the threshold. What else the test showed. Small samples and
their limits. The three decisions: stop, change and test again, or extend. The value of a
negative result. A short business case: cost, benefit, risk, who must agree. The step from a
test to normal operation: training, systems, budget, an owner. Why many tests never get past
this step.

**Outcome 4.** Conditions that help: time, safety to speak, tolerance of a failed test, access
to customers, a manager who asks for ideas. Conditions that block: blame, targets with no
slack, "we tried that before", ideas that go up and never come back. The manager's response to
a bad idea and to a failed test. Credit for the people who had the idea.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Siobhan is the operations manager for a group of four seaside hotels. Online reviews praise the
rooms and complain about breakfast: queues at 8:30 and cold food. A receptionist suggests that
guests book a breakfast time on their phone at check-in. The head chef says that guests are on
holiday and will not obey a timetable. The owner likes the idea and wants it in all four hotels
before the summer season, six weeks away. Siobhan has no budget for software this year.

Tasks:

1. State the assumption in the idea that carries the most risk. Plan a test that costs almost
   nothing.
2. Select the measure and the result that would justify an extension to all four hotels.
3. Plan how Siobhan deals with the owner's timetable and the head chef's doubts.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real idea that the learner developed with other people and
  tested at work. A test that gave a negative result is acceptable evidence.
- It includes the problem and how it was found, the test plan written before the test, the
  results, and the recommendation as presented to a person who can decide on it.
- It shows the part that other people played in the idea and the test.
- Data about customers, service users and staff is made anonymous.

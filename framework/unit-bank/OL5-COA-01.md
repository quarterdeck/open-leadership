## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to coach and mentor people at work with a recognised process and clear agreements.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the use of coaching and mentoring at work | 1.1 Analyse the differences between coaching, mentoring, line management and counselling<br>1.2 Assess the benefits and the limits of coaching and mentoring for the person, the team and the organisation<br>1.3 Select coaching, mentoring or a different response to a development need, with reasons |
| 2. Plan coaching and mentoring relationships with clear agreements | 2.1 Apply an agreement that states the aims, the roles, confidentiality, records and how the relationship ends<br>2.2 Assess the interests of third parties, such as the line manager, and agree what each one is told<br>2.3 Justify the ethical limits of their practice, which include when to refer a person to other help |
| 3. Apply a recognised process in coaching and mentoring sessions | 3.1 Apply a staged process through a series of sessions with more than one person<br>3.2 Select methods of questioning, listening, challenge and feedback that fit the person and the stage<br>3.3 Apply mentoring methods that share experience and leave the decision with the person<br>3.4 Assess the progress of each person against the aims in the agreement |
| 4. Evaluate their own coaching and mentoring practice | 4.1 Evaluate their practice with evidence from feedback, records and the results for the people they worked with<br>4.2 Plan their development as a coach and mentor, with reasons |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Coaching as help to think, mentoring as help from a person with more experience
of the same work. Where the two overlap. Counselling and therapy as work for trained
specialists. The conflict of roles when a line manager coaches. Evidence of benefit: confidence,
performance, retention, readiness for promotion. Limits: time, the skill of the coach, a person
who does not want it, a problem that needs a decision from management. Alternatives: training,
a change to the job, a performance procedure.

**Outcome 2.** The parts of an agreement and why each part matters. Two-party and three-party
agreements. What a sponsor can ask and what they cannot. Confidentiality and its exceptions:
risk of harm, serious misconduct, a legal duty. Records and UK data protection law. Power
between a senior mentor and a junior person. Dependence, and how to plan the end from the
start. Signs that a person needs other help, and how to refer them with care.

**Outcome 3.** A staged process in plain words: agree the aim of the session, examine the
present situation, widen the options, test them, agree an action, review it next time. Process
models as an aid that has limits: a rigid order can stop the coach from following the person.
Challenge that names a contradiction or an assumption. Work with feelings as information.
Mentoring methods: stories from experience, introductions to other people, a view of how the
organisation works. The risk that the mentor makes a copy of themselves. Measures of progress
that the person sets.

**Outcome 4.** Sources of evidence about a coach: feedback forms, a conversation at the end,
session notes, recordings made with consent, a peer or a supervisor. Results for the person
compared with the satisfaction of the person. Patterns across people. A development plan that
names skills, practice and support.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Tomasz is an engineering manager at a software company of 200 people. He coaches Leila, a
product designer from a different department, and mentors Joe, a new engineering team lead.
Leila's director agreed to the time for the sessions, and now asks Tomasz if Leila is "ready
for a senior role". Joe copies each of Tomasz's habits, and last week he used Tomasz's exact words to
refuse a request from sales. It went badly. Tomasz made no written agreement with either
person.

Tasks:

1. Write the agreement that Tomasz needs with Leila and her director. Justify what the
   director is told.
2. Analyse what has gone wrong in the mentoring of Joe and select methods to correct it.
3. Plan how Tomasz evaluates his practice with both people.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: portfolio of work evidence, observation at work, professional discussion,
reflective account, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from at least 12 hours of practice with at least three people. At least
  one relationship is coaching and at least one is mentoring. Each relationship has three or
  more sessions.
- It includes the agreement for each relationship, session records and the feedback of each
  person.
- An assessor observes at least one session, or reviews a recording, with the consent of the
  person.
- Records of real people are made anonymous.

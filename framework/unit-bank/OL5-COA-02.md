## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to plan how to make coaching and mentoring a normal part of how an organisation works.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the present place of coaching and mentoring in an organisation | 1.1 Analyse how coaching and mentoring can support the aims of the organisation<br>1.2 Assess how much coaching and mentoring happens now, with evidence from more than one source<br>1.3 Analyse the conditions in the organisation that help coaching and mentoring and the conditions that block them |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** What "a coaching culture" means in practice: managers ask before they tell,
people ask for feedback, development conversations are normal. Links to organisational aims:
retention, succession, service quality, change. Sources of evidence: staff survey, interviews,
records of schemes, the behaviour of senior people. Conditions that help: time, trust, senior
example, skills. Conditions that block: blame, targets that reward only speed, managers who are
promoted for technical skill, shift patterns, remote sites.

**Outcome 2.** Forms: the manager who coaches, a pool of internal coaches, external coaches,
mentoring schemes, reverse mentoring, peer coaching, group coaching. The cost and reach of
each. A start with volunteers compared with a start for everybody. Selection and training of
coaches and mentors. Matching by need, by choice or by a coordinator. Ground rules, a code of
conduct and a route for concerns. Supervision or peer support for coaches. Confidentiality
inside one employer. Equal access for part-time, shift and remote staff. A business case in
plain figures.

**Outcome 3.** Measures of use: numbers, hours, who takes part and who does not. Measures of
quality: feedback, completion, complaints. Measures of effect: promotion, retention, engagement,
performance. The problem of proof when many things change at once. Reports that protect the
content of sessions. The manager's own habits as part of the culture.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Nkechi is the learning and development manager for an NHS community trust with 3,000 staff. Two
years ago the trust trained 40 managers in coaching skills. A survey shows that only six still
coach. Ward and team managers say that they have no time, and that "coaching" is now the word
used before a capability procedure starts. The chief nurse wants a mentoring scheme for newly
qualified staff by April, because one in four leaves in the first year. Nkechi has one
coordinator for two days each week and a small budget.

Tasks:

1. Analyse why the first investment in coaching did not last.
2. Plan a mentoring scheme for newly qualified staff that the trust can operate with these
   resources. Justify your choices.
3. Select the measures that Nkechi reports to the chief nurse after 12 months.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, presentation, professional discussion.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence is a plan for a real organisation, or a real part of one with at least 30
  people, that the learner knows well.
- The assessment of the present position uses evidence from at least two sources, one of which
  is the direct views of staff.
- The plan includes resources, costs, measures and a named person who can decide on it. The
  learner shows that they presented the plan to that person or to a comparable audience.
- Staff views and records are made anonymous.

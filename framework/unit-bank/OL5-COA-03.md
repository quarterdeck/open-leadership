Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to review their own coaching and mentoring practice and improve it.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the purpose and methods of reflective practice | 1.1 Analyse how reflection changes the practice of a coach or mentor<br>1.2 Assess methods of reflection for their strengths and limits<br>1.3 Select methods that fit their own practice, with reasons |
| 2. Apply reflective methods to their own sessions | 2.1 Apply a structured method of reflection to a series of their own sessions<br>2.2 Analyse patterns in their practice, such as habits, assumptions and reactions to particular people<br>2.3 Analyse how their own values, background and position affect their sessions |
| 3. Apply feedback and supervision to their practice | 3.1 Select sources of feedback on their practice that do not depend only on their own view<br>3.2 Apply supervision or peer review to a case that they found difficult<br>3.3 Evaluate the differences between their own view of their practice and the view of other people |
| 4. Plan and evaluate their development as a coach or mentor | 4.1 Assess their practice against a stated set of standards for coaches or mentors<br>4.2 Plan development that deals with the most important gaps<br>4.3 Evaluate the effect on their sessions of one change that they made |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Reflection as a cycle: what happened, what I thought and felt, what it means,
what I will do. Reflection during a session and reflection after it. Description compared with
analysis. Methods: a journal, a review sheet after each session, an audio recording made with
consent, a conversation with a peer, supervision. Limits: memory favours the coach, a journal
can become a diary of events, a peer can be too kind.

**Outcome 2.** Questions for a session review: who did the thinking, where did I lead, what did
I avoid, what did I feel. Patterns: rescue, advice, a rush to action, favourite questions,
discomfort with silence or emotion. Strong reactions to a person as information about the coach.
Values and bias. Differences of age, sex, ethnicity, class and seniority between the coach and
the person. The effect of the coach's position in the same organisation.

**Outcome 3.** Feedback from the people coached, during and after the relationship. Why people
give polite feedback, and how to ask better questions. Observation by a peer. Supervision in
plain words: a regular conversation with an experienced practitioner about the work, for
quality, learning and support. How to prepare a case. How to protect the identity of the person
in supervision. Blind spots.

**Outcome 4.** Standards: a competence list that the organisation or the centre uses, or one
that the learner writes and justifies. Priorities among gaps. Development methods: practice
with a focus, observation of other coaches, reading, training, more supervision. Evidence that
a change in the coach changed the sessions.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Gareth is the operations manager of a hospice charity and one of its four volunteer internal
coaches. His feedback forms are all positive. In a peer review, a colleague listens to a
recording of one of his sessions and says that Gareth spoke for more than half of the time and
suggested three solutions. Gareth is surprised and a little hurt. He also sees that he looks
forward to sessions with one nurse manager and puts off sessions with a fundraiser who cries
easily. The charity has no supervision for its coaches.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the difference between Gareth's feedback forms and the peer review.
2. Select reflective methods that help Gareth to examine his reactions to the two people.
3. Plan Gareth's development for the next six months. Include the support that he needs from
   the charity.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: reflective account, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence is about the learner's own practice: at least six hours of coaching or mentoring
  sessions with at least two people. The hours can be the same hours that give evidence for
  another unit.
- It includes reflective records of at least six sessions, made within two days of each
  session, and a later analysis of the patterns in them.
- It includes feedback from at least two sources other than the learner, and a record of at
  least two supervision or peer review conversations.
- The people coached or mentored cannot be identified from the evidence.

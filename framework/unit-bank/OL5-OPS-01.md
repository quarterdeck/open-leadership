## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to turn objectives into an operational plan and manage its delivery.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the objectives and the demand that an operational plan must meet | 1.1 Analyse how the objectives of the organisation set the priorities of their area<br>1.2 Assess the demand on their area for the period of the plan, and how it varies<br>1.3 Assess the capacity of the people, equipment and suppliers that the area depends on |
| 2. Plan the operations of their area for a period | 2.1 Plan the activities, resources and responsibilities, with milestones<br>2.2 Select measures and targets that show delivery, quality and cost<br>2.3 Justify the priorities of the plan where demand is more than capacity<br>2.4 Plan the response to a disruption that is likely in the period |
| 3. Apply the plan and control its delivery | 3.1 Apply a regular review of results with the people who do the work<br>3.2 Analyse the causes of a gap between the plan and the result<br>3.3 Select a corrective action and justify it against the alternatives |
| 4. Evaluate the delivery of the plan and their own practice | 4.1 Evaluate the results of the period against the objectives, with reasons for the result<br>4.2 Evaluate their own practice as a planner and plan changes to it |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The line from strategy to the annual plan of a function or department.
Objectives that pull in different directions, for example cost and service. Sources of demand
data: orders, forecasts, past seasons, contracts, changes in policy. Regular variation and
sudden variation. Capacity as people, skills, hours, equipment, space and supply. The step in
a process that limits the output of the whole process. The cost of capacity that is not used.

**Outcome 2.** The parts of an operational plan: objectives, activities, owners, resources,
dates, measures, assumptions. Ways to match capacity to demand: overtime, temporary staff,
flexible skills, stock, changes to the times of work, contracts with other suppliers. Measures
that lead and measures that lag. Targets that are possible to meet. Choices when the plan
cannot do everything, and who agrees them. Likely disruptions: absence, breakdown, the failure
of a supplier, a peak in demand. Links to the plans of other departments.

**Outcome 3.** The rhythm of review: daily, weekly, monthly. Visible results that the team can
read. Variation that is normal compared with a signal that needs action. Causes of a gap: the
plan was wrong, the conditions changed, the work was not done as planned. Corrective options
and their side effects. Decisions to escalate. Changes to the plan under control, with a
record.

**Outcome 4.** Results against each objective. The part that the plan played in the result and
the part that events played. Accuracy of forecasts and estimates. The views of the team,
customers and other departments. Habits of the planner: too much hope, too little spare time,
late decisions.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Aleksandra is the production manager at a manufacturer of chilled ready meals with 120 shop
floor staff. The sales director has won a supermarket contract that adds a fifth to volume from
April. The main packing line already runs close to its limit on weekdays. The night shift has
six vacancies, and agency cover costs half as much again. The annual maintenance shutdown is
planned for the first week of April. The managing director's objectives for the year are full
delivery of every order and a 3% cut in unit cost.

Tasks:

1. Assess the gap between demand and capacity from April. State the data that Aleksandra needs.
2. Propose an operational plan for the first quarter of the contract, with measures and targets.
3. State the choices that Aleksandra must take to the directors, with a recommendation.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, project report,
professional discussion, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from an operational plan that the learner made, or made a large part of,
  for a real team, function or service, and from at least three months of its delivery.
- It includes the plan, the data on demand and capacity behind it, and records of the review of
  results.
- It includes one corrective action, with the analysis that led to it and its effect.
- Commercial details and the names of people are removed where the organisation requires it.

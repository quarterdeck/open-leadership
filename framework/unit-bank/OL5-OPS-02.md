Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to plan, lead and close a project with a defined scope, budget and risk.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Justify a project and its scope | 1.1 Justify the project with a statement of its benefits, costs and alternatives<br>1.2 Analyse the needs of the stakeholders and agree a scope that states what is excluded<br>1.3 Select a delivery approach that fits the certainty of the requirements, and justify it |
| 2. Plan the schedule, budget, risks and roles of a project | 2.1 Plan the tasks, dependencies and milestones, and the sequence that sets the end date<br>2.2 Plan the budget and the resources, with an allowance for uncertainty<br>2.3 Assess the risks to the project and plan a response and an owner for each one<br>2.4 Plan the roles, the decision rights and the reports of the project |
| 3. Apply controls that keep a project within its scope, time and budget | 3.1 Apply regular reports of progress, cost and risk to the sponsor<br>3.2 Assess the effect of a requested change on time, cost and benefits before a decision is made<br>3.3 Analyse the causes of a delay or an overspend and select a response |
| 4. Evaluate a project and their leadership of it | 4.1 Plan the close of the project and the handover to the people who operate the result<br>4.2 Evaluate the result of the project against the benefits in its justification<br>4.3 Evaluate their own leadership of the project team and plan changes to their practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The reasons for a project and the option to do nothing. Benefits that can be
measured and benefits that cannot. The sponsor, the users, the suppliers and the people who lose
something. Scope, and the cost of scope that grows without a decision. Two broad approaches:
plan the whole project at the start, or deliver in short stages and learn from each stage. The
conditions that suit each approach.

**Outcome 2.** A breakdown of the work into packages. Estimates, and the reasons that estimates
are usually too low. Dependencies between tasks. The longest chain of dependent tasks, which
sets the earliest end date. Budget lines, contingency and the approval of spend. Risks to time,
cost, quality and people. Responses: avoid, reduce, share, accept. A chart of who does the work,
who decides and who must be told. People who work on the project part-time and report to another
manager.

**Outcome 3.** Progress measured by completed results as well as by time spent. A short, honest
status report. Early warning signs. A change procedure: the request, the assessment of its
effect, the decision, the record. The balance between time, cost, scope and quality. Ways to
recover time, and what each one costs. Suppliers and contracts in general terms. When to
recommend that a project stops.

**Outcome 4.** Acceptance of the result by the users. Documents, training and support after the
project. Release of the team and of the budget that is left. Benefits that appear only after the
close, and who measures them. A review of lessons with the team and the sponsor. Leadership
without line authority. Conflict in a project team and how the learner dealt with it.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Imran is the IT service manager at a university. He must replace the room and timetable system
before teaching starts in late September. The budget is £180,000 and it is now March. Each of
the five academic schools wants the system to keep its own local rules. The supplier's first
delivery was three weeks late. Imran's best analyst works on the project two days a week and has
just been asked to help with student enrolment as well. The sponsor, the registrar, has told the
schools that nothing will change for them.

Tasks:

1. Write a scope statement for the project. Include the items that are excluded.
2. Assess the five largest risks. Propose a response and an owner for each one.
3. Plan what Imran says to the registrar this week, and what he asks her to decide.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence,
professional discussion, presentation.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real project that the learner managed, with a budget or a stated
  resource limit, a sponsor and a team of at least three people.
- It includes the justification, the plan, the risk record, at least three progress reports and
  one assessed change request.
- It includes an evaluation of the result. If the benefits are not yet measurable, the learner
  states how and when they will be measured.
- Names of people and commercial details of suppliers are removed where the organisation
  requires it.

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to collect, analyse and interpret evidence to make and justify a management decision.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan the evidence that a management decision needs | 1.1 Analyse a management problem and state the question that the decision must answer<br>1.2 Select evidence from more than one type of source<br>1.3 Assess the quality and the limits of each source |
| 2. Analyse evidence and interpret the findings | 2.1 Apply suitable methods to summarise numerical data and show its patterns<br>2.2 Analyse the views and experience of the people that the decision affects<br>2.3 Assess if a pattern in the data shows a cause, an association or chance<br>2.4 Apply the law and the organisation's rules to the collection and use of the evidence |
| 3. Justify a decision from the evidence | 3.1 Evaluate the options against the evidence, with their costs, benefits and risks<br>3.2 Justify a recommendation and state how certain the evidence makes it<br>3.3 Select a form of presentation that fits the audience and shows the evidence fairly |
| 4. Evaluate a decision and their own use of evidence | 4.1 Evaluate the result of the decision against the result that was expected<br>4.2 Evaluate how their own assumptions affected the evidence that they collected and the weight that they gave it |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** A decision question that is specific enough to answer. Types of source, for example the
organisation's own data, the views of the people affected, the experience of practitioners and
published research. Evidence that already exists compared with evidence to collect. Samples,
and who is missing from them. Tests of quality: who collected it, how, when, for what purpose,
and what they had to gain. The cost and time of more evidence against the cost of a wrong
decision.

**Outcome 2.** Mean, median and range, and when each one misleads. Trends, seasons and one-off
events. Rates and percentages compared with raw counts. Comparison of groups of different
sizes. Surveys, interviews and group discussions, and how to find themes in the answers. Two
things that move together without one being the cause of the other. A change that would have
occurred without any action. Small samples. UK data protection law in general terms, consent,
anonymity and honesty with the people who give their views.

**Outcome 3.** Options, including the option of no change. Costs and benefits in money and in
other terms. The best case, the expected case and the worst case. Evidence that disagrees, and
what to do with it. A statement of what is known, what is assumed and what is not known. Charts
and tables that show the data without distortion. A one-page summary for a busy reader, with
the detail behind it. A decision that can be tested on a small scale first.

**Outcome 4.** A measure of success set before the decision. A comparison of the forecast with
the result. Common errors of judgement: a search for evidence that agrees with a first view,
too much weight on a vivid story, loyalty to a past decision, the view of the most senior
person. Ways to reduce these errors: a colleague who argues the other side, a written record of
the reasons at the time of the decision.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Niamh manages the outpatient physiotherapy service of an NHS trust. One in seven patients
does not attend a booked appointment, and the waiting list is at fourteen weeks. The clinical
lead wants to book more patients than there are slots. The trust's digital team offers text
reminders, at a cost to Niamh's budget. The booking data shows that missed appointments are
most common on Monday mornings and among patients from two postcode areas with poor bus
services. Several clinicians say that the patients who miss appointments do not value the
service.

Tasks:

1. State the decision question. List the evidence that Niamh has and the evidence that she
   lacks.
2. Assess what the booking data can show about the cause and what it cannot show.
3. Recommend a decision, or a trial, to the divisional manager. State how certain the evidence
   is.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, portfolio of work evidence,
presentation, professional discussion.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real decision in the learner's area of responsibility, which the
  learner made or recommended.
- It uses more than one type of source, and it includes numerical data that the learner analysed.
- It includes the report or presentation that the learner gave to the decision makers.
- Data about people is made anonymous, and the learner states how they met the organisation's
  data protection rules.

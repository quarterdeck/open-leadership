## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to measure quality and lead an improvement to a process or a service.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Assess the quality of a process or a service | 1.1 Analyse what the customers, the users and the regulators of the process or service require<br>1.2 Select measures and standards of quality that reflect those requirements<br>1.3 Assess the present performance against the standards, and its variation over time |
| 2. Analyse a process to find the causes of poor quality | 2.1 Apply a map of the process to show its steps, handovers, delays and repeated work<br>2.2 Analyse data on failures to find the few causes that produce most of them<br>2.3 Assess the cost of poor quality to the organisation and to its customers |
| 3. Plan and apply an improvement | 3.1 Select an improvement and justify it against the alternatives<br>3.2 Plan a trial on a small scale, with a measure of success that is set in advance<br>3.3 Apply the trial with the people who do the work<br>3.4 Evaluate the trial and justify the decision to adopt, adjust or stop the change |
| 4. Evaluate how an improvement is kept and how they lead improvement | 4.1 Plan the standards, training and checks that keep the improvement in place<br>4.2 Evaluate how they involve the team in improvement, and plan changes to their practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Quality as fitness for the purpose of the customer or user. Requirements that
are stated, requirements that are assumed and requirements in law or regulation. Measures of
the result and measures of the process. Standards and tolerances. Prevention of faults compared
with inspection for faults. A simple chart of results over time. Normal variation compared with
a real shift. Complaints as data.

**Outcome 2.** A walk through the process in the place where the work is done. Steps that add
value for the customer and steps that add none. Forms of waste: delay, rework, movement, excess
stock, work that nobody asked for, skills that are not used. Counts of failures by type, and
the common finding that a small number of causes produce most failures. The cost of faults
found inside the organisation, the cost of faults that reach the customer, and the cost of
prevention.

**Outcome 3.** Ideas from the people who do the work. Criteria for selection: effect, cost,
time, risk. The cycle of plan, trial, check and decide. A trial that is small, short and
reversible. A measure before and after. Side effects on other steps and other teams. Honest
reports of a trial that failed. Small, frequent improvements compared with one large redesign.

**Outcome 4.** Written standard methods, kept by the people who use them. Training and
handover. Checks and audits that fade over time. The owner of the process. Reasons that
improvements slip back. A team that reports problems without fear of blame. Time for
improvement in a normal week. The manager's own reaction to bad news.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Dafydd manages a support team of fourteen people at a company that sells payroll software to
small businesses. The team answers 95% of tickets within the four-hour target. However, a
third of tickets are reopened because the first answer did not solve the problem. Most reopened
tickets are about year-end tax forms. The product team says that the help pages are clear.
Senior agents fix hard cases themselves and do not record the fix. Two large customers have
given notice. The operations director wants the response target cut to two hours.

Tasks:

1. Propose measures of quality for the support service. Explain the limit of the present
   target.
2. Describe how Dafydd can find the causes of the reopened tickets.
3. Plan a four-week trial of one improvement, with its measure of success.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence,
presentation, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real process or service that the learner is responsible for, and
  from an improvement that the learner led.
- It includes the process map, the measures before and after the trial, and the analysis of
  causes.
- It includes confirmation, such as a witness statement, that the people who do the work took
  part.
- If the trial did not improve quality, the evidence is still valid. The learner evaluates the
  reasons and the next step.

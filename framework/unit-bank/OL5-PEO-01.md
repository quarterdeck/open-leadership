Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to build a team, develop trust and lead it to sustained results.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse what a team needs to achieve its purpose | 1.1 Analyse the purpose of a team and the results that the organisation needs from it<br>1.2 Assess the mix of skills, experience and working preferences in the team against that purpose<br>1.3 Analyse how the team has developed and the factors that hold it back |
| 2. Plan the structure and the ways of working of a team | 2.1 Plan the roles and the membership of a team, with reasons<br>2.2 Select an approach to bring new members or a new group into the team, and justify it<br>2.3 Agree with the team how it will make decisions, meet and deal with disagreement<br>2.4 Plan how members in different places or on different working patterns will work together |
| 3. Apply leadership practices that develop trust and cooperation | 3.1 Apply practices that build trust between team members and between the team and its leader<br>3.2 Select a leadership approach that fits the situation and the people, and justify it<br>3.3 Analyse a case of low trust or poor cooperation and act on its cause |
| 4. Evaluate the results of the team and their own leadership of it | 4.1 Evaluate the results of the team over time against its objectives<br>4.2 Evaluate their own leadership with the use of feedback from team members<br>4.3 Plan changes that will sustain the results of the team |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The difference between a team and a group of people with the same manager. A
purpose that the members can state. Technical skills, and the skills of work with other people.
Differences in how people prefer to work, and the limits of any tool
that sorts people into types. How teams change over time: early caution, conflict about aims and roles, settled ways of
working. The limits of models with fixed stages. Why a change of members or of task can
move a team back. The effect of team size.

**Outcome 2.** Roles that are clear and that fit together. Gaps and overlaps. The choice between
people who are similar and people who are different. The first weeks of a new member. The merger
of two teams with different habits. Written agreements about how the team works, and their
limits. Who decides, who is consulted, who is told. Useful disagreement. Remote and hybrid work:
contact, visibility, time zones, the risk of an inner and an outer group. Links with other
teams.

**Outcome 3.** Trust as a judgement about ability, honesty and good intent. Behaviour of a
leader that builds trust: promises kept, mistakes admitted, credit shared, information passed
on. A climate in which people can report an error or question a decision without fear. Styles of
leadership from directive to delegating, and the conditions that suit each. Signs of low trust:
blame, silence, work that is checked twice, decisions that are reopened. Causes that lie in the
system as well as in the people.

**Outcome 4.** Results over quarters, compared with a single good month. Measures of output,
quality and the health of the team: turnover, absence, what members say. Results that depend
on one person, and the risk in that. Structured feedback to the leader, and why people soften
it. Dependence on the leader compared with a team that performs when the leader is absent.
Handover and succession.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Rhys manages product engineering at a software company with 90 staff. Four months ago the
company merged its web team in Cardiff with a mobile team that it bought in Leeds. The eleven
engineers now report to Rhys as one team. Releases have slipped twice. The Leeds engineers work
from home and say that decisions are made in the Cardiff office before the meetings start. The
Cardiff engineers say that the code from Leeds needs rework. The two most senior engineers each
expect to be the technical lead. The chief executive wants a joint release in ten weeks.

Tasks:

1. Analyse why the merged team does not yet perform as one team.
2. Propose the roles and the ways of working for the team, including how it makes technical
   decisions.
3. Plan the actions that Rhys takes in the first month to build trust, and state how he will
   know that they work.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
witness statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own leadership of a team of at least three people over
  a period of at least three months.
- It includes the learner's analysis of the team, the ways of working that the team agreed, and
  results of the team from the start and the end of the period.
- It includes feedback on the learner from at least two team members, collected in a way that
  lets them speak freely.
- Records of real people are made anonymous.

# OL5-PEO-03: Leading inclusively

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to create conditions in which different people can contribute fully.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse how far their area of responsibility includes different people | 1.1 Analyse evidence about who joins, who stays, who progresses and who speaks up in their area<br>1.2 Assess how the practices and the unwritten rules of the area help some people and exclude others<br>1.3 Assess the limits of compliance with UK equality law as a measure of inclusion |
| 2. Apply fair methods to decisions about people | 2.1 Apply clear criteria to decisions on selection, allocation of work, development and reward<br>2.2 Assess a decision process for bias and justify a change to it<br>2.3 Plan adjustments and flexible arrangements with the person who needs them |
| 3. Plan and apply action that lets each person contribute | 3.1 Apply practices in meetings and communication that draw contributions from all members<br>3.2 Select a response to behaviour that excludes a person, and justify it<br>3.3 Plan action with the team to remove a barrier that the evidence shows |
| 4. Evaluate the effect of their leadership on inclusion | 4.1 Evaluate the effect of their action with the use of data and the views of the people affected<br>4.2 Evaluate their own assumptions and behaviour, and plan changes to their practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Diversity as a fact about who is present. Inclusion as the experience of the
people who are present. Sources of evidence: workforce data, exit interviews, surveys, who
gets the visible projects, who talks in meetings. Small numbers, and care in what they show. UK
data protection law in general terms, where data about a person is sensitive. Unwritten rules:
hours of presence, social events, how to disagree, who is asked for an opinion. The duties of a
manager under UK equality law, in general terms. Why an area can comply with the law and still
exclude people.

**Outcome 2.** Decisions where bias has most effect: who is hired, who gets the difficult
work, who is rated high, who is promoted. Criteria written before the candidates are known. More
than one assessor. Preference for people who are like the decision-maker. Judgements formed in
the first minutes. The idea of "fit", and its risks. The duty to make reasonable adjustments, in
general terms. Flexible working. The person as the expert on their own needs. Fairness to the
rest of the team.

**Outcome 3.** Meetings: papers in advance, a turn for each person, written input, a chair who
notices interruption. Credit for ideas. Communication that reaches people on other shifts and
sites. Behaviour from the careless remark to harassment, and the response that fits each: a
private word, a challenge at the time, the formal procedure. The cost of silence from the
manager. Action that the team owns, compared with a programme that is done to it. Resistance
and fatigue, and how to answer them.

**Outcome 4.** Measures before and after an action. The time that change takes. Listening to
the people affected, and why they do not always say what they think to a manager. The manager's
own background and the experience that it does not include. A mentor from a different
background. A record of what the manager has changed.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Gurpreet is the production manager at a food manufacturer with 160 operators on three shifts.
Half of the operators are women. All nine shift supervisors are men who were promoted from the
day shift. Supervisor vacancies are filled when a manager recommends a name. The night shift
has the highest turnover, and its operators say that nobody from the offices speaks to them.
Last month an operator who uses a hearing aid asked for the safety briefing in writing. Her
supervisor told her that this was not how the line works. The site director has asked
Gurpreet why good operators leave.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the evidence in the case. State what other evidence Gurpreet needs.
2. Propose a process for the selection of supervisors, and justify each change.
3. Plan how Gurpreet responds to the request for a written briefing and to the supervisor.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, project report,
professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own area of responsibility and covers a period of at
  least three months.
- It includes the learner's analysis of evidence about the area, one decision process that the
  learner changed, and an evaluation of one action with the views of the people affected.
- Data is presented so that no person can be identified. Groups of fewer than five people are
  not reported separately.
- The reflective account for criterion 4.2 names specific assumptions and behaviour of the
  learner.

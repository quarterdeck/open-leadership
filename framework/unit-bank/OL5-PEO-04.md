Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to analyse stakeholders, and influence and communicate with them to reach an objective.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the stakeholders of an objective | 1.1 Analyse the stakeholders by their interest in the objective, their power over it and their attitude to it<br>1.2 Analyse what each main stakeholder gains and loses if the objective is reached<br>1.3 Assess their own sources of influence with each main stakeholder |
| 2. Select and apply approaches to influence stakeholders | 2.1 Select an approach to influence each main stakeholder, and justify it<br>2.2 Apply negotiation to reach an agreement that both sides can keep<br>2.3 Justify the ethical limits that they set on their influence in a case |
| 3. Plan and deliver communication to stakeholders | 3.1 Plan the message, the channel, the timing and the sender for each stakeholder group<br>3.2 Apply evidence, options and a recommendation in a case that they present to decision-makers<br>3.3 Select a response to an objection or to resistance, and justify it |
| 4. Evaluate the result and their own practice | 4.1 Evaluate the result against the objective and the state of the relationships afterwards<br>4.2 Evaluate their own practice as an influencer and plan changes |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Who is a stakeholder: people who decide, people who do the work, people who feel
the effect, people who can block. Inside and outside the organisation. A grid of interest and
power, and its limits. Supporters, opponents and people who have not decided. Stated positions
and the interests behind them. Sources of influence without authority: expertise, information,
relationships, past help, the support of a senior person. Informal networks and who listens to
whom.

**Outcome 2.** Approaches: reasoned argument, consultation, appeal to shared aims, exchange,
coalition, escalation. The match of approach to the person and to the relationship. Influence
upwards, sideways and outside the organisation. Negotiation: preparation, the least that each
side can accept, concessions of low cost and high value, the alternative if no agreement is
reached. Agreements that fail later because one side felt forced. Ethics: honesty about facts
and risks, pressure, selective information, favours. Influence compared with manipulation.

**Outcome 3.** One objective, different messages for different groups, no contradictions between
them. Who should hear first. The sender that each group trusts. A written case: the problem,
the evidence, the options, the cost, the recommendation, the decision that is asked for. A
presentation to senior people who have little time. Conversations before the meeting.
Objections as information. Resistance that comes from loss, from doubt about the evidence, or
from the history of the relationship.

**Outcome 4.** Whether the objective was reached, in part or in full, and at what cost. Support
that is real compared with consent that is only formal. The condition of relationships for the
next piece of work. What the learner did that helped and what harmed. Feedback from
stakeholders. Habits to change: too much reliance on one approach, avoidance of opponents, late
communication.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Orla is the service manager for outpatient booking at an NHS hospital trust. One patient in
eight does not attend, and each missed appointment costs clinic time. Orla wants to replace
letters with text reminders that let patients rebook. The consultants in two specialties
control their own clinic templates and do not want patients to move appointments. The digital
team has a full work plan until April. A local patient group is worried about older patients
who do not use mobile phones. The divisional director supports the idea but will not impose it.
Orla has no budget and no authority over any of these groups.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the stakeholders. State what each one gains and loses.
2. Select an approach to influence the consultants and the digital team, and justify each.
3. Plan the communication with the patient group, and state what Orla is ready to concede.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, presentation, professional
discussion, witness statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real objective at work that needed the agreement of at least three
  stakeholders, of whom at least one was outside the learner's line of authority.
- It includes the stakeholder analysis, the communication plan and the case that the learner
  presented, in writing or in person.
- It includes a witness statement or other evidence from a stakeholder about the learner's
  conduct in the case.
- Names of people and confidential commercial details are removed.

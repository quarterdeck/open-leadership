Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to manage workload and conditions so that people perform well without harm to their health.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the effect of work on health and performance in their area | 1.1 Analyse evidence of the health of their area, which includes absence, turnover, hours worked and errors<br>1.2 Assess the sources of harmful pressure in the design and the conditions of the work<br>1.3 Analyse the relation between the wellbeing of people and the results of the area over time |
| 2. Plan workload and the design of work for sustained performance | 2.1 Plan workload against the capacity of the team, with an allowance for peaks and absence<br>2.2 Apply changes to jobs, schedules or priorities that reduce harmful pressure<br>2.3 Justify to senior managers a decision to limit, delay or stop a piece of work |
| 3. Apply the organisation's procedures to support people whose health is affected | 3.1 Apply the procedures for absence and return to work to a case<br>3.2 Plan adjustments with the person and with specialist advice<br>3.3 Assess the limits of the role of a manager in a case, and select the point of referral |
| 4. Evaluate the effect of their management on wellbeing and results | 4.1 Evaluate the effect of their changes on measures of health and on results<br>4.2 Evaluate how their own working habits and behaviour affect the team, and plan changes |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Indicators: short and long absence, people who work when ill, turnover,
overtime, leave not taken, accidents, errors, complaints, survey results. What each indicator
hides. Pressure that helps performance and pressure that harms it. Sources in the work: the
amount of work, the pace, little control, unclear or conflicting roles, poor relationships,
badly managed change, lone or emotionally hard work, insecurity. The duty of an employer to
assess and reduce risks to health, which include stress, under health and safety law in general
terms. Short-term results bought at the cost of later absence and resignations.

**Outcome 2.** Capacity as hours available after leave, training, meetings and rework. Demand
that varies. Priorities that are stated, so that people know what can wait. Design of jobs:
variety, control, a whole task, feedback from the work. Shift patterns, rest and recovery.
Remote work and the blurred edge of the working day. Messages out of hours. Rules on working
time, in general terms. The case to senior managers: evidence, risk, options, the cost of no
action. Honest agreement on what the team will not do.

**Outcome 3.** Contact during absence. The return to work meeting. A phased return. Reasonable
adjustments under UK equality law, in general terms. Mental and physical ill health, long-term
conditions, the menopause, caring duties, bereavement. Occupational health and other specialist
advice. What a manager can ask, what they record and who sees it, under UK data protection law
in general terms. The effect on colleagues who cover. Cases where support and the management of
performance meet.

**Outcome 4.** Measures before and after a change. Other causes of the same result. The time
lag between a change and its effect. The example that a manager sets: hours, breaks, leave,
messages at night, reaction to bad news. What the team copies. Feedback from the team and from
peers. The manager's own health as a condition of the team's.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Kwame is a contracts manager for a regional building contractor. He is responsible for three
school refurbishment sites that must be complete before the autumn term. His five site managers
have worked six days a week since May. One has been absent with stress for a month, and Kwame
covers that site himself, with calls to the others from 6 am. A near miss with a scaffold last
week involved a site manager at the end of a 13-hour day. The operations director has just
accepted a fourth school with the same deadline and has told Kwame that the team will cope.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the evidence of risk to health and to results in the case.
2. Write the case that Kwame presents to the operations director about the fourth school, with
   options.
3. Plan the return to work of the absent site manager, and state the advice that Kwame needs.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, project report,
professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own area of responsibility and covers a period of at
  least three months.
- It includes the learner's analysis of indicators for the area, a workload plan, and one
  change with measures from before and after it.
- The learner does not include health information that can identify a person. Cases are made
  anonymous.
- For criteria 3.1 and 3.2, if no case occurred in the period, the learner explains with
  reference to the organisation's procedures how they would act in the scenario.

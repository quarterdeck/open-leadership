Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to plan for the people an organisation needs, and attract, select and keep them.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan for the people and skills that a part of the organisation needs | 1.1 Analyse the present workforce of a part of the organisation, which includes its skills, turnover and age profile<br>1.2 Assess the future demand for people and skills against the plans of the organisation<br>1.3 Evaluate the options to close a gap: recruit, develop, move people, use contractors or change the work |
| 2. Plan attraction and selection that find capable people fairly | 2.1 Analyse the labour market for a role and what the organisation offers to a candidate<br>2.2 Select methods of attraction for a role, and justify them by cost and by the candidates that they reach<br>2.3 Select methods of selection, and justify them by how well they predict performance in the job<br>2.4 Apply UK equality law and inclusive practice to the design of the process |
| 3. Plan action to keep and move talent in the organisation | 3.1 Analyse why people stay and why they leave, with data and the views of employees<br>3.2 Plan action on retention that deals with the causes that the analysis found<br>3.3 Plan succession for roles that are hard to fill or costly to lose |
| 4. Evaluate resourcing practice and their own part in it | 4.1 Evaluate a resourcing process with measures of cost, time, quality and fairness<br>4.2 Evaluate their own advice and decisions in resourcing, and plan changes with reasons |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Workforce planning for a team, a site or a function: supply, demand, gap, action.
Data on headcount, vacancies, turnover, absence, skills and expected retirements. Signals of
future demand: growth plans, new technology, new contracts, changes to funding or regulation.
Options and their trade-offs: permanent and temporary employees, apprentices, agency workers,
contractors, internal moves, redesign of jobs, automation. The limits of a forecast, and the use
of scenarios.

**Outcome 2.** Labour markets with few candidates and with many. The offer to candidates: pay, flexibility,
development, purpose, reputation, and whether the offer matches the truth of the job.
Attraction methods: internal advertisement, referral, job boards, social media, agencies,
schools and colleges, direct approach. Evidence on selection methods: structured interviews,
work samples and ability tests predict better than unstructured interviews. Cost, candidate
experience and adverse effect on groups. Positive action, reasonable adjustments, anonymous
shortlists, mixed panels. The role and the training of hiring managers.

**Outcome 3.** Turnover data by role, manager, length of service and reason. Exit interviews
and their limits. Conversations with people who stay. Causes: pay, the manager, workload,
progress, flexibility, the first ninety days. Whether to treat "talent" as a few people or as
everybody. Internal moves, career paths and secondments. Succession: critical roles, people who
are ready now or later, risk when one person holds rare knowledge. Fair access to opportunities.

**Outcome 4.** Measures: cost per hire, time to fill, quality of hire after six months,
retention in the first year, diversity at each stage of the process, the views of candidates and
hiring managers. What each measure misses. Review of own advice: where it was accepted, where it
was ignored, and what the result was.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Nadia is the HR business partner for the northern region of a parcel delivery company, with four
depots and 600 employees. The company has won a contract that adds 20 per cent to volumes from
September. One in three new drivers leaves in the first ninety days. Agency drivers now cover a
fifth of routes at almost twice the cost. Depot managers interview alone, with no set questions,
and say that they "know a driver in five minutes". A third of the experienced drivers are over
fifty-five. The regional director wants a hiring campaign to start next week.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the workforce gap for September. State the data that Nadia needs and does not have.
2. Evaluate the hiring campaign against two other options, and recommend a mix.
3. Propose changes to selection and to the first ninety days, and the measures that will show
   their effect.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, project report,
professional discussion, presentation, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own resourcing work for a real part of an organisation,
  with data from a period of at least six months.
- It includes a workforce analysis with a recommendation, the design of one attraction and
  selection process with reasons for the methods, and a retention or succession plan.
- The evaluation for criterion 4.1 uses real measures. If a measure is not available, the
  learner states how they would collect it.
- Data about candidates and employees is made anonymous. Small groups are combined so that no
  person can be identified.

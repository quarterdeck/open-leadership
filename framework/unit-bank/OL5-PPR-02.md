Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to manage the relationship between an organisation and its employees, which includes discipline, grievance and consultation.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the employment relationship in an organisation | 1.1 Analyse the interests of the employer, the employees and their representatives, and where these interests agree and conflict<br>1.2 Evaluate the ways in which employees in the organisation can give their views and affect decisions<br>1.3 Assess the state of employee relations in a part of the organisation, with evidence from more than one source |
| 2. Manage discipline and grievance cases fairly | 2.1 Apply the organisation's procedure and the principles of a fair process to a case<br>2.2 Plan an investigation that is impartial, prompt and in proportion to the matter<br>2.3 Justify advice to a manager on the outcome of a case, with reference to the evidence and to earlier cases<br>2.4 Select informal resolution where it fits the case, and justify the choice |
| 3. Plan and support consultation on a change that affects employees | 3.1 Assess whether the law or an agreement requires consultation on a proposed change<br>3.2 Plan a consultation: the people, the information, the timing and the use of the views received<br>3.3 Evaluate the effect of a consultation on the decision and on trust |
| 4. Evaluate their own practice in employee relations | 4.1 Evaluate how fair and consistent their advice is across cases and across groups of employees<br>4.2 Plan changes to their practice, with reasons |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The written contract and the unwritten expectations of each side. Shared and
opposed interests: pay, security, workload, flexibility, control of the work. Trust and
fairness: of outcomes, of process and of treatment. Employee voice: team meetings, surveys,
forums, staff networks, recognised trade unions, collective bargaining. Voice that changes
decisions compared with voice that is only heard. Signs of the state of relations: grievances,
absence, turnover, survey results, disputes, the tone of meetings with representatives.

**Outcome 2.** Conduct compared with capability. The elements of a fair process: a clear
allegation, an investigation, the chance to reply, the right to be accompanied, an impartial
decision, an appeal. The separate roles of investigator, decision-maker and adviser. Evidence:
statements, records, balance of probabilities. Suspension as a neutral act with risks.
Consistency with earlier cases, and reasons to differ. Mitigation. Informal resolution: an early
conversation, a facilitated meeting, mediation, and the cases that it does not fit. Records,
confidentiality and time limits. Grievances that allege discrimination, bullying or
whistleblowing need extra care and often specialist advice.

**Outcome 3.** Information, consultation and negotiation are different things. Changes that
commonly need consultation: redundancy, a transfer of the business, changes to terms, changes
to working patterns. Individual and collective consultation. Consultation before the decision
is final. Work with union and employee representatives. What to do with views: accept, adapt,
reject with reasons. The cost of poor consultation: legal claims, disputes, loss of trust.

**Outcome 4.** Pressure from senior managers for a fast or a fixed outcome. The adviser's duty
to the organisation and to a fair process. Patterns in case data: by manager, by site, by
protected characteristic. Sources of feedback: managers, representatives, appeal outcomes.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Harpreet is the HR adviser for the two depots of a regional bus company. The company recognises
a trade union. In March, the operations director issued new rotas at one depot with a week of
notice and no discussion. Since then, grievances at that depot have risen from two to eleven,
and short absences have doubled. A depot supervisor now wants to dismiss a driver who refused a
late shift and swore at him in the yard. The driver says that the supervisor targets union
members. The union representative has asked for a meeting about the rotas.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the state of employee relations at the depot. State the evidence that Harpreet uses.
2. Advise the supervisor on a fair process for the driver's case, and deal with the driver's
   allegation.
3. Plan the meeting with the union representative, and a consultation on the rotas.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
witness statement, reflective account, project report.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own advice or case work on at least two real cases, of
  which one is a discipline case and one is a grievance.
- It includes an investigation plan, a record of advice with reasons, and a consultation plan
  for a real or proposed change.
- Names, job titles, sites and other details that can identify a person are removed or changed.
  The learner has permission from the organisation to use the cases.
- For outcome 3, if no consultation occurred in the period, the learner plans one for the
  scenario and explains how they would evaluate it.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to analyse a learning need and design a learning programme to meet it.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse a learning need and the people who have it | 1.1 Analyse the performance problem or the opportunity behind a request for learning<br>1.2 Analyse evidence from more than one source to define the gap between present and required performance<br>1.3 Assess whether learning is the right response, a part of the response or the wrong response<br>1.4 Analyse the learners: what they know now, how they work and what they need for access |
| 2. Design a learning programme that meets the need | 2.1 Set learning objectives that state what the learner will do at work<br>2.2 Select the content, methods and sequence of the programme, and justify them with reference to how adults learn<br>2.3 Plan activities for practice, feedback and assessment that match the objectives<br>2.4 Apply inclusive and accessible design to the programme and its materials |
| 3. Plan the delivery and the evaluation of the programme | 3.1 Plan the resources, cost and time of the programme, and the part of line managers before and after it<br>3.2 Plan how to evaluate the programme, with measures that are agreed before delivery starts<br>3.3 Justify the design to the sponsor against other options, which include a cheaper one |
| 4. Evaluate a learning design and their own design practice | 4.1 Evaluate the design after a pilot or a first delivery, with evidence from learners and from work<br>4.2 Evaluate their own practice as a designer, and plan changes with reasons |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The request and the problem behind it. Questions for the sponsor: what happens
now, what must happen, how will we know. Evidence: performance data, observation, interviews,
work samples, errors and complaints. Causes that learning cannot fix: process, tools, staffing,
incentives, management. Knowledge, skill and habit need different responses. Learner analysis:
experience, prior learning, motivation, working patterns, location, language, disability,
access to technology.

**Outcome 2.** Objectives as observable actions with a standard. Less content, more practice.
How adults learn: links to what they know already, relevance to real problems, practice spaced
over time, recall, feedback, reflection, limits on how much new material a person can hold at
one time. Methods: workshops, online modules, video, simulation, case work, practice on the job,
coaching, peer groups, job aids. A blend of methods and its sequence. Assessment that matches
the objective: a knowledge check for knowledge, a demonstration for a skill. Accessible
materials: plain language, captions, readable documents, alternatives to one format.

**Outcome 3.** Make, buy or adapt. Costs: design time, delivery, materials, licences, time away
from work. Group sizes and schedules for shift and remote workers. The manager's part: a
briefing before, the chance to practise after, a follow-up conversation. Questions for an
evaluation: did people complete it, can they do the task, do they do it at work, did the
problem get smaller. A baseline before delivery. A design document or a short business case for the
sponsor. The option to do nothing.

**Outcome 4.** A pilot with a small group. Evidence: completion, assessment results,
observation of the session, feedback, performance data after some weeks. Changes to the design
and the reasons for them. Common design errors: too much content, no practice, objectives that
nobody can observe, a design that suits the designer. Feedback from facilitators and sponsors.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Delroy is the learning and development manager of a chain of 45 garden centres. The retail
director asks him for a one-day classroom course on "leadership" for all new store managers, to
run before the spring peak in ten weeks. Stock loss and staff turnover are highest in stores
where the manager is in the first year. Delroy visits three such stores. The new managers know
the products well. They avoid conversations about lateness and till errors, and they do the
rotas themselves at home. Stores cannot release a manager for a full day in March.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the need behind the request. State what learning can change and what it cannot.
2. Design an outline programme with objectives, methods and a sequence that fits the ten weeks.
3. Propose how to evaluate the programme, and the case that Delroy makes to the retail director.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, project report,
professional discussion, presentation, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real learning programme that the learner designed for a need in
  an organisation. The programme has more than one session or method.
- It includes the needs analysis with its sources, the design document with objectives and
  reasons, a sample of the materials and the evaluation plan.
- For criterion 4.1, the programme, or a part of it, has run for real learners at least once.
- Data and feedback about learners are made anonymous.

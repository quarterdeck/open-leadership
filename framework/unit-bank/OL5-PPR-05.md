## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to facilitate learning for groups, in a room and online.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan how to facilitate a session for a specific group and setting | 1.1 Analyse the group: what the members already know, why they attend and what access needs they have<br>1.2 Select facilitation methods that suit the learning outcomes, the group and the setting<br>1.3 Plan the timing, materials, room or platform and a fallback for a session |
| 2. Apply facilitation skills with a group in a room and with a group online | 2.1 Apply methods that make the learners active for most of the session<br>2.2 Apply questions and listening to draw out the knowledge and experience of the group<br>2.3 Apply techniques that give each learner a part in the session, which includes quiet learners and learners with access needs<br>2.4 Select a response to behaviour or conditions that put the learning at risk, and justify it |
| 3. Assess learning during a session and adapt the facilitation | 3.1 Apply checks during a session that show what the learners can now do<br>3.2 Justify a change that they made to a plan during a session<br>3.3 Plan with the group how the learners will use the learning at work |
| 4. Evaluate their own practice as a facilitator | 4.1 Evaluate their facilitation with evidence from learners, an observer and the results of the session<br>4.2 Plan changes to their practice, with reasons |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The difference between presenting and facilitating. Information about the group
before the session: roles, experience, mixed levels, people who were told to attend. Access needs
and reasonable adjustments. Methods: discussion, case work, practice with feedback,
demonstration, small-group tasks, questions to the whole group. The room: layout, sight lines,
noise. The platform: breakout rooms, chat, polls, shared boards, captions. A fallback for a
failure of technology or a session that runs late.

**Outcome 2.** Openings that set purpose and ground rules. Open, probing and redirected questions.
Silence as a tool. How to hold back their own opinion. Group size and pairing. Energy across a
day and across an online session. Differences online: fewer visual signals, cameras off, shorter
attention, the need for clear instructions before a breakout. Behaviour that puts learning at
risk: one person who dominates, side conversations, hostility to the subject, senior people in
the room. Work with a second facilitator or a producer.

**Outcome 3.** Checks for learning: practice tasks, questions that need application, short
quizzes, teach-back, observation of group work. Signs that a group is lost or already knows the
content. When to leave the plan and when to keep to it. Actions for the workplace: a first step,
a date, the support of the line manager.

**Outcome 4.** Sources of evidence: learner feedback, peer observation, a recording, results
of practice tasks, reports from line managers later. The limit of feedback forms that measure
only satisfaction. The facilitator's own habits: talk time, favourite learners, comfort with
conflict.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Tomasz is the learning and development manager for a parcel logistics company. He must run a
one-day session on safe loading for 40 depot supervisors. The design is finished and approved.
Half of the supervisors will be in a training room at the main depot. The other half will join
online from depot offices, some from a shared computer. Several have 20 years of experience
and say that the session is a waste of a shift. The operations director wants to sit in.

Tasks:

1. Plan how Tomasz will facilitate the day so that the online group takes a full part.
2. Select methods that use the experience of the long-serving supervisors.
3. State how Tomasz will check, during the day, that the supervisors can apply the content.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: observation at work, portfolio of work evidence, witness statement,
professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from at least two real sessions that the learner facilitated: one in a room
  and one online. Each session has six or more learners and lasts 60 minutes or longer.
- An assessor or a competent colleague observes at least one session, live or from a recording.
- It includes the learner's plan for each session, the feedback from the learners and the
  learner's evaluation.
- Learners in a recording give their permission. Feedback from real people is made anonymous.

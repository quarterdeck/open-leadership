Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to apply employment law to common situations and know when to get specialist advice.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse how UK employment law shapes the relationship between an organisation and the people who work for it | 1.1 Explain where employment rights and duties come from: legislation, decided cases, the contract, codes of practice and official guidance<br>1.2 Analyse how the status of a person as an employee, a worker or a self-employed contractor affects their rights<br>1.3 Explain how an employment dispute can be settled, from early conciliation to an employment tribunal |
| 2. Apply employment law to common situations during employment | 2.1 Apply the law on contracts to a proposed change to terms and conditions<br>2.2 Apply UK equality law to a decision about a person, which includes the duty to make reasonable adjustments<br>2.3 Apply the law on working time, pay, family leave or flexible working to a request or a query |
| 3. Apply employment law to the end of employment | 3.1 Analyse whether a proposed dismissal has a fair reason and follows a fair procedure<br>3.2 Apply the law on redundancy and consultation to a proposed reduction in posts<br>3.3 Assess the legal and other risks in a case and justify advice to a manager |
| 4. Evaluate whether their advice is current and within the limits of their competence | 4.1 Select current official guidance for a case and state the date on which they checked it<br>4.2 Assess when a case needs specialist legal advice, and justify the decision<br>4.3 Evaluate advice that they gave in a past case and plan changes to their practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess. The law changes.
The centre teaches from current official guidance and does not rely on this content for legal
detail. Employment law is not the same in each nation of the UK, most of all in Northern Ireland.

**Outcome 1.** Sources of law and how they rank. Written and implied terms of a contract. Codes
of practice and what a tribunal does with them. Official sources: government guidance, the
public conciliation service, the equality regulator, the data protection regulator. Employment
status and why the label on a contract does not settle it. Agency workers, casual work and
fixed-term work. Routes to settle a dispute: internal procedure, conciliation, settlement
agreement, tribunal. Time limits exist and are short: the practitioner checks them at once.

**Outcome 2.** The written statement of terms. Change by agreement, change under a term of the
contract, and the risks of change that is imposed. UK equality law: the protected
characteristics, direct and indirect discrimination, harassment, victimisation, reasonable
adjustments, positive action. The law on working time and paid holiday. Minimum pay law. Family
leave and pay. The right to ask for flexible working. Protection for people who raise a concern
in the public interest. The transfer of staff when a business or a service changes hands.

**Outcome 3.** The fair reasons for a dismissal in general terms. A fair procedure and the place
of the code of practice. Dismissals that are unfair whatever the procedure. Redundancy: a real
reduction in the need for work, fair selection, consultation with individuals and, for larger
numbers, with representatives, the search for other work. Notice and final pay. Risk in a case:
legal, financial, reputation, the effect on the team. Advice that states options and risks, and
leaves the decision with the manager.

**Outcome 4.** How to check that guidance is current and applies to the right nation. Records of
advice given and the sources used. Signs that a case needs a lawyer: a possible discrimination
or whistleblowing claim, a collective matter, a settlement agreement, a transfer of staff, a
senior person, a tribunal claim, facts that the guidance does not cover. The cost of late advice.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Gurpreet is the people manager for a group of six hotels. The group will close the restaurant
at one hotel for lunch and wants to change the shifts of 14 kitchen and waiting staff. Some have
contracts with fixed hours. Some have casual arrangements but have worked the same shifts for
years. One chef recently asked to work only mornings because of childcare. The general manager
wants to announce the new rota on Friday and says that anyone who refuses can leave.

Tasks:

1. Identify the areas of employment law that apply. State what Gurpreet must find out first.
2. Draft the advice to the general manager: the options, the risks and a lawful process.
3. State which points Gurpreet will check in current official guidance and which need legal
   advice.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
witness statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from at least three real cases or queries on which the learner gave advice.
  Together, the cases cover outcomes 2 and 3.
- For each case, the learner states the guidance used, its source and the date on which they
  checked it.
- At least one case shows a decision to get, or not to get, specialist legal advice, with reasons.
- If no real case covers a criterion in outcome 3, the learner uses the scenario or a case that
  the centre sets. Records of real people are made anonymous.

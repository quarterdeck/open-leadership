its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to use people data to answer a question that matters to the organisation.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Plan an analysis of people data that answers a question from the organisation | 1.1 Analyse a concern of a manager or a leadership team and state it as a question that data can answer<br>1.2 Select the data sources and measures that the question needs<br>1.3 Assess the quality of the data and state its gaps |
| 2. Apply legal and ethical standards to the use of people data | 2.1 Apply UK data protection law and the policy of the organisation to the analysis<br>2.2 Assess the risk that a result identifies a person, and select a control<br>2.3 Assess how the analysis could lead to unfair treatment of a person or a group |
| 3. Analyse people data | 3.1 Apply rates, averages, spread and trends to describe a workforce<br>3.2 Analyse differences between groups and assess whether small numbers or chance can explain them<br>3.3 Analyse a link between two measures and state what it does and does not show about cause |
| 4. Present findings that lead to action, and evaluate their own practice | 4.1 Select charts and words that make the findings clear to a reader who is not a specialist<br>4.2 Justify recommendations and state the limits of the analysis<br>4.3 Evaluate how the organisation used the findings and plan changes to their own practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Start from the decision, then find the data. Questions about turnover, absence,
recruitment, pay, progression, engagement, learning and productivity. Sources: the people system,
payroll, recruitment system, surveys, exit interviews, learning records, operational and finance
data. Definitions that differ between systems, for example headcount and full-time equivalent.
Quality: missing values, duplicates, old records, changes to codes over time. Data that does not
exist and how to collect it.

**Outcome 2.** A lawful basis and a clear purpose for each use. Use of the minimum data. Special
category data, for example health and ethnicity. Access, storage and retention. When to ask the
data protection lead for an assessment. Small groups: suppression and grouping. The difference
between analysis of groups and decisions about one person. Automated scoring of individuals and
its risks. Bias in the data and bias in the question. What employees are told.

**Outcome 3.** Counts, rates and percentages, and the right denominator. Mean and median. Spread
and outliers. Trends, seasons and rolling periods. Comparisons by team, grade, location, length
of service and protected characteristic. Small numbers and chance. Links between measures: a link
is not proof of cause, and a third factor can explain it. Simple tools: a spreadsheet, pivot
tables, a reporting tool. A check of the work by a second person.

**Outcome 4.** One message for each chart. Chart types and common faults: cut axes, too many
series, colour with no meaning. A short summary for senior readers. Costs put on a finding, with
the assumptions shown. Honest statement of limits. Follow-up: what was decided, what changed,
what the next data shows.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Eilidh is a people partner for a retailer with 45 stores. The retail director says that staff
turnover is "out of control" and wants a retention bonus for all store staff. The people system
shows leavers by store, role, length of service and reason code, but managers pick "other" for a
third of leavers. Six stores changed manager this year. Some stores have fewer than ten staff.
The director wants a recommendation for the board in three weeks.

Tasks:

1. State the question that the data can answer and select the measures.
2. Plan the analysis. State how Eilidh will deal with the poor reason codes and the small stores.
3. Describe how Eilidh will present findings that do not support the bonus.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from one real analysis, with real people data from the learner's
  organisation, that answers a question agreed with a manager or a leadership team.
- It includes the question, the data sources, a record of the checks for quality and for data
  protection, the analysis, and the report or presentation given.
- All data in the evidence is anonymous. No person can be identified from a small group.
- For criterion 4.3, the evidence includes the response of the manager or leadership team.

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to prepare a budget, monitor financial performance and act on variances.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the finances of their area of responsibility | 1.1 Analyse how the budget of their area supports the plans of the organisation<br>1.2 Analyse the income and the costs of their area, and what causes each one to change<br>1.3 Assess the effect of their area on the cash and on the surplus or profit of the organisation |
| 2. Plan a budget | 2.1 Select a method to build the budget and justify it<br>2.2 Plan a budget for a period, with the assumptions stated<br>2.3 Justify the budget, or a proposal to spend within it, to the person who approves it |
| 3. Apply financial control during the budget period | 3.1 Analyse the variances between the budget and the actual figures, and their causes<br>3.2 Apply a forecast to the end of the period and revise it as conditions change<br>3.3 Select a corrective action and assess its effect on service, people and later periods<br>3.4 Apply the organisation's financial rules for approval, purchasing and records |
| 4. Evaluate the financial performance of their area and their own practice | 4.1 Evaluate the value that the area gave for the money that it spent<br>4.2 Evaluate their own practice as a budget holder and plan changes to it |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The budget cycle of the organisation and the manager's part in it. Sources of
income or funding. Direct costs and shared costs. Fixed, variable and stepped costs. The volume
at which income covers cost. Spending on daily operation compared with spending on assets that
last for years. Surplus or profit compared with cash: an organisation can show a profit and
still have no cash. How finance works in the learner's sector: commercial, public or
charitable.

**Outcome 2.** Methods: last year's figure with adjustments, a budget built from zero, a budget
built from the planned volume of activity. The strengths and the risks of each method.
Assumptions about volume, prices, pay and timing. A profile of spend across the months.
Contingency. A simple case for spending: cost, benefit, the time to pay back, the risk. The
negotiation of a budget, and the temptation to inflate it.

**Outcome 3.** The monthly report and how to read it. Variances of price, of volume and of
timing. A variance that will correct itself compared with one that will grow. Commitments that
are not yet in the figures. A forecast of the year-end position. Corrective options: delay,
reduce, stop, find income, move budget between lines. Cuts that cost more later. Approval
limits, the separation of duties, competitive quotations, and conflicts of interest. Signs of
error or fraud, and what to do about them.

**Outcome 4.** Economy, efficiency and effectiveness: the price paid, the output for each pound
and the result for the customer or user. Cost for each unit of output, and its limits as a
measure. Comparison with past periods and with similar areas. Underspend as a problem. The
accuracy of the learner's forecasts. The use of finance colleagues. How well the team
understands the budget.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Morag is the business manager of a secondary school with 900 pupils. Five months into the
financial year, energy costs are 30% over budget and the cost of supply teachers is double the
plan, because two long-term absences were not expected. The Year 7 intake is 25 pupils below
the forecast, which reduces next year's funding. The headteacher wants to refit a computer
suite this year. He says that parents choose schools by their facilities. The governors'
finance committee meets in three weeks and expects a balanced year-end forecast.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the variances. State which ones Morag can control this year.
2. Prepare a year-end forecast with the assumptions stated, and propose corrective action.
3. Assess the proposal for the computer suite. Recommend what the finance committee is asked to
   decide.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
presentation, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real budget that the learner holds or helps to manage, over at
  least three monthly reports.
- It includes a budget or a part of a budget that the learner prepared, with its assumptions,
  and an analysis of variances with a forecast.
- It includes one corrective action or one spending proposal that the learner justified to an
  approver.
- A learner with no access to a real budget cannot meet outcome 3 from the scenario alone.
  Figures can be scaled or disguised if the organisation requires it, and pay details of real
  people are removed.

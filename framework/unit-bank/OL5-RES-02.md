## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to identify, assess and control risks in their area of responsibility.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the risks that their area of responsibility faces | 1.1 Analyse the types of risk in their area and the objectives that each one threatens<br>1.2 Analyse how the organisation's attitude to risk sets the limits of their own decisions<br>1.3 Assess the duties that law and regulation place on them as a manager of risk |
| 2. Assess and rank risks | 2.1 Apply more than one method to find the risks in their area<br>2.2 Assess the likelihood and the effect of each risk against a stated scale, with evidence<br>2.3 Justify the order of priority, with attention to risks that are unlikely and severe |
| 3. Plan and apply controls | 3.1 Select a response to each priority risk and justify its cost against the reduction in risk<br>3.2 Plan controls with owners, dates and early warning signs<br>3.3 Plan how the area continues its essential work during a serious disruption<br>3.4 Apply a regular review of the risk record and escalate the risks that are outside their authority |
| 4. Evaluate the control of risk in their area | 4.1 Evaluate how well the controls work, from incidents, near misses and tests<br>4.2 Evaluate how freely the team reports risk, and plan changes to their own practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Risk as the effect of uncertainty on objectives. Types: safety, operational,
financial, legal, information and cyber, people, supplier, environmental, reputation. Risks
that bring a possible gain as well as a possible loss. The level of risk that the organisation
accepts, and how a manager finds it out. Health and safety law, UK data protection law and
sector regulation in general terms. The duty of the manager and the duty of each employee.
Insurance and its limits.

**Outcome 2.** Methods to find risks: records of incidents and near misses, a walk through the
process, talks with the people who do the work, checklists, lessons from similar organisations,
the question "what could stop this?". A simple scale for likelihood and for effect. The limits
of a score: false precision, and risks that combine. Evidence for a rating compared with a
guess. Rare, severe events. Risks that people stop seeing because nothing has gone wrong yet.

**Outcome 3.** Four responses: avoid the activity, reduce the likelihood or the effect, share
the risk with another party, accept it. Controls that prevent, controls that detect and controls
that limit the damage. Controls that depend on people remembering, compared with controls
built into the process. The cost of a control and the new risks that it creates. Early warning
signs. Continuity: essential activities, the minimum resources, alternative sites and
suppliers, contact lists, a rehearsal. The risk record as a working tool. Routes of escalation.

**Outcome 4.** Tests of controls: checks, drills, audits. What a near miss shows. The
investigation of an incident to find causes in the system, as well as in the person. Blame, and
its effect on reports. Protection for people who raise concerns, in general terms. The
manager's own attitude to risk: too cautious, too bold, or different under pressure.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Hamza is a contracts manager at a regional construction company. He is responsible for a
classroom extension at a primary school that stays open during the work. The contract has a
penalty for each week of delay after August. The roofing subcontractor is cheap and has paid
its own suppliers late. Site staff tell Hamza in private that delivery lorries have twice
arrived at the gate as pupils were leaving. Nobody recorded either event. The site manager
says that reports only cause paperwork. The risk record for the job was last updated at tender.

Tasks:

1. Identify and assess the main risks of the job. State the evidence for each rating.
2. Select a response to the three highest risks. Justify the cost of each one.
3. Plan how Hamza can make near-miss reports normal on this site.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, project report,
professional discussion, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own area of responsibility and covers at least three
  types of risk, of which no more than one is a health and safety risk.
- It includes a risk record that the learner made or revised, with ratings, responses, owners
  and at least one review after a period of two months or more.
- It includes a continuity plan for one essential activity and an evaluation of one control
  after an incident, a near miss or a test.
- Incident records are made anonymous. The unit does not replace the specialist training that
  health and safety law requires.

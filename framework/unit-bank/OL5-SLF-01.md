Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to evaluate their own strengths, limits and effect on others, and plan their growth as a leader.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse their own strengths, limits and preferences as a leader | 1.1 Analyse evidence about their strengths and limits from at least three sources<br>1.2 Assess how their values and preferences shape their decisions and their style of leadership<br>1.3 Evaluate the usefulness and the limits of one self-assessment method that they used |
| 2. Evaluate the effect of their behaviour on other people | 2.1 Analyse feedback to find the differences between the effect they intend and the effect they have<br>2.2 Assess how their behaviour changes under pressure and what the change does to the team<br>2.3 Apply a change in their behaviour in response to feedback and evaluate the result |
| 3. Plan and manage their growth as a leader | 3.1 Select development priorities that fit their role, their aims and the needs of the organisation<br>3.2 Plan development activities, with measures of success and dates for review<br>3.3 Evaluate their progress against the plan and justify changes to it |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Sources of evidence about the self: results, feedback from several directions,
questionnaires, a reflective journal, the pattern of past decisions. Strengths that become
limits when they are used too much. Values, and where they come from. Preferences in how a person works. What a questionnaire can show and
what it cannot: it reports the person's own view, the result can change with mood and context,
and a label can become an excuse. Blind spots, and why other people see them first.

**Outcome 2.** Intention and effect. How status changes the way that people hear a manager.
Reasons why a team does not give honest feedback, and ways to make it safer to do so. Habits
under pressure: control, withdrawal, speed, avoidance of conflict. Emotions at work: how to
notice them, name them and choose a response. Small experiments with behaviour, and how to
judge the result from the reactions of other people.

**Outcome 3.** Short-term needs of the present role and long-term aims. The needs of the
organisation as well as the wishes of the person. Development from stretching work, from other
people and from formal learning. Mentors, coaches and peer groups. Reflective practice as a
routine: describe, analyse, decide, act. Measures of progress that other people can observe.
Reasons to change a plan.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Meera manages two engineering teams, 14 people in total, at a software company. Feedback from
her teams and peers says that she is clear, quick and reliable. It also says that she takes
over each hard problem, and that people have stopped bringing ideas to her. Meera is surprised.
She thought that she was helping. A senior developer has resigned and told a colleague that
there was no room to grow. Meera's director wants her to take on a third team in three months.
Meera already works most evenings.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the difference between Meera's intention and her effect. State what other evidence
   she needs.
2. Propose one change in behaviour for Meera to test, and how she can judge the result.
3. Select three development priorities for Meera before she takes the third team. Give reasons.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: reflective account, portfolio of work evidence, professional discussion,
work-based assignment, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own role over a period of at least three months.
- It includes feedback from at least three sources, of which one or more is a person that the
  learner manages or leads.
- It includes a development plan, a record of the change in behaviour that the learner applied
  for criterion 2.3, and a review of progress.
- People who gave feedback are not named. The learner states how the feedback was collected.

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to apply ethical principles to decisions at work and act with professional integrity.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the ethical content of decisions at work | 1.1 Analyse a decision at work to identify the people affected and the duties that conflict<br>1.2 Apply two or more ethical approaches to the decision and assess where their conclusions differ<br>1.3 Assess how the law, the organisation's policies and professional standards apply to the decision |
| 2. Apply professional judgement to a dilemma that the rules do not settle | 2.1 Select a course of action and justify it with principles, evidence and likely consequences<br>2.2 Assess the pressures and biases that can distort their judgement<br>2.3 Justify the choice to decide alone, to seek advice or to escalate |
| 3. Apply the practices that protect integrity and trust | 3.1 Apply the organisation's rules on conflicts of interest, gifts and confidential information<br>3.2 Analyse the routes to raise a concern and the protection for a person who raises one<br>3.3 Assess how their own conduct affects the ethical standards of their team |
| 4. Evaluate their own ethical practice | 4.1 Evaluate a past decision of their own against their values and professional standards<br>4.2 Plan changes to their practice, with reasons |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** What makes a decision an ethical one: harm, fairness, honesty, rights, trust.
Three common approaches: judge by consequences, judge by duties and rules, judge by the
character that the act shows. Why the approaches can disagree. The difference between legal,
permitted by policy and right. Areas of law that often apply: UK equality law, UK data
protection law, employment law, health and safety law, law on bribery. Professional standards
and codes of conduct as a public promise.

**Outcome 2.** Dilemmas in which two good things conflict: loyalty and honesty, the person and
the group, the short term and the long term. A structured way to decide: facts, people
affected, options, tests, decision, record. Simple tests: would I explain this in public, would
I accept it if I were on the other side. Pressures: targets, authority, loyalty to a group,
time, self-interest. Small steps that make a wrong act seem normal. When to ask for advice, and
from whom.

**Outcome 3.** Conflicts of interest: real, possible and perceived. Declaration and withdrawal
from a decision. Gifts and hospitality. Confidentiality and its limits, which include a risk of
harm. Personal data and the need to know. Routes to raise a concern inside and outside the
organisation, and the legal protection for workers who report wrongdoing. The manager as an
example: what the manager walks past, rewards and jokes about.

**Outcome 4.** Stated values and the values that a person's actions show. Reasons that people
give themselves after a poor decision. Records of decisions as a basis for review. Feedback
from a trusted colleague. Habits that support good judgement: time to think, a second opinion,
a written reason.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Nia manages recruitment for a hotel group with twelve sites. A general manager needs a head
chef soon. He has a preferred candidate, who is the partner of his deputy. He asks Nia to skip
the panel interview and not to see the two other applicants. One of them told Nia in confidence
about a health condition. The general manager has heard a rumour and asks her what "the issue"
is with that applicant. The group's policy requires a panel, but senior managers often ignore
it. Part of Nia's bonus depends on how fast she fills vacancies.

Tasks:

1. Identify the duties that conflict in this case and the people affected.
2. Decide what Nia does about the request to skip the panel and the question about the
   applicant. Justify each decision.
3. State what Nia declares, records or escalates, and to whom.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, reflective account, professional discussion, portfolio
of work evidence.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence uses at least two real decisions or dilemmas from the learner's own work. One of
  them is a decision that the learner made.
- It shows the reasoning of the learner, with the approaches applied, the options that the
  learner rejected and the reasons.
- People and organisations in the cases are made anonymous. The learner does not disclose
  confidential information in the evidence.
- For criterion 3.2, the learner refers to the procedure of their own organisation. If it has
  none, the learner states what a suitable procedure contains.

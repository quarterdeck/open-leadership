Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to analyse how the structure, culture and external context of an organisation affect its performance.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse how the structure of an organisation affects its performance | 1.1 Analyse how the structure divides work, authority and information<br>1.2 Assess the strengths and weaknesses of the structure for the present aims of the organisation<br>1.3 Evaluate how the formal structure and informal networks together affect one decision or process |
| 2. Analyse the culture of an organisation and its effects | 2.1 Analyse the culture of an organisation, or of a part of it, with evidence from what people do, say and reward<br>2.2 Assess the differences between the stated values and daily behaviour<br>2.3 Evaluate the effect of the culture on performance, conduct and the experience of staff |
| 3. Analyse the external context of an organisation | 3.1 Analyse the political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental factors that affect the organisation<br>3.2 Assess the expectations and the power of external stakeholders<br>3.3 Evaluate how well the organisation responded to one change in its context |
| 4. Apply the analysis to their own area of responsibility | 4.1 Justify changes in their own area that improve the fit between structure, culture and context<br>4.2 Evaluate how their own behaviour as a manager maintains or changes the culture |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Ways to group work: by function, product, place, customer or project, and
mixtures of these. Tall and flat structures. Central control and local freedom. Spans of
control. What each choice makes easy and what it makes hard: speed, cost, expertise,
co-ordination, accountability. Gaps and overlaps between units. Informal networks: who people
really ask, who can block a decision. Reasons why the same structure works in one organisation
and fails in another.

**Outcome 2.** Culture as shared assumptions about how things are done. Visible signs: stories,
routines, language, layout, who gets promoted, what gets measured, what is tolerated. Subcultures
in sites, professions and shifts. Limits of the idea: culture is hard to measure, people
describe it differently, and leaders see a kinder version. Methods to collect evidence:
observation, surveys, interviews, records of complaints and concerns. Culture and safety,
honesty, inclusion and retention. Culture after a merger or fast growth.

**Outcome 3.** A scan of the environment under six headings, and the weakness of a scan that is
only a list. Sector, market and competitors, or the equivalent for a public service or a
charity. External stakeholders: customers, funders, regulators, suppliers, partners,
communities. Expectations, power and interest. Areas of law that shape most organisations.
Responses to a change: ignore, comply, adapt, lead. Evidence of a good response: speed, cost,
effect on customers and staff.

**Outcome 4.** Fit: a structure and a culture that suit the context and the aims. What a middle
manager can change alone, what needs agreement and what to propose upwards. Small changes with
large effects: who attends a meeting, who holds a decision, what is praised. The manager as a
carrier of culture. Consistency between words and actions.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Pawel is a contracts manager at a regional construction company. A year ago the company
bought a smaller family firm, and Pawel now manages site managers from both. The larger
company runs each decision through head office. The site managers from the family firm are used
to deciding on site, and their jobs finish faster. They also report far fewer near misses, and
Pawel doubts that fewer happen. Clients now ask for more safety and carbon records with each
bid. Head office plans one new reporting system for all sites. Two experienced site managers
have said that they will leave if "the paperwork people" win.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the two cultures. State the evidence that Pawel needs about near misses.
2. Assess how the change in client demands affects the argument between head office and the
   sites.
3. Justify two changes that Pawel can make in his own area, and one that he proposes to his
   director.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence is an analysis of the learner's own organisation, or of a part large enough to
  have its own structure and culture.
- The analysis of culture uses evidence from two or more methods. The learner's opinion alone is
  not enough.
- It includes one external change from the last three years and the response of the
  organisation to it.
- People are not named. Information that is commercially sensitive is left out or made general,
  with the agreement of the employer.

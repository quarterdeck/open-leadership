## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to contribute analysis and proposals to the strategy of an organisation and align a team with it.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Analyse the strategy of the organisation and the place of their area in it | 1.1 Analyse the aims, choices and assumptions in the strategy of the organisation<br>1.2 Assess how their area creates value for customers and for the organisation, and at what cost<br>1.3 Assess the strengths and weaknesses of their area against the strategy |
| 2. Analyse evidence and options for a strategic proposal | 2.1 Select evidence from customers, operations, staff and the outside environment<br>2.2 Analyse the opportunities and threats that the evidence shows for their area<br>2.3 Evaluate two or more options against stated criteria, which include cost, risk and capability |
| 3. Justify a proposal to the people who decide strategy | 3.1 Justify a proposal with evidence, costs, risks and measures of success<br>3.2 Apply knowledge of the planning process and of the priorities of decision makers to the timing and form of the proposal |
| 4. Plan the work of a team to carry out the strategy | 4.1 Plan objectives and priorities for the team that follow from the strategy<br>4.2 Justify the work that the team stops or reduces to make room for the priorities<br>4.3 Evaluate their own contribution to strategy and plan how to improve it |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Strategy as a set of choices: which customers, which products or services, how
to win or how to serve, what to leave out. The difference between a strategy, a plan and a
target. Assumptions behind a strategy, stated and unstated. How an area adds value: income,
cost, quality, reputation, capability. An honest internal review: skills, systems, capacity,
relationships. The view from the middle: what senior people do not see.

**Outcome 2.** Evidence that a middle manager holds: customer comments, complaints, lost sales,
operational data, staff ideas, what suppliers and competitors do. Simple tools: strengths,
weaknesses, opportunities and threats; a scan of the environment; a comparison with other
organisations. The limits of each tool. Options that are really different. Criteria: fit with
the strategy, cost, return, risk, capability, time, effect on people. The option to do nothing.

**Outcome 3.** The planning cycle of the organisation: when plans and budgets are fixed, and
who is consulted. Formal and informal routes for a proposal. A short business case: the
problem, the options, the recommendation, the cost, the risks, the measures. What decision
makers worry about. A pilot as a way to reduce risk. How to respond when a proposal is
refused.

**Outcome 4.** From strategy to team objectives. Priorities, and the hard part: what to stop.
How to explain the strategy so that each team member can connect it to their work. Local
measures that match the strategy. Conflicts between local targets and the strategy. Feedback
to senior managers about what works and what does not. Review of the learner's own influence
on strategy.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Mei is area manager for eleven shops of a garden centre chain. The company strategy is to grow
online sales and offer collection from shops. Online orders count towards a central target, not
the target of the shop, so Mei's shop managers treat collections as unpaid work and give them
little space. Mei's own figures show that customers who collect an order spend more in the shop
café and on plants. Two of her shops lose money. Her director has asked each area manager for
one page of proposals before the board's planning day in six weeks.

Tasks:

1. Analyse the assumptions in the company strategy that Mei's evidence supports or questions.
2. Evaluate two options that Mei could propose. Recommend one, with costs, risks and measures.
3. Plan how Mei aligns her shop managers with the strategy before any target changes.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, presentation, professional discussion,
portfolio of work evidence.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence includes a real proposal that the learner made, or prepared for submission, to
  the people who decide strategy or plans in their organisation.
- The proposal uses evidence from three or more sources and compares two or more options.
- It includes the team objectives or priorities that the learner set, and the work that the
  team stopped or reduced.
- Information that is commercially sensitive is left out or made general, with the agreement of
  the employer. Customers and colleagues are not named.

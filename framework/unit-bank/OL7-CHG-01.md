# OL7-CHG-01: Leading strategic change and innovation

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to lead change and innovation across an organisation.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate the need and the capacity for strategic change in an organisation | 1.1 Critically evaluate the external and internal pressures that make strategic change necessary<br>1.2 Appraise the capacity of the organisation for change, which includes its culture, its skills, its present load and its record<br>1.3 Critically evaluate theory and research on why large changes succeed or fail, and form an independent view |
| 2. Formulate a strategy for change across an organisation | 2.1 Formulate a case and a direction for change that the board and the workforce can test<br>2.2 Design a programme of change with its sequence, governance, resources and measures of benefit<br>2.3 Appraise the short-term and long-term effects of the change on people, money and reputation<br>2.4 Justify the pace and scope of the change, and the work that will stop to make room for it |
| 3. Design the conditions for innovation in an organisation | 3.1 Design the structures, funds and decision rules that let the organisation explore new ideas while it runs present operations<br>3.2 Appraise the set of innovation projects against the strategy and the appetite for risk<br>3.3 Justify how the organisation ends failed projects and uses what it learned from them |
| 4. Lead strategic change and critically evaluate their own leadership of it | 4.1 Lead senior colleagues and other stakeholders through a strategic change<br>4.2 Synthesise evidence of progress from different sources and change the programme in response<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their own leadership of change and formulate changes to their practice |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Pressures: markets, funding, regulation, technology, demography, climate, public
trust. Slow decline compared with crisis as a start for change. Capacity: leadership time,
skills, money, systems, trust between levels, the number of changes already in progress. The
record of past programmes and what staff learned from them. Debates in the research: planned
change compared with change that emerges, change led from the top compared with change spread
by networks, the weak evidence for common claims about failure rates. Culture as a result of
change as much as a tool for it.

**Outcome 2.** A case that is honest about losses. A direction that is clear enough to guide
decisions without a full plan. Portfolio, programme and project. Sequence: what must come
first, what can run together, where to prove the idea. Governance: sponsor, programme board,
decision rights, reports to the board. Benefits that are defined, owned and measured after the
programme ends. Effects on jobs, and the duties to consult under UK employment law in general
terms. Effects on customers, regulators, funders and the public. Pace: the risk of delay
compared with the risk of overload. Decisions to stop work.

**Outcome 3.** The tension between efficient operations now and exploration for later. Options:
a separate unit, protected time, partnerships, acquisition, open calls for ideas. Funds released
in stages against evidence. Who can say yes, who can say no, and how fast. A balanced set of
projects: improvements to the present, extensions, new ventures. Appetite for risk, set by the
board. Rules for ending a project without blame. How learning from a project reaches the rest
of the organisation. Ethics and public acceptability of new technology, which includes
automation and artificial intelligence.

**Outcome 4.** The coalition of senior people, and what to do when it splits. Consistency
between the message and the behaviour of the top team. Leaders of leaders: how middle managers
receive, translate and sometimes block a change. Work with trade unions, regulators, investors
or elected members. Evidence of progress: measures, visits, staff voice, external review. The
courage to change or stop a programme. Personal resilience, and the leader's own part in what
went wrong.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Rajinder is the deputy chief executive of a multi-academy trust with 14 schools and 1,100 staff
in the north of England. Pupil numbers are forecast to fall by 12 per cent in five years, and
three primary schools already have a deficit. The trustees want one finance, people and estates
service for all schools within 18 months. They also want a decision on the use of artificial
intelligence tools for lesson planning and marking. Head teachers defend the independence of
their schools. Two of them have told parents that support staff jobs will go. A new curriculum
programme started in September and is behind schedule.

Tasks:

1. Appraise the capacity of the trust for these changes. Justify what Rajinder proposes to
   stop, delay or combine.
2. Design the governance of the shared service programme. Include the part of head teachers.
3. Formulate how the trust explores artificial intelligence tools safely. State the decision
   rules and how a failed trial ends.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account, portfolio of work evidence.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real strategic change that crosses functions or sites, in which the
  learner has a leading part. The change does not need to be complete.
- It includes a strategy for the change with its case, programme design, governance and
  appraisal of effects, as presented to a board, an executive team or a comparable group.
- It includes a design for how the organisation supports innovation, with reference to
  published research and to evidence from the organisation.
- It includes evidence of the learner's leadership from at least two senior colleagues or
  stakeholders, and a critical evaluation of that leadership by the learner. Commercially
  sensitive information and the identities of people are removed.

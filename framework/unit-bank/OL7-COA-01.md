Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to coach and mentor senior people, with clear contracts and boundaries.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate approaches to the coaching and mentoring of senior people | 1.1 Critically evaluate the evidence for the main approaches to coaching and mentoring<br>1.2 Appraise how power, isolation and public accountability change the work with senior people<br>1.3 Formulate their own approach to practice and justify it against the evidence |
| 2. Design contracts and boundaries for work with senior people | 2.1 Design a contract between the coach, the senior person and the organisation that states aims, confidentiality, reporting and the end of the work<br>2.2 Formulate boundaries for conflicts of interest, sensitive information and the limits of their competence<br>2.3 Justify their response when a contract or a boundary comes under pressure |
| 3. Lead coaching and mentoring work with senior people | 3.1 Lead a series of sessions that connects the aims of the person to the needs of the organisation<br>3.2 Synthesise information from different sources, such as feedback from colleagues and business results, into the work<br>3.3 Lead sessions that test the thinking of a senior person on a complex decision with incomplete information<br>3.4 Appraise progress with the person and with the organisation, within the terms of the contract |
| 4. Critically evaluate their own practice with senior people | 4.1 Critically evaluate the effect of their practice on the people they work with and on the organisation<br>4.2 Appraise the limits of their competence and their use of supervision<br>4.3 Formulate a plan for their development as a coach and mentor of senior people |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Families of approach in plain words: work on goals and actions, work on
solutions and strengths, work on thoughts and beliefs, work on the person's history and
patterns, work on the system around the person. The quality of the research on each: small
studies, self-report, results measured by the coach. The finding that the relationship matters
as much as the method. The senior role: few honest sources of feedback, decisions with no
correct answer, public and board scrutiny, the cost of doubt shown in public. Mentoring by a
person who has held a similar office. An approach of their own that is coherent, has limits and
can change.

**Outcome 2.** The parties: the person, the sponsor, the board or chair, the people function,
the coach. Who is the client. Meetings of three parties at the start, the middle and the end.
What is reported and by whom. Fees or time for an internal coach. Sensitive information: plans
for restructure, price-sensitive facts, the conduct of other people. Conflicts: work with two
rivals, work with a person and their manager, other paid work for the same organisation. The
line between coaching and therapy, and between coaching and consultancy. Pressure on a
contract: a request for an opinion on a person, a wish to continue with no end, a disclosure of
wrongdoing.

**Outcome 3.** Aims that serve both the person and the organisation, and what to do when they
diverge. Sources of information: feedback from colleagues, a profile, observation of meetings,
results, the person's own account. Agreement about who sees each source. Questions that open up
a strategic decision: assumptions, second-order effects, the views that are absent from the
room. Challenge to a person with more status than the coach. Work on transitions: a first
executive role, a new board, a crisis, an exit. Reviews of progress that keep the content of
sessions private.

**Outcome 4.** Evidence of effect: change in behaviour seen by other people, decisions made,
results, the view of the sponsor. The difficulty of attribution. Flattery, dependence and
collusion as risks of work with powerful people. Supervision as a place to test judgement.
Fitness to practise: load, wellbeing, currency of knowledge.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Priya is the group people director of a construction company with 1,400 staff. She is also a
trained coach. The chief executive asks her to coach Martin, who became managing director of
the civil engineering division three months ago. Two of Martin's directors have complained that
he overrules them in front of clients. The chief executive wants "a quiet word on progress each
month". Priya sits on the committee that decides executive pay. In the second session Martin
says that he doubts the figures in a large bid that the board approves next week.

Tasks:

1. Appraise whether Priya is the right coach for Martin. Justify your view.
2. Design the contract that this work needs, if it continues. State what the chief executive
   is told.
3. Formulate Priya's response to what Martin said about the bid.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: portfolio of work evidence, observation at work, professional discussion,
reflective account, project report.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from at least 20 hours of coaching or mentoring with at least three people
  in senior roles. Each relationship has four or more sessions.
- It includes the contract for each relationship, a case study of one relationship from start
  to end, and feedback from each person and from one or more sponsors.
- An assessor reviews a recording or observes a session, with the consent of the person.
  People and organisations cannot be identified from the evidence.
- A written statement of the learner's approach to practice refers to published research and
  to the learner's own evidence.

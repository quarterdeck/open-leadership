Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to use supervision and an ethical framework to maintain the quality of coaching practice.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate the purposes and forms of coaching supervision | 1.1 Critically evaluate the evidence that supervision improves the quality and safety of coaching<br>1.2 Appraise one-to-one, group and peer supervision, and internal and external supervisors<br>1.3 Justify the supervision arrangement for their own practice |
| 2. Formulate an ethical framework for coaching practice | 2.1 Synthesise principles from published codes of ethics, the law and organisational policy into a framework for their own practice<br>2.2 Appraise ethical dilemmas in which duties to the person, the sponsor and other people conflict<br>2.3 Justify a decision on an ethical dilemma from practice, with each step of the reasoning shown<br>2.4 Appraise how the framework deals with differences of power, culture and identity between the coach and the person |
| 3. | 4. Critically evaluate their own use of supervision and ethics | 4.1 Synthesise what they learned in supervision into changes in their practice<br>4.2 Critically evaluate their own decisions in ethically difficult cases<br>4.3 Formulate a plan to maintain their ability to practise safely |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Three purposes of supervision: quality of the work, development of the coach,
support for the coach. The thin research base, and what practitioners report. Supervision
compared with line management, with mentoring of the coach and with therapy. Forms and their
trade-offs: depth, cost, variety of view, safety to speak. The independence of the supervisor.
Frequency in proportion to the amount of practice. What a coach brings: a case, a pattern, a
doubt, a reaction.

**Outcome 2.** Common principles in codes of ethics: benefit to the person, avoidance of harm,
autonomy, honesty, fairness, confidentiality, competence. Law in general terms: UK data
protection law, UK equality law, duties to report certain crimes and risks of harm, the terms
of a contract. Policy: whistleblowing, safeguarding, conflicts of interest. Dilemmas: a
disclosure of wrongdoing, a risk to the person's health, a sponsor who wants information, two
roles with one person, an aim that harms other people. A method for a decision: facts, parties,
duties, options, consultation, decision, record. Power and difference in the coaching room.

**Outcome 3.** A coaching service inside an organisation and a group of external coaches.
Selection and continued approval of coaches. Minimum supervision. Agreements, records and
storage. Feedback and complaints, and a route that people trust. What a quality lead can see
and what stays private. Action on a concern: a conversation, more supervision, a pause in
practice, removal. The reputation of coaching in the organisation as an asset that one case
can damage.

**Outcome 4.** Preparation for supervision and honest choice of cases. The cases that a coach
avoids bringing. Evidence that supervision changed practice. A review of past decisions with
later knowledge. Fitness to practise: load, wellbeing, continued learning, insurance where it
applies, an ending plan for clients if the coach must stop.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Declan is the director of organisational development at a national parcel carrier. He leads a
pool of 14 internal coaches and coaches two senior managers himself. The coaches meet as a
peer group twice a year, and nobody checks their practice. One coach, Ffion, tells Declan
that a depot manager she coaches has altered driver hours records to meet a target. The depot
manager spoke in confidence. Ffion's agreement with him says "everything stays private". The
operations director, who sponsors the coaching pool, is that depot manager's line manager and
Declan's peer.

Tasks:

1. Appraise the duties that conflict in this case. Justify what Ffion and Declan each do
   next, step by step.
2. Design supervision and quality arrangements for the coaching pool that would reduce the risk
   of a similar case.
3. Formulate the wording about confidentiality that each future agreement contains.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: portfolio of work evidence, project report, professional discussion,
reflective account, presentation.

Evidence requirements:

- The learner receives at least six hours of supervision of their own coaching practice during
  the unit, from a supervisor who does not manage them. A record of each session is included,
  with the changes to practice that followed.
- A written ethical framework for the learner's practice is included, with its sources and its
  application to at least two dilemmas from real practice.
- A design for supervision and quality arrangements is included, for a real group of at least
  five coaches or for the group in the scenario.
- People and organisations cannot be identified from the evidence.

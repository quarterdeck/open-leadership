Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to evaluate how an organisation creates value and redesign how it operates.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate how an organisation creates value | 1.1 Critically evaluate how the activities of the organisation create value for its customers, owners and other stakeholders, and where value is lost<br>1.2 Appraise the fit between the strategy and the present operating model<br>1.3 Synthesise internal and external evidence to compare the performance of the organisation with that of comparable organisations |
| 2. Design the way in which an organisation measures and governs its performance | 2.1 Design a set of financial and non-financial measures that shows present results and the capability to produce future results<br>2.2 Critically evaluate the behaviour that the present measures and targets cause<br>2.3 Formulate the arrangements by which senior leaders review performance and act on it |
| 3. Design a change to an operating model | 3.1 Formulate options for the operating model, with the assumptions behind each one<br>3.2 Appraise the options for their effect on cost, service, risk, people, society and the environment<br>3.3 Justify the selected design to the board or the equivalent body<br>3.4 Design the transition, which includes how service continues while the model changes |
| 4. Lead a change to an operating model and appraise its results | 4.1 Lead senior colleagues to agree and start the change<br>4.2 Appraise the early results against the case for the change, and adjust the design<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their own judgement and conduct as the leader of the change |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Value as different stakeholders define it: customers, owners, funders, staff,
the public. The chain of activities from supply to the customer, and the support activities
around it. The parts of an operating model: processes, structure, decision rights, technology,
data, people and skills, locations, suppliers and partners. Signs of poor fit: a strategy that
promises one thing and an operation that is built for another. Comparison with other
organisations, and the limits of comparison. Debates about efficiency against resilience.

**Outcome 2.** Financial and non-financial measures. Measures of results and measures of the
capability to produce future results. The line of sight from board measures to team measures.
Targets that distort: effort moves to the measured work, figures are managed, and risk is
hidden. Short-term results against long-term health. The rhythm, membership and conduct of
performance reviews at senior level. Reports to a board, to owners, to a regulator or to the
public. The quality of the data behind the reports.

**Outcome 3.** Design choices: do the work inside, buy it or share it with a partner. Central
control compared with local freedom. Standard service compared with service that is adapted to
each customer. Automation and the use of artificial intelligence, with the effect on jobs and
on accountability. The whole-life cost of a model, and the costs that move to other parties.
Dependence on suppliers. UK employment law and the duty to consult, in general terms.
Environmental effect and social value. Phased transition, parallel operation and the point of
no return.

**Outcome 4.** A coalition of senior leaders with different interests. Decisions with
incomplete information. Honest communication with staff whose jobs change. Early measures of
whether the design works. The readiness to change a design that the leader argued for. The
long-term effects on reputation and trust. The leader's own stake in the result.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Yetunde is the chief operating officer of a national advice charity with 30 local offices and
600 staff and volunteers. Each office runs its own telephone line, opening hours and case
records. Grant income has fallen for three years, and the trustees want running costs cut by
15% in two years. Demand for online advice is rising, yet the clients in greatest need mostly
come in person. Several offices depend on local funders who expect a local service. The board
report shows only the number of clients seen. A consultant has proposed one national contact
centre.

Tasks:

1. Critically evaluate the present operating model against the charity's purpose and funding.
2. Formulate three options for the operating model. Appraise each one.
3. Design the measures that the trustees need to govern performance during and after the change.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real organisation, or a large part of one, to whose senior
  decisions the learner has access.
- It includes an evaluation of the present operating model, a designed set of performance
  measures and an appraised proposal for change, as presented to senior decision makers.
- It draws on published research and on data from the organisation, and it judges the strength
  of both.
- For outcome 4, if the change has not started, the learner gives evidence of how they led
  senior colleagues to a decision, and a reflective account of their own conduct. Confidential
  information is removed.

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to develop other leaders and shape the culture of an organisation.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate the culture of an organisation and the forces that sustain it | 1.1 Critically evaluate evidence of the present culture, which includes the gap between stated values and daily behaviour<br>1.2 Appraise how strategy, structure, systems, reward and the conduct of leaders sustain the culture<br>1.3 Critically evaluate the evidence that leaders can change a culture by intent |
| 2. Formulate and lead action to shape the culture that the strategy needs | 2.1 Formulate the culture that the strategy needs in terms of behaviour that people can observe<br>2.2 Design a connected set of actions that changes what the organisation measures, rewards and tolerates<br>2.3 Lead the senior team to act in line with the intended culture<br>2.4 Justify the indicators of culture that the board or the owners receive |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Culture as shared assumptions, habits and stories about how things are done.
Visible signs and the beliefs under them. Evidence: surveys, exit data, complaints, incidents,
who is promoted, what gets time at senior meetings, what people say when no senior person is
present. Sub-cultures by site, profession and level. What a senior person does not see, and
why. The systems that hold a culture in place. The debate about whether culture can be managed
or only influenced. The quality of the evidence for culture change programmes. Culture as a
cause of failures of conduct and safety.

**Outcome 2.** Culture that follows from strategy, compared with a list of values on a wall.
Behaviour stated so that a person can see it and count it. Levers: who is hired,
promoted and dismissed, what is measured and rewarded, where money and time go, and how leaders
act when things go wrong. The behaviour that is tolerated as the
real standard. The time that culture takes to change, and the pressure for quick results.
Cynicism after earlier programmes. Disagreement inside the senior team. The interest of the
board, regulators and the public in culture. Indicators that are hard to game.

**Outcome 3.** The move from the leadership of teams to the leadership of people who lead.
Direction without detailed control. What to delegate and what to keep. Accountability that is
clear and fair. The senior team as a team. The identification of future leaders, and bias in
who is seen as having potential. Development through hard assignments, feedback, coaching and
moves across the organisation. Succession for critical posts. The leader who gets results in a
way that harms other people: evidence, fairness, the signal that the response sends. Exits at
senior level.

**Outcome 4.** The shadow that a senior leader casts. Why feedback to senior people is filtered,
and how to get it straight. Sources: structured feedback from several levels, a coach or a
mentor, the board, exit interviews, results. Decisions under pressure as the true test of
values. Effects that appear after the leader has moved on. Limits of the leader's own knowledge
and judgement.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Tariq is the director of services at a national charity that runs 40 advice centres with 600
staff. The strategy commits the charity to open learning from mistakes. An external review of a
serious safeguarding failure found that staff in three regions had raised concerns, and that
their managers had told them to keep waiting times down. The regional head with the best
figures on waiting times and funding leads one of those regions, and two of her managers have
resigned this year. The trustees want assurance within three months. The chief executive wants
no public dispute before a major funding bid.

Tasks:

1. Critically evaluate what the case shows about the culture of the charity and what sustains
   it.
2. Design the actions that Tariq leads with the regional heads over the next year, and the
   indicators that the trustees receive.
3. Formulate the action that Tariq takes with the regional head, and justify it.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, presentation, professional
discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own leadership of at least two people who lead teams
  or functions, over a period of at least six months.
- It includes a critical evaluation of the culture of the organisation, or of a large part of
  it, that uses evidence from at least three sources.
- It includes the plan of action that the learner led, with indicators, and feedback on the
  learner from people at more than one level.
- People are made anonymous. If the organisation can be identified, the learner has its
  consent to use the material.

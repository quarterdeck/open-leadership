its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to develop a people strategy and a workforce plan that support the strategy of the organisation.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. | 2. ## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** How a strategy for growth, cost, quality, service or innovation changes the
people that an organisation needs. The debate about fit with strategy and investment in people
as a source of advantage in itself. Environment: demographics, migration, skills supply, pay
pressure, new technology, regulation, expectations of work. Evidence about the workforce: size,
cost, skills, age and service profiles, turnover, engagement, diversity, productivity. Weak
evidence and how to act on it.

**Outcome 2.** A people strategy as a small number of choices with consequences. Options and
their trade-offs: cost against resilience, speed against capability, central control against
local freedom. The employment offer and what the organisation asks in return. Fairness,
inclusion and the long-term health of the workforce as strategic matters. Consultation with
recognised unions and staff forums. The board's view of risk. A strategy that managers can
explain in a few sentences.

**Outcome 3.** Time horizons. Drivers of demand: activity, productivity, new services, technology.
Supply: retirements, turnover, internal movement, the external market, training pipelines.
Scenarios compared with a single forecast. Critical roles and roles that are hard to fill.
Succession for senior and specialist posts. Actions: recruit, develop, redeploy, retain, change
the work, change the place or pattern of work, reduce. Full cost of the workforce. Sensitivity
of the plan to its assumptions.

**Outcome 4.** The people director as a member of the top team. Alignment with finance and
operations plans. Governance: who owns the plan, how often it is reviewed, what triggers a
change. Measures of progress and measures of result. Reports to a board. Unintended effects on
groups of employees. Sources of challenge to their own judgement.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Marcus is the director of people at a council that employs 6,000 staff. The council must cut its
budget by a tenth in three years while demand for adult social care grows. A third of its social
workers and planning officers can retire within five years. Agency staff fill one post in six in
children's services at a high cost. Councillors have promised no compulsory redundancies. The
chief executive wants a people strategy and a workforce plan for the cabinet in four months. The
trade unions expect to be involved from the start.

Tasks:

1. Appraise the workforce risks. State the evidence that Marcus needs and its likely gaps.
2. Formulate two strategic options for the workforce and justify a choice between them.
3. Design the approach to forecasts and scenarios, and the governance of the plan.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own work on a people strategy or a workforce plan for
  an organisation, or for a part of one with 100 or more people.
- It includes the learner's analysis of strategy and environment, the options considered, the
  forecasts with their assumptions, and the plan with costs and measures.
- It includes evidence that the learner presented the work to a senior decision-making group, and
  the response of that group.
- Commercially sensitive figures can be changed or scaled. Individuals are not identified.

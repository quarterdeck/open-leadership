its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to set the direction for employment relations and reward across an organisation.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate the state of employment relations and reward in an organisation | 1.1 Critically evaluate the level of trust and the quality of employee voice, with evidence from more than one source<br>1.2 Critically evaluate how well the present reward arrangements support the strategy and treat people fairly<br>1.3 Appraise the external factors that will affect employment relations and reward in the next three to five years |
| 2. Formulate the direction for employment relations across an organisation | 2.1 Formulate an approach to employee voice, consultation and negotiation that fits the organisation<br>2.2 Design arrangements that prevent collective disputes and resolve them when they occur<br>2.3 Justify how much freedom line managers have and how the organisation keeps decisions consistent |
| 3. Design the direction for reward across an organisation | 3.1 Formulate reward principles that the organisation can explain to its employees<br>3.2 Design the outline of pay structures, progression, benefits and recognition that follows those principles<br>3.3 Appraise the risks in equal pay, pay gaps and senior pay, and design a response<br>3.4 Justify the cost of the proposals against what the organisation can afford |
| 4. Lead a major change in employment relations or reward and appraise its results | 4.1 Lead a consultation or a negotiation on a change that affects a large group of employees<br>4.2 Synthesise the effects that decisions on reward and on employment relations have on each other<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their own conduct and judgement in the change, and its effect on trust, cost and reputation |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Views of the employment relationship: shared interests, competing interests,
and a mix of the two. Evidence of trust and voice: surveys, grievances, disputes, turnover,
tribunal claims, the tone of meetings with representatives, what people say outside formal
channels. Reward evidence: market position, pay distribution, progression rates, pay gap
reports, cost, what employees value. External factors: the labour market, inflation, changes in
UK employment law, sector pay settlements, union activity, public and investor opinion.

**Outcome 2.** Forms of voice: recognised trade unions, staff forums, direct involvement, and
combinations. Collective bargaining and its scope. Partnership and its critics. Legal duties to
inform and consult, in general terms. Dispute procedures, outside conciliation and mediation.
Industrial action and plans for continuity. The line manager as the daily face of employment
relations. Consistency through policy, training, data and review of cases. Different approaches
in different parts of one organisation.

**Outcome 3.** Principles: market position, internal fairness, pay for the job, the person or
the result, transparency. Job evaluation and grading in outline. Progression by service, skill
or contribution. Variable pay and the evidence about its effects. Benefits, pensions and
flexibility as part of the total offer. Recognition that costs little. Equal pay law in general
terms and the use of an equal pay review. Pay gap reports. Senior pay: governance, the
remuneration committee, public scrutiny. Affordability and the cost of change.

**Outcome 4.** Preparation for a negotiation: interests, limits, authority, alternatives.
Conduct at the table and away from it. Communication with employees during a dispute. How a pay
decision changes relations, and how the state of relations limits pay decisions. Long-term cost
of a short-term settlement. Personal integrity under pressure from the board and from
representatives.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Siobhan is the director of human resources at a university with 4,500 staff and three
recognised trade unions. Income has fallen and the governing body wants to end automatic annual
pay progression and move to progression by contribution. An equal pay review last year found
gaps in professorial pay that have no clear explanation. One union has already held a strike
ballot on workload. Heads of department make their own retention payments, and nobody holds a
record of them. The vice-chancellor wants the change agreed within the academic year.

Tasks:

1. Critically evaluate the risks of the proposal for employment relations and for equal pay.
2. Formulate reward principles and an approach to negotiation that Siobhan can defend to the
   governing body and to the unions.
3. Design the control of retention payments by heads of department.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, professional discussion, witness
statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own work on the direction of employment relations and of
  reward for an organisation, or for a part of one with 100 or more people.
- It includes the learner's evaluation of the present state, the proposals with their costs, and
  the analysis of equal pay and pay gap risk.
- For criterion 4.1, it includes a witness statement or record of a real consultation or
  negotiation in which the learner had a leading part.
- Pay data is anonymous. The positions of representatives and individuals are not attributed by
  name.

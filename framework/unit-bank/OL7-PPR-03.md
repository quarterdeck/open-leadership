Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to design structures and lead development work that improve how an organisation performs.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate how the present organisation helps or limits performance | 1.1 Critically evaluate the fit between the strategy and the structure, processes, roles and decision rights of the organisation<br>1.2 Appraise the informal organisation: relationships, habits, power and what people believe is rewarded<br>1.3 Synthesise evidence from data, interviews and observation into a diagnosis that senior colleagues accept as fair |
| 2. Design a structure for an organisation or a large part of one | 2.1 Formulate design criteria from the strategy<br>2.2 Design two or more structural options and appraise the trade-offs of each<br>2.3 Design the roles, accountabilities, decision rights and links between units for the selected option<br>2.4 Justify the selected design, with its costs, its risks and its effects on people |
| 3. Lead development work that improves how people work together | 3.1 Design development work at the level of the team, between groups or across the organisation, from the diagnosis<br>3.2 Lead work that involves the people affected in the diagnosis and the solution<br>3.3 Justify the ethical basis of the work: consent, confidentiality and the use of what people say |
| 4. Lead the transition to a new design and appraise the results | 4.1 Design a transition plan that meets the legal duties to consult and treats people fairly<br>4.2 Appraise the effect of the design and the development work on performance and on people<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their own practice as a designer and a leader of development work |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** An organisation as a system: its aims, the division of work and authority,
the flow of work, the people and what is rewarded affect each other. Signs of poor fit: slow decisions, duplicated work, gaps between
units, overloaded roles, conflict at boundaries. The informal organisation and why a chart does
not show it. Methods of diagnosis: process and decision mapping, activity and cost data,
interviews, group sessions, surveys, observation. The diagnosis as a shared product. The risk
that a diagnosis only confirms the view of the sponsor.

**Outcome 2.** Design criteria as tests that any option must pass. Bases for grouping work:
function, product or service, customer, place, process. Matrix, project and network forms.
Centralise or devolve. Layers and spans of control. Accountability for results. Decision rights
and who is consulted. Links across units: shared goals, joint teams, liaison roles, shared
information. No design is free of trade-offs. The debate about hierarchy and self-managed
teams. Cost of the structure and cost of the change. Design led by strategy compared with design
around present people.

**Outcome 3.** Organisation development as planned work, based on behavioural science, to
improve how an organisation functions. Levels: person, team, between groups, whole system.
Forms of work: team development, large group events, work on conflict between units, feedback
of survey data, inquiry into what works well. Participation and its limits. The role of the
practitioner: expert or helper to the client's own process. Contract with the client. Who the
client is. Consent, confidentiality, and the harm from data that is used against people.

**Outcome 4.** The sequence of a transition. Consultation with individuals and representatives
under UK employment law, in general terms. Fair selection for roles. Support for people who move,
stay or leave. Loss of knowledge and of working relationships. Performance during the dip that
follows a restructure. Measures: speed and quality of decisions, cost, service, engagement,
turnover of the people the organisation most needs. The habit of repeated restructure and the
evidence about its results.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Iwan is the director of people and organisation development at a national health charity
with 900 staff. Two charities merged 18 months ago. There are still two fundraising teams, two
helplines and two sets of regional offices. Donors receive appeals from both. Staff describe
themselves by the name of their old charity. The trustees want one structure and a saving of a
tenth of staff costs within a year. The chief executive came from the larger charity, and staff
from the smaller one expect to lose most.

Tasks:

1. Design the diagnosis. State how Iwan will make it fair in the eyes of both groups.
2. Formulate design criteria and appraise two structural options for fundraising and regions.
3. Design development work to run beside the restructure, and justify its ethical basis.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, professional discussion, witness
statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from real design or development work that the learner led for an
  organisation, or for a part of one with 50 or more people.
- It includes the diagnosis and its evidence, the design criteria, the options with an appraisal
  of each, and the transition plan.
- It includes evidence of at least one piece of development work that the learner led with a
  group, and the agreement that the learner made with the client about consent and
  confidentiality.
- Individuals and their statements are made anonymous. If the design is not yet in place, the
  learner states for criterion 4.2 the measures and the first results available.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to set the direction for learning and for the development of leaders across an organisation.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate the capability that an organisation needs and how well it builds that capability | 1.1 Critically evaluate the capabilities that the strategy of the organisation demands, now and in the next three to five years<br>1.2 Appraise the present learning provision: its reach, its cost and its effect on performance<br>1.3 Appraise the strength of the evidence for the approaches to learning and to leadership development that the organisation uses |
| 2. Formulate a learning strategy for an organisation | 2.1 Formulate priorities for learning that follow from the strategy of the organisation<br>2.2 Justify where the organisation invests, where it stops, and what it provides itself or buys<br>2.3 Design the governance of learning: the roles of the learning function, line managers, learners and suppliers, and the use of technology |
| 3. Design how an organisation develops its leaders | 3.1 Formulate what the organisation expects of leaders at each level, in terms that can be observed<br>3.2 Design a system that connects the identification of potential, assessment, experience, programmes, coaching and succession<br>3.3 Appraise how fair the access to development is, and design changes |
| 4. Lead the strategy and appraise its value to the organisation | 4.1 Lead senior colleagues to act as sponsors and as developers of other people<br>4.2 Design an evaluation that connects learning to the results of the organisation, and judge its findings<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their own leadership of learning and its long-term effect on the organisation |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Capability at the level of the organisation, compared with the skills of
individuals. Sources of future need: strategy, technology, regulation, the age profile of
experts. Evidence about present provision: spend for each employee, who gets access, completion,
change in performance, the view of line managers. Activity measures that say little about
effect. Debates in the subject: how far leadership can be taught, formal programmes compared
with experience, learning styles and other popular ideas with weak evidence, the claims made
for new technology.

**Outcome 2.** A learning strategy as a set of choices under a limited budget. Mandatory learning
and its share of time and money. Critical skills. Develop inside or recruit from outside. Central
and local provision. Suppliers: selection, contracts, dependence. Qualifications and
apprenticeships as funding and career routes. Platforms, content libraries, data and artificial
intelligence: benefits, costs and risks. The line manager as the main influence on whether
learning is used. A culture in which people share knowledge and admit mistakes.

**Outcome 3.** Expectations of leaders that are specific to the organisation and to the level.
Transitions between levels and what a leader must stop as well as start. Potential compared with
present performance, and the weak reliability of ratings of potential. Assessment for
development: feedback from several sources, development centres, profiles used for
self-awareness. Experience as the main developer: stretch roles, moves across functions,
projects, exposure to the board. Programmes, coaching, mentoring and sponsorship. Succession
plans and talent reviews. Access: who is nominated, by whom, and which groups are missing.
Openness about who is on a list.

**Outcome 4.** Senior leaders as teachers, sponsors and examples. The case to a board for
long-term investment during pressure on cost. Evaluation: a chain of evidence from learning to
behaviour to results, comparison groups, time lags, the limits of a single return-on-investment
figure. Measures of leadership strength: internal fill rate for senior roles, readiness of
successors, retention of people with potential, engagement of teams. What the learner's own
development shows to others.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Amara is the group director of learning at a construction company with 3,200 employees. The
company has won large contracts that need digital design and low-carbon building skills. Most of
the learning budget goes on mandatory safety courses. Site managers are promoted for technical
skill, and three major projects have lost staff because of poor leadership. A leadership
programme for senior managers has run for six years with good feedback and no evidence of
effect. Half of the contracts directors can retire within six years. The board has asked for a
learning strategy with a flat budget.

Tasks:

1. Critically evaluate the present provision and state what Amara will propose to stop.
2. Design a system to develop site managers and successors to the contracts directors.
3. Design an evaluation that the board will accept as evidence of value.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, work-based assignment, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own work on the direction of learning or of leadership
  development for an organisation, or for a part of one with 100 or more people.
- It includes the learner's evaluation of present provision, the strategy with its investment
  choices, and the design of the leadership development system.
- It includes an evaluation design and, where they exist, findings from at least one programme
  or activity.
- It includes evidence that senior decision-makers considered the strategy. Individuals named in
  succession or talent records are made anonymous.

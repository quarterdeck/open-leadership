## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to use financial information and risk analysis to make strategic decisions.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate the financial position and performance of an organisation | 1.1 Critically evaluate the financial statements and management information of the organisation over more than one period<br>1.2 Appraise how the organisation is funded, and the cost and the conditions of that funding<br>1.3 Synthesise financial and non-financial information to judge if the organisation can sustain its strategy |
| 2. Appraise a strategic investment | 2.1 Appraise the investment from its cash flows over its whole life, with the value of money over time<br>2.2 Critically evaluate the assumptions of the appraisal with tests of sensitivity and with scenarios<br>2.3 Justify a recommendation that weighs the financial return against the effects on people, society, the environment and reputation |
| 3. Design the way in which an organisation governs strategic risk | 3.1 Formulate a statement of the risk that the organisation accepts in pursuit of its strategy, for approval by the board<br>3.2 Critically evaluate the principal risks to the strategy and the ways in which they combine<br>3.3 Design the ownership, reports and independent assurance of the principal risks |
| 4. Lead a strategic decision on finance and risk | 4.1 Formulate a financial plan that funds the strategy, with reserves for uncertainty<br>4.2 Lead the decision through the governance of the organisation with incomplete information<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their own judgement in the decision, which includes their response to challenge |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** The statement of income and costs, the statement of assets and debts, and the
statement of cash flows. What each one hides. Measures of return, of the ability to pay debts
as they fall due, of borrowing and of efficiency. Trends and comparison with similar
organisations. The effect of accounting choices on reported results. Sources of funds: owners,
lenders, retained surplus, grants, donors. Conditions that lenders and funders set. Reserves.
Finance in commercial, public and charitable organisations. Signs of financial strain before
the accounts show it.

**Outcome 2.** Cash flows compared with accounting profit. Whole-life cost. Discounted cash
flows, the rate of return and the payback period, with the weakness of each method. The cost
of delay and the value of the option to wait. Optimism in forecasts of cost, time and demand.
Changes to one assumption at a time, and whole scenarios. Benefits and harms that have no
market price. Ways to buy, lease or share an asset. Debates about how far ahead a board can
see.

**Outcome 3.** The link between strategy and risk: no return without risk. The amount of risk
that the organisation can bear and the amount that it chooses to bear. Principal risks:
strategic, financial, operational, legal and regulatory, technology, climate, reputation. Risks
that combine and spread. Events that nobody listed. The duties of directors and trustees in
general terms. Three layers of control: the managers who own the risk, the functions that
oversee it and independent review. Reports that inform a board without false comfort.

**Outcome 4.** Long-term financial plans and their assumptions. Reserves, headroom and the
ability to change course. The roles of the board, its committees, the executive, auditors and
regulators. How to present uncertainty to decision makers. Pressure to give the answer that
the board wants. Group loyalty at senior level. Disagreement on the record. Review of a major
decision after the event.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Kwame is the finance director of a regional bus company with 400 vehicles. The board must
decide whether to replace 60 diesel buses with electric buses. A government grant pays a third
of the price if the order is placed within five months. The city council plans a clean air
charge in two years, which is not yet confirmed. Passenger numbers are 12% lower than five
years ago. The depot needs a new power supply, and its cost is uncertain. The bank loan has
conditions on debt that the purchase would test. The managing director strongly favours the
order.

Tasks:

1. Appraise the investment. State the assumptions that matter most and test them.
2. Critically evaluate the principal risks of the order and of delay, and how they combine.
3. Formulate the recommendation and the conditions that Kwame puts to the board.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, presentation, professional discussion,
reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real organisation whose financial statements and risk reports the
  learner can use, and from one real strategic decision with a large financial effect.
- It includes an evaluation of the financial position over at least three years, an investment
  appraisal with tests of its assumptions, and a designed approach to the principal risks.
- It includes the paper or presentation that the learner gave to senior decision makers, and a
  reflective account of the learner's part in the decision.
- Published accounts can be used as they are. Unpublished figures are disguised where the
  organisation requires it.

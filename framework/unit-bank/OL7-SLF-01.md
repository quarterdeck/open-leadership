Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to evaluate their own leadership critically and act ethically under pressure and scrutiny.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate their own leadership and its effect on the organisation | 1.1 Critically evaluate evidence about their leadership from the people they report to, their peers, the people they lead and the results of the organisation<br>1.2 Appraise how their values, assumptions and experience shape their strategic decisions<br>1.3 Critically evaluate how their position and power change the information that reaches them |
| 2. Formulate ethical positions on decisions in which obligations compete | 2.1 Critically evaluate an organisational decision in which the obligations to different groups conflict<br>2.2 Formulate a position that states the trade-offs, and justify it to the people who hold them to account<br>2.3 Appraise the short-term and long-term effects of the decision on people, money and reputation |
| 3. Lead with integrity under pressure and scrutiny | 3.1 Appraise the pressures that lead senior leaders to poor judgement, with reference to their own role<br>3.2 Design safeguards that bring challenge and bad news to them and to the senior team<br>3.3 Lead a response to a failure or a criticism that is open about the facts and about responsibility |
| 4. Design their own development for long-term effect as a senior leader | 4.1 Synthesise evidence and reflection into priorities for their development<br>4.2 Design a plan that protects their judgement, health and energy in a demanding role<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their progress and their effect on the leaders who report to them |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Why honest feedback becomes rare as seniority increases. Evidence from a
governing body, peers, direct reports, staff surveys, exit data and results. The strength of
each type of evidence. Assumptions that a career has made invisible: about people, risk, growth
and control. The shadow of a senior leader: small remarks that the organisation treats as
orders. The present debates on what leadership is, and the weak evidence behind many popular
claims about it.

**Outcome 2.** Obligations to owners or funders, staff, customers or service users, suppliers,
communities and future generations. Decisions in which each option harms a group: closures,
redundancies, prices, safety, use of data, use of automation. Ethical reasoning from
consequences, duties and character, and the limits of each at organisational scale. The
difference between an explanation and an excuse. Accountability to a board, to owners, to a
regulator or to the public. Effects that appear years later.

**Outcome 3.** Conditions that come before ethical failure: targets with no questions about
method, a team that agrees too fast, isolation, tiredness, a run of success, loyalty that is
valued above truth. Safeguards: a person with licence to disagree, direct contact with front
line staff and customers, independent review, protected routes to raise concerns, records of
dissent. Conduct under scrutiny from the media, regulators and staff. Apology, correction and
remedy. Legal advice, and a legal duty of candour where one applies.

**Outcome 4.** Development at senior level: fewer courses, more reflection, peers, coaching and
challenge from outside the organisation. Sources of energy and causes of exhaustion. Limits and
recovery. Life outside the role. The development of other leaders as a measure of a senior
leader. Succession, and the right time to move on.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Imran is chief operating officer of an NHS trust. A year ago he set a hard target to cut the
waiting list, and he praised the service that improved fastest. An internal review now shows
that staff in that service removed patients from the list without the correct checks. Two
administrators say that they raised concerns and that nobody replied. A local journalist has
asked for a comment. The chief executive wants a short statement that blames a software fault.
The board meets in nine days. Imran knows that his target and his praise are part of the cause.

Tasks:

1. Critically evaluate Imran's part in the failure. State the evidence that he needs about his
   own leadership.
2. Formulate the position that Imran takes to the chief executive and the board on the
   statement. Justify it.
3. Design safeguards that bring concerns of this type to the senior team sooner.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: reflective account, work-based assignment, professional discussion, portfolio
of work evidence, witness statement.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own senior role, or from work with organisation-wide
  effect, over a period of at least six months.
- It includes evidence about the learner's leadership from three or more groups, and a critical
  account of one decision in which obligations competed.
- It includes the safeguards that the learner designed and an account of their effect, or of
  the response of the senior team to them.
- People are made anonymous. Material that is confidential to a board, or that is commercially
  sensitive, is summarised with permission.

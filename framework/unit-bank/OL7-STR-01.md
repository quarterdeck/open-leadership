Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to develop a strategy from evidence and lead its execution.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Critically evaluate the strategic position of an organisation | 1.1 Critically evaluate evidence about the customers, the competitors or comparable organisations, and the environment of the organisation<br>1.2 Appraise the resources and capabilities of the organisation, and which of them are hard for others to copy<br>1.3 Synthesise the analysis into a statement of the strategic issues that the organisation must resolve |
| 2. Formulate a strategy from evidence | 2.1 Formulate strategic options that differ in substance<br>2.2 Critically evaluate the options for fit, feasibility, risk and acceptability to stakeholders<br>2.3 Justify a choice, which includes what the organisation will not do and the assumptions on which the choice depends |
| 3. Design and lead the execution of a strategy | 3.1 Design a plan that connects the strategy to resources, structure, measures and accountability<br>3.2 Lead the communication of the strategy so that leaders at each level can act on it<br>3.3 Design measures and review points that test the assumptions early |
| 4. Lead the review and adaptation of a strategy | 4.1 Critically evaluate progress and the causes of the differences from the plan<br>4.2 Justify decisions to continue, adapt or stop parts of the strategy<br>4.3 Critically evaluate their own leadership of strategy and its effect on the result |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Sources of evidence and their quality: market and customer data, financial
results, the views of staff and partners, published research. Position in a market, or in a
system of public or voluntary services. Resources and capabilities: what the organisation has
and what it can do. Capabilities that are valuable, rare and hard to copy. Dependence on a few
customers, funders, suppliers or people. Bias in strategic analysis: evidence chosen to fit a
view that is already held. The step from pages of analysis to a short list of issues.

**Outcome 2.** Debates about strategy: deliberate plans and strategies that emerge from action;
position in a market and strength from within; analysis and experiment. The limits of each
view. Types of choice: where to compete or serve, how to be different, how to grow, what to
own and what to buy, when to partner. Options that are true alternatives. Scenarios as a test.
Tests of an option: fit, feasibility, risk, return, acceptability. Trade-offs, and the cost of
a strategy that tries to please each group. Decisions with incomplete information.

**Outcome 3.** Reasons why strategies fail in execution: too many priorities, no change to
budgets, old measures, unclear ownership, middle managers who were told and not involved. The
link to budgets, structure, people and systems. A small number of priorities with one owner
each. Communication as repeated conversation in which leaders at each level translate the
strategy for their area. Leading and lagging measures. Early signals that an assumption is
wrong.

**Outcome 4.** A rhythm of review at senior level. Causes of variance: the plan, the execution
or the environment. The pull to continue with a failing course because of the money and pride
already spent. The decision to stop. Honest reports to a board or to owners. Learning that
changes the next strategy. The effect of the senior leader's attention, patience and
consistency on execution.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Angus is managing director of a family-owned company that makes commercial kitchen equipment.
It employs 240 people. One pub chain gives 55 per cent of sales and has asked for a price cut.
The owners want a steady dividend. The sales director wants to export. The operations director
wants to sell service contracts on installed equipment, which needs engineers that the company
does not have. The last three-year plan listed 40 projects, and few were finished. The owners
expect a new strategy in four months.

Tasks:

1. Synthesise the position of the company into three or four strategic issues. State the
   evidence that Angus still needs.
2. Formulate two options that differ in substance. Critically evaluate them and state what the
   company stops doing under each.
3. Design the execution of the preferred option so that it does not repeat the failure of the
   last plan.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, presentation, professional discussion,
portfolio of work evidence.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from a real strategy for an organisation, or for a large part of one, in
  which the learner had a leading role.
- It includes the analysis, the options that were rejected with reasons, and the assumptions
  behind the choice.
- It covers at least six months of execution, with a review against measures. If the strategy
  is newer, the learner critically evaluates the execution of an earlier strategy for criteria
  4.1 and 4.2.
- Material that is commercially sensitive or confidential to a board is summarised with
  permission. People are not named.

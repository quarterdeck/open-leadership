Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to work with boards, regulators, partners and the public, and respond to the external environment.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. | 2. Lead relationships with stakeholders whose interests compete | 2.1 Critically evaluate the interests, power and legitimacy of the stakeholders of the organisation<br>2.2 Formulate an approach to engagement that states the priorities and the trade-offs between groups<br>2.3 Lead engagement with a regulator, a funder, a partner or a public body on a contested matter |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** Forms of governing body: a company board, charity trustees, school governors, a
council, a board of a public body, owners of a family firm. Direction and oversight compared
with management. The roles of chair, chief executive, non-executives and committees. Duties of
directors and trustees in general terms. Assurance: internal controls, audit, risk reports.
Common failures: a board that manages, a board that only approves, papers that hide the bad
news, one dominant person, members who are all alike. Reviews of board effectiveness.

**Outcome 2.** Who counts as a stakeholder, and the debate about whose interests come first.
Power, legitimacy and urgency. Groups with a strong claim and little power. Regulators and
inspectors: their purpose, their powers, and the value of an open relationship. Funders,
commissioners and investors. Partnerships, joint ventures and supply chains. Trade unions and
staff bodies. Communities and campaign groups. Engagement compared with communication.
Negotiation at senior level. Lobbying, and its ethical limits.

**Outcome 3.** Methods to read the environment: scanning, trends, scenarios, weak signals.
Uncertainty, and the difference between a forecast and a scenario. Areas of law and regulation
that shape strategy: company or charity law, competition, employment, UK equality law, UK data
protection law, health and safety law, environmental regulation, rules of a sector.
Obligations beyond the law: environmental effect, social value, fair treatment in the supply
chain. Responses: comply, adapt, shape, exit. The cost of a late response.

**Outcome 4.** Accountability to a governing body, owners, funders, regulators, staff and the
public. Annual reports and other public reports. Honest measures, and reports that include
failures. Reputation as the result of conduct over time. Scrutiny from the media, social
media, inquiries and elected bodies. Preparation for a crisis. Who speaks, what is said, and
when. Legal advice and openness. Recovery of trust after a failure.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Yasmin is chief executive of a charity that houses homeless young people in three cities. A
council contract gives half of its income. The council now wants each young person moved on
within twelve weeks, and Yasmin's data shows that many return to the street if they leave that
soon. The chair of trustees, a former council officer, wants to accept the terms. Two trustees
want to refuse and speak out in public. A betting company has offered a large donation that
would cover the gap for a year. Staff have started a petition against it. The contract
decision is due in five weeks.

Tasks:

1. Critically evaluate the interests, power and legitimacy of each stakeholder in the decision.
2. Formulate the response that Yasmin recommends to the trustees on the contract and on the
   donation. Justify it.
3. Design the information and the process that the trustees need for a sound decision, given
   the position of the chair.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: work-based assignment, project report, professional discussion, portfolio of
work evidence, witness statement, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence comes from the learner's own work with a governing body and with external
  stakeholders over a period of at least six months.
- It includes a critical evaluation of governance in the learner's organisation, with the
  improvements that the learner designed.
- It includes an account of one contested engagement that the learner led, and a response to an
  external change that the learner put to the governing body. A witness statement from a board
  member, a chair or a senior external contact supports the account.
- Board papers and stakeholder records are summarised with permission. People and partner
  organisations are made anonymous.

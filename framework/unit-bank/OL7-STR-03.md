Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. The level and the credits of this unit are
its own. The unit is not on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.

## Purpose

This unit enables the learner to design and complete an investigation into a strategic question at work, and act on the findings.

## Learning outcomes and assessment criteria

The learner must meet each criterion.

| Learning outcome | Assessment criteria |
|---|---|
| 1. Formulate a strategic question for investigation | 1.1 Justify the choice of question with reference to a strategic decision that the organisation faces<br>1.2 Critically evaluate published research and the existing evidence in the organisation on the question<br>1.3 Formulate the question, aims, scope and limits of the investigation, and agree them with a sponsor |
| 2. Design an investigation that is sound and ethical | 2.1 Design a method, and justify the data sources, the sample and the tools against the alternatives<br>2.2 Appraise the ethical risks, which include consent, confidentiality and their own position in the organisation, and design controls for them<br>2.3 Design the collection, storage and deletion of personal data to meet UK data protection law |
| 3. Synthesise evidence into findings | 3.1 Lead the collection of data to the design, and justify each change to it<br>3.2 Synthesise evidence in numbers and in words into findings that answer the question<br>3.3 Critically evaluate the strength of the findings, with their limits, possible bias and other explanations |
| 4. Formulate recommendations and lead action on them | 4.1 Formulate recommendations that follow from the findings, with costs, risks and priorities<br>4.2 Justify the recommendations to the people who decide, and lead the agreement of action<br>4.3 Critically evaluate the investigation and their own practice as an investigator |

## Indicative content

This content is a guide for the centre. It is not a list of things to assess.

**Outcome 1.** What makes a question strategic: a decision depends on the answer, the answer is
not known, and the stakes are large. Questions that are too wide, too narrow or already
answered. A question that hides a preferred answer. Search and critical reading of published
research: the method, the sample, the context, who paid for it. Evidence that the organisation
already holds. The sponsor: a senior person who wants the answer and can act on it. A written
proposal with scope, limits, time and resources.

**Outcome 2.** Designs: survey, interviews, focus groups, observation, analysis of existing
data, comparison of cases, a trial or pilot. Numbers and words, and reasons to combine them.
Samples and who is left out. Design of questions that do not lead. Validity and reliability in
plain terms. The insider as investigator: access and knowledge on one side, power and
assumptions on the other. Informed consent, the right to refuse, anonymity in small groups,
harm to participants. Ethical review by a person outside the investigation. Personal data:
purpose, minimum data, secure storage, a date for deletion.

**Outcome 3.** A record of what was done and why. Changes of plan when access or response is
poor. Analysis of numbers: description, comparison, trends, the difference between a link and a
cause. Analysis of words: themes, coding, cases that do not fit. Use of several sources to
test one finding. Honest treatment of results that surprise or disappoint the sponsor. Limits:
sample size, response rate, timing, the investigator's own bias. Explanations that compete.

**Outcome 4.** The line from question to evidence to finding to recommendation. Recommendations
that a board can decide on: specific, costed, in order of priority, with risks and owners. A
report that a busy reader can use: a summary first, the method in brief, the detail in
appendices. Presentation to decision makers, and how to respond to challenge. Feedback to the
people who took part. Plans to act and to measure the effect. Review of the investigation:
what the learner would repeat and what they would change.

## Workplace scenario

Use this scenario for discussion, or for assessment if the learner cannot use a case from their
own work.

Rhiannon is director of people at a university. One in five professional services staff left
last year, twice the figure of three years ago. The finance director is sure that the cause is
pay. Some heads of department blame the hybrid working policy, which Rhiannon wrote. Exit
interviews are held for fewer than a third of leavers, and the notes are in free text. The
vice-chancellor wants costed recommendations for the budget round in four months. Several
departments have fewer than ten staff, and people there fear that their answers will identify
them.

Tasks:

1. Formulate the question and the scope of the investigation. State who the sponsor is and
   what is left out.
2. Design the method. Justify it against one alternative, and state the controls for consent,
   anonymity and Rhiannon's own interest in the result.
3. Explain how Rhiannon tests the two explanations that senior people already hold, and how
   she reports a finding that one of them does not want.

## Assessment

Suitable methods: project report, presentation, professional discussion, reflective account.

Evidence requirements:

- The evidence is a report of one complete investigation that the learner designed and led on a
  real strategic question for an organisation. A guide to length is 6,000 to 8,000 words, plus
  appendices.
- The report includes the agreed proposal, the method with its ethical controls, the evidence
  collected by the learner, the findings, the limits and the recommendations.
- The learner presents the findings to the sponsor or to another decision maker. The evidence
  includes the response and the action agreed, or the reasons why no action was agreed.
- Participants are anonymous and gave informed consent. The evidence contains no raw personal
  data.

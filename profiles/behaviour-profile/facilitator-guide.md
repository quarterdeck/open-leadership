# OL Behaviour Profile: facilitator guide

Version 0.1.0 (draft, before the pilot).

A facilitator is a person who gives the profile to others and helps them to use the result: a
coach, a trainer, a people practitioner or a manager. The facilitator is never the line manager
of a person who takes the profile, because the person who scores the answers sees them. You do not need a licence or a
certificate. You do need to read this guide and the
[technical manual](technical-manual.md), and to follow the rules on
[data protection](../data-protection.md).

## What you must say every time

1. The profile is for development. It is never used for a decision about a person.
2. It is voluntary. A person who does not take part has no disadvantage.
3. It is a draft that is not yet tested. The result is a suggestion.
4. The person decides who sees the result. The facilitator who scores the answers sees it, and
   nobody else does unless the person agrees.
5. No result is good or bad.

If your organisation cannot keep these five promises, do not use the profile.

## Before

- Agree the purpose with the person or the team. "To understand how we work together" is a
  purpose. "To find out who fits" is not permitted.
- Give each person the notice from the data protection page, and time to decide.
- Tell people how long it takes (about 8 minutes) and when they get the result.
- For a team, ask each person in private if their result can be part of the team report. Do
  not ask in front of the group.

## The questionnaire

- Use the 48 items in the order of the item bank, with the instruction and the response
  labels from section 5 of the technical manual.
- Ask people to answer about themselves at work now.
- Score the answers with the open scoring code. Do not change the scoring.

## A one-to-one conversation (45 minutes)

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 5 minutes | Repeat the five points. Ask what the person wants from the conversation |
| 10 minutes | The person reads the report. Ask: "Where do you recognise yourself? Where not?" |
| 15 minutes | Take the one or two dimensions that matter most to the person. Ask for a real situation from the last month for each. Let the example carry the conversation, not the score |
| 10 minutes | Costs and uses. "When does this help you? When does it cost you?" |
| 5 minutes | One thing to try in the next two weeks, and how the person will know if it worked |

Good questions:

- "What would your team say about this?"
- "When are you different from this description?"
- "What do you want to keep? What do you want to do more or less often?"

If the person disagrees with a result, accept it. They may be right: the profile is short,
it is not yet tested, and behaviour changes with the situation. Ask what description would be
more accurate.

## A team session (90 minutes)

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 10 minutes | The five points. The purpose. Agree that what people share stays in the room |
| 15 minutes | Explain the four dimensions with an everyday example of each. People place themselves on each line from their own report, if they choose to |
| 20 minutes | Show the team spread. For each dimension: "What do we see? Does it match our experience?" |
| 25 minutes | Small groups take one dimension each and use the questions in the team report |
| 15 minutes | Two or three agreements about how the team works, each with an owner and a review date |
| 5 minutes | Close. Remind people that they own their results |

A person can take part in the session without sharing a result.

## Errors to prevent

| Error | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "She is a high Voice" | A score becomes a label for a person | Say "her Voice score is higher" or, better, describe the behaviour |
| A result used as an excuse: "I am low Method, so I am late" | The profile describes a tendency. It does not remove a responsibility | Ask what the person will do about the cost |
| A manager asks for the team's individual results | It breaks the promise, and people will not answer honestly again | Refuse. Offer the team report |
| A result used to choose a person for a job or a task | The profile is not validated for that, and it is not permitted | Refuse, and explain why |
| Combining scores into types | The profile has four independent lines. Types hide most of the information | Read each line, then how they work together |
| Treating a small difference as real | Bands overlap | Look at the bands, not only the marks |

## Words to use

Say "tends to", "more often", "prefers". Do not say "is a", "always", "cannot". Do not use
words from medicine or psychology, such as "disorder", "symptom" or "diagnosis". If a person
tells you about a health matter, listen, and point them to the right support. It is not a
subject for this profile.

## Used with the OL Preference Profile

The behaviour profile describes how a person says that they act. The preference profile describes the
conditions that a person likes. If a person takes both, compare them: a difference between
what a person prefers and how they act is often the most useful part of the conversation.

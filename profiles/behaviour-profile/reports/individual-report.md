# OL Behaviour Profile: individual report template

Version 0.1.0 (draft). Text in `{braces}` is replaced for each person. For each dimension, keep
the paragraph for the person's position and remove the other two.

Design rules for a person who builds this report:

- Show each score as a mark on a straight horizontal line from 0 to 100, with the band as a
  thicker part of the line. Use one neutral colour for all four lines.
- Do not use a circle, a wheel, quadrants, a colour for each dimension or a letter code.
- Do not join the four scores into a type or a label for the person.

## Voice: {voice.position}

Voice is how readily you take the lead and state a view.

**Lower.** You tend to listen first and to speak when you have something considered to say.
You let others lead when they want to. People are likely to find you easy to work with and
hard to read. Useful: you hear what others miss, and you do not fight for position. The cost:
your view can arrive too late, or never, and others can take your silence for agreement. Try
this: before a meeting, decide the one thing that you will say, and say it in the first ten
minutes.

**Middle.** You lead in some situations and follow in others. You probably state a view when
you know the subject or feel strongly, and hold back when you do not. Useful: you can adapt to
the group. The cost: people cannot always predict which of the two they will get. Try this:
notice what makes you hold back, and decide if that reason is good.

**Higher.** You tend to take charge, to say what you think early, and to try to persuade.
People are likely to know where you stand. Useful: you give direction and get things moving.
The cost: quieter people can stop offering ideas, and you can win a discussion that you
needed to hear. Try this: in your next meeting, ask two people for their view before you give
yours.

## Warmth: {warmth.position}

Warmth is how much you attend to other people and to relationships.

**Lower.** You tend to keep to the task and to keep some distance. You probably do not spend
much time on small talk. Useful: you stay objective, and difficult decisions about people do
not stop you. The cost: people can think that you do not care, and they tell you less. Try
this: start one conversation each day with a question about the person, and listen to the
answer.

**Middle.** You are friendly when the situation asks for it and businesslike when it does
not. Useful: you can give both support and distance. The cost: under pressure you may drop the
attention to people first. Try this: when work gets busy, check who you have not spoken to.

**Higher.** You tend to notice what others need, to make people welcome and to give them
time. People are likely to bring you their problems. Useful: you build trust and hold a group
together. The cost: you can delay a hard message, take on other people's problems, and run
out of time for your own work. Try this: when you must give difficult feedback, write the
plain version first, then deliver that version kindly.

## Method: {method.position}

Method is how much you prefer plans, order and completion.

**Lower.** You tend to start when the need is close, to work in bursts and to tolerate
disorder. Useful: you stay flexible and do not over-plan. The cost: late starts put pressure
on you and on others, and details can slip. Try this: for one important task, agree a first
small step and a date for it with another person.

**Middle.** You plan when the task needs it and improvise when it does not. Useful: you fit
the effort to the task. The cost: your standard can vary with your interest in the work. Try
this: decide at the start how exact a piece of work must be, and tell the people who depend
on it.

**Higher.** You tend to prepare, to start promptly, to follow the plan and to finish. You
like things in order. Useful: people can rely on you, and your work is accurate. The cost: a
change of plan can frustrate you, and you can spend effort on a level of finish that nobody
needs. Try this: ask "what is good enough here?" before you start.

## Exploration: {exploration.position}

Exploration is how much you look for new ideas and change.

**Lower.** You tend to prefer what is known and proven, and you value routine. Useful: you
protect what works, and you see the risks of a new idea. The cost: you can reject a change
before you test it, and others can see you as a block. Try this: when you hear a new idea,
ask one question about how it would work before you give your opinion.

**Middle.** You like some variety and some routine. You probably accept a change when the
reason is clear. Useful: you can connect people who want change with people who do not. The
cost: you may wait for others to make the case. Try this: name one thing in your work that
you would change, and one that you would protect.

**Higher.** You tend to look for variety, new ideas and new ways to do things. Routine tires
you. Useful: you bring ideas and energy for change. The cost: you can start more than you
finish, and change things that worked. Try this: before you start something new, say what you
will stop or finish first.

## How your dimensions work together

- Higher Voice with lower Warmth can look forceful. Higher Voice with higher Warmth can look
  encouraging. The Voice is the same.
- Higher Exploration with lower Method produces many starts. Higher Exploration with higher
  Method produces fewer, finished changes.

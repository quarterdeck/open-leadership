# Data protection for the profiles

Version 0.1.0 (draft).

A profile result is personal data. These rules apply to each organisation that gives an Open
Leadership profile to people. The organisation is the controller of the data under UK data
protection law. This page is a design standard. It is not legal advice. Read the current
guidance of the Information Commissioner's Office before you start.

## The rules

1. **Voluntary.** A person chooses to take part. A person who says no has no disadvantage. An
   employer does not make a profile a condition of a job, a course or a team event.
2. **Informed.** Before the questionnaire, the person reads a notice in plain words: what the
   profile is, what it is not, who will see the result, how long it is kept, and how to delete
   it.
3. **The person sees the result first** and decides who else sees it. A facilitator sees it
   only if the person agrees. A manager does not get a copy from the organisation.
4. **Minimal data.** Collect the answers and a way to give the result to the person. Do not
   collect date of birth, home address or other data that the profile does not need.
5. **No special category data.** No item and no report text refers to health, belief,
   politics, sexual life or ethnic origin. Do not add such questions.
6. **Short retention.** Delete identified answers and results after 12 months or less, or
   sooner if the person asks. Tell the person the period.
7. **Deletion on request.** The person can delete their result at any time, and the
   organisation confirms it.
8. **No decisions about a person.** Do not use a result for recruitment, selection, promotion,
   pay, redundancy or discipline. A result is never the only basis, or a part of the basis, for
   such a decision.
9. **No sale and no other use.** Do not sell results, and do not use them for marketing.
10. **Team reports protect the person.** A team report shows no name without that person's
    agreement, and it shows no summary for fewer than three people.
11. **Research data is anonymous.** Data for the pilot or for norms has no name, no email
    address and no employer name. Group questions are optional and have broad categories.
12. **Security.** Limit access to the people who need it. Protect data in storage and in
    transit.

## Lawful basis

The organisation decides and records its lawful basis. In employment, consent is often weak
because the employer has more power than the worker. For that reason rules 1 and 8 are strict:
they make the choice real. Consider a data protection impact assessment before a first use,
because a profile evaluates personal aspects of people.

## The notice for the person

A model text. Change the parts in brackets.

> This questionnaire is the [OL Behaviour Profile / OL Preference Profile]. It describes how
> you tend to work. It is for your own development. It does not measure ability, and it has no
> right or wrong answers.
>
> You do not have to take part. If you do, you will see your result first. Nobody else sees
> it unless you agree. [Organisation] will not use it for any decision about your job.
>
> [Organisation] keeps your answers and your result for [period, 12 months or less] and then
> deletes them. You can ask [contact] to delete them sooner.
>
> The profile is a draft that is not yet tested. Read your result as a suggestion, and say so
> if it does not fit you.

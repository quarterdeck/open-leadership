# OL Preference Profile: facilitator guide

Version 0.1.0 (draft, before the pilot).

A facilitator is a person who gives the profile to others and helps them to use the result: a
coach, a trainer, a people practitioner or a manager. The facilitator is never the line manager
of a person who takes the profile, because the person who scores the answers sees them. You do not need a licence or a
certificate. You do need to read this guide and the
[technical manual](technical-manual.md), and to follow the rules on
[data protection](../data-protection.md).

## What you must say every time

1. The profile is for development. It is never used for a decision about a person.
2. It is voluntary. A person who does not take part has no disadvantage.
3. It is a draft that is not yet tested. The result is a suggestion.
4. The person decides who sees the result. The facilitator who scores the answers sees it, and
   nobody else does unless the person agrees.
5. No side is better than the other, and a preference is not a limit.

If your organisation cannot keep these five promises, do not use the profile.

## Before

- Agree the purpose. "To agree how we work together" is a purpose. "To decide who works from
  home" is a decision about people, and it is not permitted.
- Give each person the notice from the data protection page, and time to decide.
- Tell people how long it takes (about 10 minutes) and when they get the result.
- For a team, ask each person in private if their result can be part of the team report.

## The questionnaire

- Use the 60 items in the order of the item bank, with the instruction and the response
  labels from section 5 of the technical manual.
- Score the answers with the open scoring code. Do not change the scoring.

## A one-to-one conversation (45 minutes)

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 5 minutes | Repeat the five points. Ask what the person wants from the conversation |
| 10 minutes | The person reads the report. Ask: "Where do you recognise yourself? Where not?" |
| 15 minutes | Compare the preferences with the present job. "Where does your work fit what you prefer? Where does it not, and what does that cost you?" |
| 10 minutes | The "put it to use" section: what to ask for, and what to offer |
| 5 minutes | One request that the person will make, to whom, and when |

A balanced result is a real result. Do not push a balanced person to choose a side.

If the person disagrees with a result, accept it, and ask what would be more accurate.

## A team session (90 minutes)

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 10 minutes | The five points. The purpose. Agree that what people share stays in the room |
| 15 minutes | Explain the five scales with an everyday example of each side. People place themselves on each line from their own report, if they choose to |
| 15 minutes | Show the team spread. "What do we see? Does it match our experience?" |
| 30 minutes | Take the two scales with the widest spread, or the two that the team selects. Use the questions in the team report |
| 15 minutes | Write two or three agreements, each with a review date |
| 5 minutes | Close. Remind people that they own their results |

A person can take part in the session without sharing a result.

## Errors to prevent

| Error | Why it matters | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "He is an Alone" | A position becomes a label, and the balanced zone disappears | Say "he prefers more quiet time" |
| Sorting people into two groups on a scale | Most people are near the middle. Two groups invent a difference | Show the line and the bands |
| A preference used as a refusal: "I am Flexible, I do not do plans" | A preference is not a limit | Ask what would make the plan easier to keep |
| A preference used against a person: "she is Alert, do not give her the project" | It is a decision about a person, and it is not permitted | Stop it, and explain why |
| The Pressure scale read as a statement about health | It is a normal difference between people | Use the words "steady" and "alert". Do not use words from medicine. If a person raises a health matter, listen, and point them to the right support |
| A manager asks for individual results | It breaks the promise | Refuse. Offer the team report |
| Combining results into types | The profile has five independent lines | Read each line |

## Used with the OL Behaviour Profile

The behaviour profile describes how a person says that they act. This profile describes what a person likes
and needs. Where the two differ, ask about it. For example, a person can prefer to work alone and have a higher
Voice score. Ask what is behind the difference.

Some scales of the two profiles are expected to go together (see section 9 of the technical
manual). Do not present an agreement between the two profiles as proof that either is
correct.

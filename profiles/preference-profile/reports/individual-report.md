# OL Preference Profile: individual report template

Version 0.1.0 (draft). Text in `{braces}` is replaced for each person. For each scale, keep the
paragraph for the person's position and remove the other two.

Design rules for a person who builds this report:

- Show each result as a mark on a straight horizontal line with a description at each end, the
  band as a thicker part of the line, and the balanced zone shaded. Use one neutral colour for
  all five lines.
- Do not use a circle, a wheel, quadrants, a colour for each scale or side, or a letter code.
- Do not join the five results into a type or a label for the person.

## Company: {company.position}

Company is about how much you want other people around you when you work.

**Alone.** You do your best work with quiet and privacy. Company for long periods tires you,
and you recover alone. What helps: time without interruption, notice before a meeting, the
option to think before you answer. The cost to watch: others can lose sight of you and of
your work. Ask for: blocks of quiet time. Offer: a regular, planned point of contact.

**Balanced.** You like both company and quiet, and you probably need some of each in a week.
What helps: a mix that you can adjust. The cost to watch: you may not notice which of the two
you are short of until you are tired. Ask yourself at the end of a week which you needed more.

**With others.** You do your best work in a group, and you think by talking. Long periods
alone drain you. What helps: shared work, people within reach, frequent short contact. The
cost to watch: you can interrupt people who need quiet, and long periods of remote work may cost you effort.
Ask for: regular time with others. Offer: to check before you interrupt.

## Focus: {focus.position}

Focus is about what holds your attention.

**Practical.** You prefer the concrete, the present and what can be used. Long discussions of
theory lose you. What helps: examples, a clear use, a next step. The cost to watch: you can
dismiss an idea before its use is clear. Ask for: the practical point first. Offer: patience
for ten minutes of "what if".

**Balanced.** You like ideas with a use, and practice with a reason. What helps: both the
reason and the example. The cost to watch: little, apart from impatience with either extreme.
You can often translate between the two sides of a team.

**Conceptual.** You prefer ideas, reflection and the meaning behind things. Routine detail
loses you. What helps: time to think, hard problems, room to ask why. The cost to watch: you
can stay with the idea after others need a decision. Ask for: time to think before a
decision. Offer: a plain summary and a next step.

## Decisions: {decisions.position}

Decisions is about what you look at first when you decide.

**By criteria.** You decide first by rules, logic and what is fair in principle. You expect
people to look after themselves. What helps: clear criteria and facts. The cost to watch: a
correct decision can fail because of how people receive it. Ask for: the facts. Offer: one
question before you decide, "who does this affect, and how will they hear it?"

**Balanced.** You weigh the criteria and the people, and the order depends on the case. What
helps: time to hear both. The cost to watch: when the two conflict, the decision can be slow.
Decide in advance which comes first for this decision.

**By people.** You decide first by the effect on the people involved. You feel what others
feel. What helps: contact with the people affected, and time to consult. The cost to watch:
you can delay a decision that is necessary and unwelcome, and carry other people's troubles.
Ask for: time to talk to people. Offer: clear criteria that you will keep to.

## Planning: {planning.position}

Planning is about how settled you want things to be before you act.

**Flexible.** You prefer to keep options open, to decide late and to act in the moment. A
fixed plan feels like a limit. What helps: a goal with freedom in the method, and short
cycles. The cost to watch: late changes cost other people time, and some risks get no
thought. Ask for: room to adapt. Offer: an early warning when you change course.

**Balanced.** You plan what matters and leave the rest open. What helps: a plan with room in
it. The cost to watch: others may not know which parts of your plan are fixed. Say which
parts are fixed.

**Planned.** You prefer to think first, weigh the options, settle the course and keep to it.
A sudden change costs you effort. What helps: notice, clear steps and time to prepare. The
cost to watch: you can keep to a plan after the facts change, and others can find you slow
to start. Ask for: notice of changes. Offer: a point in each plan where you check if it still
fits.

## Pressure: {pressure.position}

Pressure is about how soon you notice risk and feel the weight of events.

**Steady.** You stay calm when things go wrong, and you recover fast. It takes much to worry
you. What helps: you need little here. The cost to watch: you can miss a risk that others
see, and your calm can look like a lack of care. Ask: "what are you worried about that I am
not?" Offer: your calm, when others need it.

**Balanced.** You feel pressure when the stakes are real, and you settle when they are not.
What helps: a clear view of what matters. The cost to watch: several small pressures at one
time can add up without notice.

**Alert.** You notice risk early, you think about what can go wrong, and you feel pressure
sooner than others. What helps: information, notice, a plan for the bad case, and time to
recover after a peak. The cost to watch: worry can use energy that the work needs, and
others can read your concern as a lack of confidence. Ask for: early information. Offer:
your list of risks, which a team needs.

This scale describes a normal difference between people. It says nothing about health.

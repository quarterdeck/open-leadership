# Proposed framework architecture

Status: **the Phase 1 proposal**. The decisions that followed are in the files in
[`framework/`](../framework/). Where this document differs from them, they are correct. Some
titles changed later: for example the Level 7 coaching certificate and the first grade of the
recognition scheme.
The facts behind it are in [source-notes.md](source-notes.md).

Open Leadership is an independent, unregulated framework. This proposal uses the public rules of
the Regulated Qualifications Framework (RQF) for levels and sizes, so that UK readers can judge
the level and scope of each qualification, and so that a route to regulation stays open.

## 1. Design principles

1. **One unit bank.** Every qualification is a set of units from one bank. A unit is written one
   time and used in each qualification that needs it.
2. **One credit system.** One credit is 10 hours of total qualification time (TQT).
3. **Public size rules.** Award: 120 hours or less. Certificate: 121 to 369 hours. Diploma: 370
   hours or more.
4. **Three levels first.** Levels 3, 5 and 7. They match first-line, middle and senior roles.
   Levels 2, 4 and 6 can be added later from the same bank.
5. **Assessment at work.** A learner shows each outcome with evidence from real work. All
   assessment material is public, so an assessment cannot depend on a secret question.
6. **Open foundations.** Level descriptors come from the Ofqual descriptors (Open Government
   Licence). Content comes from published research and openly licensed public standards.

## 2. Levels

OL writes its own descriptor for each level. Each descriptor has three strands. The first two
follow the RQF. The third is added by OL for management practice.

| Strand | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | What must the learner know and understand? |
| Skills | What must the learner be able to do? |
| Scope | How much responsibility, and for whom and for what? |

| Level | Typical learner | Comparable level of demand |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Team leader, supervisor, new first-line manager | A level |
| 5 | Middle manager, experienced first-line manager | Foundation degree |
| 7 | Senior manager, director | Master's degree |

## 3. Credit, size and unit rules

| Rule | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Credit | 1 credit = 10 hours TQT |
| Unit size | One standard size at each level: 5 credits at Level 3, 6 credits at Level 5, 10 credits at Level 7 |
| Guided learning | A guide share of TQT: 40% at Level 3, 35% at Level 5, 25% at Level 7 |
| Level of units | At least two thirds of the credit at the level of the qualification. The remainder can come from one level below |
| Prior learning | A centre can recognise prior learning for up to half of a qualification |
| Entry | No formal entry requirements. Guidance on the role that a learner needs for the work evidence |

A standard unit size makes the rules of combination simple: a qualification is a number of units.

### Unit bank

Units are grouped by domain. A code shows the level, the domain and a number, for example
`OL5-PEO-02`.

| Code | Domain | Examples of subjects |
|---|---|---|
| SLF | Leading yourself | Self-awareness, time and priorities, professional development, ethics |
| PEO | Leading people | Teams, performance, difficult conversations, inclusion, wellbeing |
| COA | Coaching and mentoring | Coaching skills, mentoring, coaching culture, supervision |
| OPS | Managing operations | Planning, quality, projects, problem solving, data |
| RES | Managing resources | Budgets, finance, risk, procurement |
| CHG | Change and improvement | Change, innovation, continuous improvement |
| STR | Strategy and organisation | Strategy, organisation design, stakeholders, governance |
| PPR | People practice | Resourcing, employee relations, reward, learning design, facilitation, people data |

Estimated size of the bank: 50 to 60 units across the three levels.

Each unit has: a purpose, learning outcomes with measurable verbs, assessment criteria, a short
workplace scenario, evidence requirements and guidance for the assessor.

## 4. The qualification ladder

Titles use the pattern in the specification. See section 10 for a question on the prefix.

### A. Management

| Qualification | Credits | Units |
|---|---|---|
| OL Level 3 Award in Management Practice | 10 | 2 |
| OL Level 3 Certificate in Management Practice | 25 | 5 |
| OL Level 5 Certificate in Management Practice | 18 | 3 |
| OL Level 5 Diploma in Management Practice | 42 | 7 |
| OL Level 7 Certificate in Strategic Management | 20 | 2 |
| OL Level 7 Diploma in Strategic Management | 40 | 4 |

Emphasis: operations, resources, change, with core units on people.

### B. Leadership

| Qualification | Credits | Units |
|---|---|---|
| OL Level 3 Award in Leadership Practice | 10 | 2 |
| OL Level 5 Certificate in Leadership Practice | 18 | 3 |
| OL Level 5 Diploma in Leadership Practice | 42 | 7 |
| OL Level 7 Diploma in Strategic Leadership | 40 | 4 |
| OL Level 3 Award in Workplace Coaching | 10 | 2 |
| OL Level 5 Certificate in Coaching and Mentoring | 18 | 3 |
| OL Level 7 Certificate in Executive Coaching and Mentoring | 20 | 2 |

Emphasis: leading yourself, leading people, strategy, with the coaching and mentoring pathway.

Management and leadership share the core units at each level. A learner with one diploma needs
only the other pathway units for the second.

### C. People practice

| Qualification | Credits | Units |
|---|---|---|
| OL Level 3 Certificate in People Practice | 25 | 5 |
| OL Level 5 Diploma in People Practice (pathway: people management) | 42 | 7 |
| OL Level 5 Diploma in People Practice (pathway: learning and development) | 42 | 7 |
| OL Level 7 Diploma in Strategic People Practice | 60 | 6 |

The Level 5 diploma has 3 core units, 3 pathway units and 1 optional unit. The core units come
from the shared domains, so a people professional and a line manager study the same material on
ethics, evidence and culture.

**Competence framework.** OL writes its own. It is a grid: practice areas down the side, three
depths across the top (support, practise, lead). The practice areas come from openly licensed
public sources: the Civil Service HR Career Framework and the apprenticeship standards for HR
and L&D. It does not use the Profession Map of the incumbent body.

**Recognition scheme.** Three grades, on qualification plus evidenced experience:

| Grade | Knowledge | Experience | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| OL Associate in People Practice | Level 3 certificate, or a comparable qualification | 1 year in a people practice role | Written account, corroborated by another person |
| OL Practitioner in People Practice | Level 5 diploma, or a comparable qualification | 2 years | Written account and a professional discussion |
| OL Senior Practitioner in People Practice | Level 7 diploma, or a comparable qualification | 4 years, some at strategic level | Written account and a professional discussion |

Each grade has a route for experience only, and renewal with a record of continuing
development. The scheme needs an owner that operates it (see section 10, question 1).

## 5. Assessment and grading

| Rule | Proposal |
|---|---|
| Methods | Work-based assignment, portfolio of work evidence, observation, professional discussion, reflective account, project |
| Examinations | None |
| Standard | Criterion based. The learner must meet each assessment criterion |
| Unit result | Pass or refer. A referred learner can submit again |
| Qualification result | Pass. No grades in the first version |
| Quality assurance | The centre marks. A second person checks a sample. The rules are in the centre guide |
| Authenticity | Evidence comes from the learner's own work, with a statement from a witness at work |

## 6. The two profiles

Both profiles use public-domain items from the International Personality Item Pool, a five-point
rating scale, and continuous scores. Both are for development and self-awareness. They are not
validated for recruitment or selection.

### D. OL Behaviour Profile

Purpose: to describe how a person tends to act with people and tasks at work, in words that a
team can use.

Four independent dimensions. Each is a continuous score. There are no types, no quadrants, no
circle and no letter codes.

| Working name | What it describes | Research base |
|---|---|---|
| Voice | How readily a person takes the lead and states a view | Agency: the assertiveness part of extraversion |
| Warmth | How much a person attends to others and to relationships | Communion: friendliness and the caring facets of agreeableness |
| Method | How much a person prefers plans, order and completion | Conscientiousness |
| Exploration | How much a person looks for new ideas and change | Openness |

### E. OL Preference Profile

Purpose: to describe the working conditions in which a person does their best work, for
conversations about roles, routines and team agreements.

Five two-sided scales. Each result is a position on a line, with a band that shows the
uncertainty and a "balanced" zone in the middle. There are no types, no colours and no wheel.

| Working name | The two sides |
|---|---|
| Company | Work with others, or work alone |
| Focus | Concrete and practical, or conceptual and imaginative |
| Decisions | By criteria and logic, or by the effect on people |
| Planning | Planned and settled, or flexible and open |
| Pressure | Calm and steady, or alert to risk |

### How the two differ, and what they share

- The behaviour profile describes what others see a person do. The preference profile describes
  what a person likes and needs. A person can prefer to work alone and still speak up readily.
- They share one item pool and one scoring engine (code, with tests).
- Both draw on the same five personality domains, so some scores will correlate. Each profile
  uses different facets, and no item counts towards a reported score in both. The technical
  manual reports the correlations. If the pilot shows that two scores measure the same thing,
  one is removed.
- Each reported score has 12 items or more.

### Evidence plan

- The technical manual follows the sections of the EFPA Test Review Model (openly licensed).
- Pilot: 300 respondents as a minimum, 500 or more as the target, UK working adults. At least
  100 complete the profiles a second time after 2 to 8 weeks.
- Analysis: internal consistency (alpha and omega), test-retest, factor structure, and fairness
  across groups. The analysis code is in the repository.
- Norms come from the pilot sample. No open norm set for UK working adults exists.

### Data protection by design

- Taking part is voluntary. The person sees the result first and decides who else sees it.
- No item or report text refers to health, belief or politics.
- Identified results have a short retention period, and the person can delete them. The
  research data set is anonymous.
- The terms state that a profile must never be the only basis for a decision about a person.

## 8. Badges and certificates

Quarterdeck has a badge for its own programmes: a rounded panel with a rosette, an infinity
symbol and a line of text. Kris proposes to reuse this design for OL qualifications.

Proposal:

- Keep the shape and the layout, so that the badge is Quarterdeck's own design and looks like no
  incumbent mark.
- Change the text. The present text says that a programme is accredited. An OL badge must not
  use "accredited", "approved", "certified by" or "regulated" (hard rule 3). It states the
  qualification, for example "Open Leadership" above "Level 5 Diploma in Management Practice".
- One badge design for all qualifications, with the level and title as text. One more for each
  grade of the recognition scheme.
- The badge files are added in Phase 2 with the credit and assessment rules, which say who can
  issue a badge and for what.

## 9. Versions and governance

- Each unit, qualification and profile has a version number: major, minor, patch. A major change
  alters what a learner must do. A minor change adds or clarifies. A patch corrects an error.
- Changes are proposed as GitHub issues and pull requests.
- `GOVERNANCE.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md` are written in Phase 2, after the questions below are
  answered.

## 10. Questions for Kris before Phase 2

From the specification:

1. Does Quarterdeck act as the awarding body, or does a separate legal entity own the framework?
   This decides who issues certificates and who operates the recognition scheme.
2. Is a route to Ofqual recognition wanted later? See
   [issue 2](https://github.com/quarterdeck/open-leadership/issues/2).
3. The licence is now MIT for all content. MIT permits any person to deliver and certify OL
   qualifications commercially. Is that the intention, or is a protected certification mark
   wanted for delivery that Quarterdeck approves?
4. Does Quarterdeck host the profile tools, and on which platform?
5. Who signs off the human review of the comparison and positioning page?

From the research:

6. **The name.** No registered trade mark for "Open Leadership" was found, but the official
   register could not be searched by an automated tool, and the phrase is in wide use. A person
   must search the UK IPO register for "Open Leadership" and "OL" in classes 9, 16, 35, 41 and 42
   before launch.
7. **The prefix "OL".** Seven live UK trade marks for "OL" exist, with owners in other fields,
   and "OL" also means "Ordinary Level" in some education systems. Proposal: write "Open
   Leadership" in full in each title ("Open Leadership Level 5 Diploma in Management Practice")
   and use "OL" only as a short form in running text.
8. **National Occupational Standards.** Their reuse terms are not confirmed, because the site
   refuses automated requests. A person must read the terms page. Until then, OL takes no text
   from them.
9. **Size of the Level 7 people practice diploma.** The proposal is 60 credits. The incumbent
   qualification is 120 credits. Which scope is wanted?
10. **Grades.** The proposal is pass or refer only. Are merit and distinction wanted at Level 7?
11. **The badge.** Is the present badge Quarterdeck's own design, with no licence from a CPD
    body? Does the OL badge keep the Quarterdeck "Q" rosette, or does it get an OL symbol? This
    depends on question 1.
12. **A strengths profile.** See [issue 1](https://github.com/quarterdeck/open-leadership/issues/1).

# Name-clash report: "Open Leadership" and "OL"

Date of research: 18 November 2026.

**This is a factual search, not legal advice.** It does not replace a clearance search by a trade mark attorney. The official UK IPO register could not be searched directly (see section 3). Trade mark results come from a third-party mirror and from web search.

Key to source labels:

- **[opened]** means the page was opened and read.
- **[search result]** means the item was seen only in a search result list or snippet.
- **[lookup]** means a direct WHOIS, DNS or HTTP lookup from the command line.

---

## 1. Conclusion

### "Open Leadership": risk MEDIUM

Reasons:

- No registered trade mark for the exact words "OPEN LEADERSHIP" was found for the UK, the EU or the US in the mirror that was used. That part of the risk looks low, but it is not confirmed on the official register.
- The phrase is in wide, established use in the same field (leadership development). Examples are Charlene Li's 2010 book, Mozilla's Open Leadership Framework, the Red Hat / Open Organization "Open Leadership Definition", The Open Leadership Network (which sells training and certifications), and a Cegos course.
- Two of those are openly licensed leadership frameworks. An "open, MIT-licensed leadership framework" called "Open Leadership" is very close to them in idea. Confusion with Mozilla's "Open Leadership Framework" is the most probable problem. It is a reputation and search-visibility problem more than a rights problem.
- The phrase is descriptive. In UK executive education, "open programmes" means open-enrolment courses. The name will be hard to protect and hard to find in search results.
- One active UK company has the name: OPEN LEADERSHIP CONSULTING LTD (dormant).
- openleadership.com, .org and .co.uk are all taken. Two are for sale by resellers.

### "OL": risk MEDIUM

Reasons:

- The mirror shows seven live UK registrations for the mark "OL", with different owners. The classes of most could not be confirmed. One owner (Upland Software, from the Objectif Lune software business) holds a US "OL" mark in classes 9, 41 and 42. Its UK mark (UK00913134747) probably has similar classes. This must be checked on the IPO register.
- Olympique Lyonnais holds two UK "OL" marks. The classes were not confirmed.
- No Ofqual awarding organisation or regulated qualification uses the prefix "OL". That is good.
- "OL" has other meanings in education. It means "Ordinary Level" in the Irish Leaving Certificate and in Sri Lanka (O/L). It recalls the old UK "O Level". In HR and L&D it is a common short form of "organisational learning". A title such as "OL Level 5 Diploma" can be read as "Ordinary Level Level 5".
- A two-letter prefix is weak as a brand and easy to confuse.

### Short recommendation (not legal advice)

"Open Leadership" can probably be used, but it is crowded and weak. Before launch, get a search of the official IPO register for both names in classes 9, 16, 35, 41 and 42. Think about a more distinctive framework name or a longer prefix (see section 7).

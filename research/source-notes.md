# Source notes

Date of research: 18 November 2026. The list of sources, with links and licences, is in
[SOURCES.md](../SOURCES.md).

These notes record public facts only: levels, sizes, hours, credits, titles, theme areas and
assessment methods. They contain no unit specifications, learning outcomes, assessment criteria,
questionnaire items or report text from any proprietary source. Theme areas are in our own words.

Status words:

- **Confirmed**: the source page was opened and the fact was read there.
- **Search summary**: the fact came from a search result. The page was not opened.
- **Not confirmed**: no opened source gave the fact.

Facts that came only from memory are left out.

## 1. The Regulated Qualifications Framework (RQF)

Source: Ofqual Handbook, section E (gov.uk, Open Government Licence v3.0). All confirmed.

- Levels: Entry 1 to 3, then Levels 1 to 8. Levels 4 to 8 align with higher education levels.
  Level 3 compares with A level, Level 5 with a foundation degree, Level 7 with a master's degree.
- Each level has two descriptors: knowledge and understanding, and skills.
- The awarding organisation chooses the level that fits best. A perfect fit is not necessary.
- Total Qualification Time (TQT) is the estimated time a typical learner needs. It includes
  Guided Learning Hours (GLH) and all other study and assessment time.
- GLH is the time that a teacher or supervisor delivers or supervises in real time.
- Credit is optional. One credit is 10 hours of TQT.

| Size term | TQT |
|---|---|
| Award | 120 hours or less |
| Certificate | 121 to 369 hours |
| Diploma | 370 hours or more |

"Extended Diploma" has no threshold in the Ofqual rules.

What Levels 2 to 7 expect (our summary of the OGL descriptors):

| Level | Knowledge | Skills |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Facts and procedures in one area of work | Does clear tasks, solves simple problems, checks if the action worked |
| 3 | Facts, procedures and some theory | Deals with tasks that are defined but not routine, and judges the methods and results |
| 4 | Practical, technical or theoretical knowledge with some breadth | Deals with non-routine problems, analyses information, reviews methods and results |
| 5 | Broad knowledge, with awareness of different views | Finds a way forward where the problem is only broadly defined, uses research, evaluates results |
| 6 | Advanced knowledge, used critically | Makes solutions where many factors interact, analyses complex information |
| 7 | Very advanced knowledge that the holder can reshape | Deals with many interacting factors, evaluates theory, designs research or strategy |

## 2. National Occupational Standards (NOS)

- NOS state the performance, knowledge and understanding necessary for competence in a work
  function. They are published on ukstandards.org.uk and approved on a four-nation basis.
  Confirmed (Department for the Economy, Northern Ireland).
- Skills Development Scotland manages the programme for the governments of Scotland, Wales and
  Northern Ireland. England uses apprenticeship standards instead. Search summary.
- Management and leadership: a new pan-sector suite is reported as live from August 2026. Its
  functional areas and number of standards are not confirmed.
- Human resources: a suite reviewed from 2024 and approved in 2025 is reported. Search summary.
- Learning and development: a suite approved in June 2021 is reported, with a review due in 2026.
  Search summary.
- **Licence and reuse terms: not confirmed.** The NOS site refused automated requests. A person
  must read its terms page in a browser. Until then, OL treats NOS as "facts only" and takes no
  text from them.

## 3. Management and leadership qualifications in the UK market

Two awarding bodies set the pattern that UK employers know: the Chartered Management Institute
(CMI) and the Institute of Leadership & Management (ILM, a City & Guilds brand). Their material
is copyright. These are facts about structure, from their public pages.

### Levels and target roles (confirmed)

| Level | Typical learner |
|---|---|
| 2 and 3 | Team leaders, supervisors, first-line managers |
| 4 and 5 | Middle managers |
| 6 and 7 | Senior managers and senior leaders |
| 8 | Most senior executives (CMI only) |

### Sizes (confirmed)

Both bodies offer Award, Certificate and Diploma at most levels, on the Ofqual thresholds. Some
levels have an Extended Diploma.

Minimum figures for CMI's main management and leadership qualification (GLH / TQT / credits):

| Level | Award | Certificate | Diploma | Extended Diploma |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 24 / 60 / 6 | 52 / 130 / 13 | 148 / 370 / 37 | none |
| 3 | 22 / 50 / 5 | 58 / 130 / 13 | 168 / 370 / 37 | none |
| 4 | 20 / 60 / 6 | 45 / 130 / 13 | 145 / 390 / 39 | none |
| 5 | 14 / 40 / 4 | 44 / 130 / 13 | 162 / 370 / 37 | 216 / 540 / 54 |
| 6 | 15 / 50 / 5 | 34 / 130 / 13 | 118 / 370 / 37 | none |
| 7 | 18 / 70 / 7 | 36 / 140 / 14 | 106 / 370 / 37 | 196 / 640 / 64 |

ILM figures:

- Older suite credit bands: Award up to 12 credits, Certificate 13 to 36, Diploma 37 or more.
- Level 7 (older suite): Award 7 credits, Certificate 15, Diploma 40, Extended Diploma 60.
- New 2026 suite at Levels 3 and 5 has two strands. A knowledge strand, assessed by assignment
  (Award 46 hours TQT, Certificate 121). A skills strand, assessed by a portfolio of work
  evidence (Award to Extended Diploma, 80 to more than 1,000 hours TQT).

### Structure (confirmed)

- Unit based. The learner combines units until the minimum for the size is reached.
- Most units are optional. Rules of combination set a minimum of credit at the level of the
  qualification and permit a capped amount from the level below or above.
- Unit sizes: about 3 to 8 credits at Level 3 and 6 to 20 credits at Level 7.

### Share of guided learning (calculated from the confirmed figures)

No public rule sets a ratio of GLH to TQT. The share decreases as the level increases: about
40% to 45% at Level 3, 35% to 45% at Level 5 and 15% to 30% at Level 7. Short taught awards have
a much higher share.

### Theme areas (our words)

- Level 3: the organisation, leading people and teams, communication, operations, change,
  projects, problem solving, budgets, conflict, wellbeing, personal development.
- Level 5: principles of management and leadership, people and performance, stakeholders,
  projects, change, operational planning, finance, culture, risk, data, innovation.
- Level 7: strategic leadership, strategy, organisation design, finance, risk, partnerships,
  change and innovation, culture, data, applied research, personal development.
- Both bodies also have a coaching and mentoring family at Levels 3, 5 and 7.

### Assessment (confirmed)

- Approved centres deliver and assess. The awarding body gives assignment briefs and marking
  sheets, and checks the centre's marking.
- Work-based assignments are the main method. Other permitted methods: case study, observation,
  presentation, professional discussion, project, reflective log, portfolio of evidence.
- Criterion based. The learner must meet every criterion. The result is pass or refer.
- No examinations.

### Professional recognition (confirmed)

CMI gives membership grades on qualification plus experience. Chartered Manager has three routes:
a degree plus 3 years in a management role (or 5 years of experience) with a written submission
and a professional discussion; a recent Level 5 to 8 qualification plus 3 years in a role, with
a shorter assessment; or an apprenticeship end-point assessment.

## 4. People practice qualifications in the UK market

The Chartered Institute of Personnel and Development (CIPD) sets the pattern. Its material is
copyright. Sizes are confirmed from the Ofqual register (OGL).

| Level | Qualification type | TQT | GLH | Credits | Units |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Foundation certificate | 240 | 120 | 24 | 4, all mandatory |
| 5 | Associate diploma (two: people management; organisational L&D) | 420 | 175 | 42 | 7: 3 core, 3 pathway, 1 optional |
| 7 | Advanced diploma (two: strategic people management; strategic L&D) | 1,200 | 240 | 120 | 8: 4 core, 3 pathway, 1 optional |

- The core units are the same in the two diplomas at each level. The pathway units differ.
- Entry: no formal requirements. Minimum age 16, 18 and 21.
- Assessment: scenario-based assignments. No examination. The centre marks, and CIPD checks.
  Units are pass or fail. Level 7 assessments have pass, merit and distinction.
- Recognition of prior learning is limited to 50% of the qualification.
- Theme areas (our words). Level 3: the organisation, use of data, professional conduct, the
  employee life cycle. Level 5 core: culture and performance, decisions from evidence, ethics.
  Level 5 pathways: employee relations, resourcing and talent, reward; or informal learning,
  programme design, facilitation. Level 7 core: the business environment, people strategy,
  personal effectiveness, a research project.
- Membership: lower grades come directly from a qualification. The chartered grades need a
  separate assessment of experience: a written account with a person who corroborates it, or a
  professional discussion of about 60 minutes with an independent assessor. Confirmed from the
  public terms of the assessment. The detail of each grade is search summary only.
- The CIPD Profession Map exists and is copyright with no open licence. OL does not use it.

### Recognition schemes in other professions (confirmed)

| Scheme | Pattern |
|---|---|
| Science Council registers | Three tiers that match Levels 3, 5 and 7, with 1, 2 and 4 to 5 years of experience. Experience can replace the qualification |
| Engineering Council (UK-SPEC) | The same five competence areas at each title, with more depth at each step. Yearly renewal |
| CMI Chartered Manager | Qualification plus years in a role, then a written account and a professional discussion |

The common pattern: three tiers at about Levels 3, 5 and 7; one set of competence areas used at
each tier with more depth; knowledge shown by a qualification or an equivalent; practice shown
by a written account that another person corroborates, and a professional discussion with an
independent assessor; a minimum time in a role; a route for experience only; renewal with
continuing development.
